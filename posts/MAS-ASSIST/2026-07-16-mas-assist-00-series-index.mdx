# Maximo Assistant on MAS 9: A Practical Eight-Part Guide to the watsonx-Powered Assistant

> **Who this is for:** Maximo administrators, maintenance leaders, and technical teams deciding whether — and how — to turn on the generative AI in MAS 9. If you want to know what the Maximo Assistant *actually does*, what it *does not do*, and what it costs to run, this series is for you. If you are building the full asset-intelligence pipeline, the other MAS add-on series (Health, Predict, Monitor, Visual Inspection) cover their own ground; this series is the deep treatment of the AI layer that sits across all of them.

**Read time:** ~16 minutes for this index | Full series: ~2.5 hours | **Updated:** 20 October 2026 — expanded to eight parts for MAS 9.2

---

## Why This Series Exists

Generative AI is the single most over-promised and under-explained capability in MAS 9. Every vendor deck has a chatbot on it. Every upgrade conversation eventually reaches the question: *"So does Maximo do AI now?"*

The honest answer is: **yes, and it is more specific — and more bounded — than the marketing suggests.** The Maximo Assistant is real. It is powered by IBM watsonx.ai foundation models. It can draft a work order from a plain-English description, translate a natural-language question into a Maximo query, recommend a failure code with a confidence score, and surface similar historical records so a new technician benefits from a veteran's past fix. That is genuinely useful, and it represents the biggest change in how people interact with Maximo since the web UI replaced the fat client.

But it is an **assistant**, not an oracle. (In MAS 9.2 it became *agentic* — it can act as a single agent across a defined set of AI tools — but the tools are ones you expose and a human still confirms every write. More on that below.) It drafts; you approve. It recommends; you accept, modify, or reject. It learns from *your* data, which means it is only as good as the two-plus years of work-order history you feed it. And it does not come for free — it needs watsonx.ai, the Maximo AI Service on OpenShift, trained models, a feedback loop, and AppPoints to license it.

Most teams meet this capability through a demo and a licensing line item, then discover the gap between "the demo drafted a work order" and "our technicians trust it in production." This series closes that gap. Eight parts, each grounded in documented MAS 9.0, 9.1, and 9.2 behavior, each concrete about what watsonx does and does not do — and, from Part 7 on, explicit about where IBM-documented capability ends and what you would have to build yourself begins.

## What the Assistant Actually Does — At a Glance

Before you pick a reading path, it helps to see the whole capability surface on one page. Everything in the eight parts is an expansion of one of these rows. The **Evidence** column says how firmly each row is grounded: *IBM-documented* means IBM describes it for the Assistant/AI Service; *IBM, other product* means IBM documents it, but under a different product; *Build (9.2 MCP)* means IBM gives you the toolkit but not the feature — you build it.

| Capability | What it does concretely | Evidence | Where it lives in the series |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conversational assistant** | Ask questions in plain language in Manage, Health, and dashboards; agentic across a defined tool set in 9.2 | IBM-documented (9.1; agentic 9.2) | Parts 1, 8 |
| **Natural-language work order creation** | Draft a work order from a plain-English problem description | IBM-documented (9.2 MCP write examples) | Part 2 |
| **AI-powered search** | Replace filter queries with plain-English searches — assets, SRs, WOs, meters, PMs by default; any enabled Manage object since April 2026 | IBM-documented | Part 2 |
| **Field value recommendations** | Suggest priority, work type, failure codes, craft, and more — each with a confidence score | IBM-documented (9.1) | Part 2 |
| **Similar-record detection** | Surface similar work orders and records (9.1) and similar tickets (9.2 Tickets app) | IBM-documented | Part 3 |
| **Knowledge base search** | Pull answers from manuals, SOPs, and repair narratives | IBM-documented (docsearch config) | Part 3 |
| **Remote-expert collaboration** | Connect a field tech to an SME | IBM, other product — Maximo Collaborate (ex-Assist) | Part 3 |
| **Guided troubleshooting** | Walk a tech from symptom to check to likely cause | IBM-documented ("How do I…" prompts) | Part 4 |
| **Failure-code identification** | Recommend failure class, problem, cause, and remedy codes | IBM-documented (9.0+) | Part 4 |
| **PM optimization** | Recommend which PMs to cut, tighten, or extend | TMG assessment — confirm in your build | Part 4 |
| **Query supply-chain objects** | Ask about items, inventory balances, and PRs in plain language | IBM-documented once an admin enables those objects (April 2026 FC "supports all objects") | Parts 2, 5, 7 |
| **Create an item from a description** | Draft a new item master record for review | Build (9.2 MCP) — not native | Part 7 |
| **Draft a purchase requisition** | Assemble PR lines from a request and leave it in WAPPR | Build (9.2 MCP) — not native | Part 7 |
| **Duplicate-item similarity** | Flag "this item probably already exists" before creation | Build (9.2 MCP + your own similarity) — not native | Part 7 |

Read that list twice, because it is also a list of things the Assistant *does not* do. It does not run your integrations, restructure your data model, forecast failures (that is Maximo Predict, a different application), or take any action without a human confirming it. In MAS 9.2 the Assistant can *act* — IBM's documented write examples are changing a work order status, creating a follow-up work order, and creating a service request through the Maximo MCP server — but only through tools you expose, and every write still ends with a person confirming. That single fact — a human decides — is the spine of the whole series.

## What You Need to Run It

The second thing worth knowing before you start is that none of these capabilities is a switch you flip in Manage. They are served by the **Maximo AI Service**, a workload you deploy on OpenShift and connect to **watsonx.ai**, running models trained on **your** data. Part 5 walks the full deployment; here is the prerequisite checklist so the scope is honest from page one:

| Prerequisite | Why it is required | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| **A watsonx.ai instance** (SaaS or on-premises) | Supplies the foundation models behind natural language and generation | Parts 1, 5, 6 |
| **The Maximo AI Service on OpenShift** | The bridge that connects watsonx.ai to Manage, Mobile, and the rest — nothing works without it | Parts 1, 5 |
| **2+ years of failure-coded work order history** | The recommendation models learn from *your* data, not IBM's — thin history means weak recommendations | Parts 2, 5 |
| **A trained set of recommendation models** | Field, failure-code, and similar-record recommendations are trained per environment | Parts 2, 4, 5 |
| **AppPoints entitlement** | The shared MAS licensing currency that gates who gets AI access | Part 6 |
| **A governed feedback loop** | Turns everyday accept/reject decisions into a model that improves over time | Parts 5, 6 |

<aside>

💡 **Key insight:** The most common way teams get this wrong is scoping it as a software install. The operator install is a day or two. The prerequisites that actually determine value — clean historical data, trained models, a governed feedback loop, the right people with access — are weeks of work spanning platform, data, and maintenance disciplines. If you internalize only one thing from this index, make it that.

</aside>

## Key Themes Across the Series

**It is agentic within a defined tool set — and a human still decides.** Through MAS 9.1 the Assistant was a pure assistant. In MAS 9.2 IBM describes it as able to "act as a single agent" that analyzes a request and works across a defined set of AI tools, and the Maximo MCP server lets it perform writes — but only through the tools you expose, and with a human confirming. The Assistant drafts a work order and asks *"would you like me to submit it?"* It recommends a failure code with a confidence score; you accept, modify, or reject. Treat it as a very fast, very well-read junior colleague — not as an autopilot.

**It learns from your data.** watsonx.ai supplies the language understanding, but the recommendations are trained on *your* history. Two-plus years of work orders with real failure codes is the difference between recommendations people trust and recommendations people mute. Garbage in, muted out.

**Deployment is a project.** watsonx.ai, the AI Service operator, model training, and a feedback loop add up to a 2–3 week pilot for a focused team — not a feature flag. Budget for it honestly.

**Governance comes first.** Where your data goes (SaaS vs on-premises watsonx.ai), who can use the AI (AppPoints), and how you keep humans in the loop (confidence + accept/reject) are decisions to make *before* go-live, not after.

**The feedback loop is the engine.** Accept/modify/reject is not just a UX nicety — it is the training signal. A governed feedback loop is what turns a mediocre day-one model into a genuinely helpful one by month six.

**Version matters — confirm what you are on.** MAS 9.0 brought watsonx problem-code suggestion (Work Order Intelligence). **MAS 9.1** brought the AI assistant in Manage, Health, and dashboards, similar records, and AI-recommended field values, all served by the **Maximo AI Service** (which replaced the earlier AI broker on 1 August 2025). **MAS 9.2** (GA 25 June 2026) made the Assistant agentic, added the **Maximo MCP server** for custom tools built from automation scripts, object structures, and workflows, put the assistant in Maximo Mobile (online only), and redesigned the AI configuration app. The **April 2026 Feature Channel** let the assistant find data in any Manage object, including custom objects, once enabled. Training and output data are currently English. Part 8 is the dated capability map; confirm exactly which version and Feature Channel level your environment runs before planning.

<aside>

💡 **Key insight:** These themes are not separate lessons — they are one lesson viewed from several angles. The Assistant is a *bounded, trained, governed recommender*. Every part of this series is really an argument that the boundaries are the feature, not a limitation. A maintenance system of record earns trust precisely because it does not let an AI act unchecked — and that is why this one is safe to put in front of the people who keep your plant running.

</aside>
