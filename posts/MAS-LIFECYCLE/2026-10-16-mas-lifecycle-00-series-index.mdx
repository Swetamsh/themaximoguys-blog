# The Maximo Support Cliff: Every Date That Matters, 2026–2031

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** Executives, asset-management leaders, procurement, and Maximo owners who need one reliable answer to "what is the support position of our Maximo, and what is the next date we can't miss?" — without reading ten IBM pages and four partner blogs to assemble it.

## 🔥 The Quarter Everyone Is Behind On

On 30 September 2026, the last optional year of IBM Extended Support for Maximo 7.6.1.x closed. Sixteen days ago, as you read this. If you are on 7.6.1, the calendar did not give you a warning shot. It gave you a cliff edge, and you are standing on the version that already fell off the main support path.

Here is the part that makes it a *cliff* rather than a *slope*: the 7.6.1 date is not alone. In the next 257 days, two more IBM dates land that decide whether your estate is supported, compliant, or neither:

- **30 April 2027** — dual support under the MAS license for EAM 7.6.1.x ends. For some customers that is a support deadline. For others, per IBM's own words, it is a point after which "EAM usage rights expire and continued use would be out of compliance."
- **30 June 2027** — MAS 9.0.x transitions to Extended Support.

And behind you, already in the rear-view mirror, is **30 April 2026**, when MAS 8.7, 8.8 and 8.9 went out of support and 8.10 and 8.11 dropped to Extended Support.

> 💡 **Key insight:** This is not one deadline. It is five clocks running at once, each with a different owner, a different contract consequence and a different cost of missing it. The mistake we see most often is treating them as a single "upgrade by next year" item and then discovering that the *licensing* clock expires before the *technical* clock.

This series exists to put all five clocks on one page, show you which ones we confirmed on IBM's own pages and which still rest on partner sources, and give you the plan for the next 90 days. Part 0 is the map. Parts 1–4 are the territory.

## 🔬 How We Verified Every Date

The brief for this series was blunt: *do not state a date as fact until you have seen it on IBM's own page.* Some dates we could confirm that way. Some we could not, and we say so. Here is the labeling used throughout:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **IBM-confirmed** | We read the date on an IBM support or lifecycle page in October 2026 |
| **IBM-derived** | IBM states the term (for example, "one year of Extended Support"); the calendar date is arithmetic from an IBM date |
| **Partner-sourced** | Only a partner, vendor or analyst write-up states it; IBM's own page was not readable or did not carry it |
| **Open** | IBM has not published the date; we refuse to guess it |

A word on why this matters. Two of the most-quoted dates in the Maximo market — the 7.6.1 Extended Support end and the dual-support end — circulate mostly through partner blogs, each repeating the other. We went looking for IBM's own pages for both. The dual-support date turned out to be on an IBM support page updated as recently as 8 July 2026. The 7.6.1 dates are spread across three or four IBM pages that each carry a *piece* of the picture. Part 1 reconciles them. For this index, the table below is what survived verification.

## 🧭 Clock 1: Maximo 7.6.1 — Past the Main Road

Maximo Asset Management 7.6.1.x is the version most long-running estates still call "Maximo." IBM's end-of-support announcement (first made in April 2022 under announcement letter 922-024) draws the lines like this:

- **30 September 2025:** regular base support is no longer provided.
- **Extended Support:** one year, available for purchase.
- **Sustained Support:** up to five years, available for purchase.

The eligibility rules IBM lists for the extended options are specific: be current on IBM Subscription and Support, be on **7.6.1.3 or later**, be unable to upgrade before 30 September 2025, and show that you are preparing an upgrade roadmap. If you are on 7.6.1.0, .1 or .2, the first conversation with IBM is not "how do I buy Sustained Support?" — it is "what do I need to apply to get to .3 so I qualify?"

What Sustained Support gives you is a narrower product than most executives assume. Partner write-ups describe it as usage and how-to help plus fixes that already existed before the end-of-support date, with no new security patches. IBM's own text on the page we read does not itemise inclusions beyond the terms — so for planning, assume the partner description is right and confirm in writing. Part 1 treats this in depth, including the security, audit and insurance angles that don't appear on any lifecycle page.

> ⚠️ **If you also run IBM Control Desk 7.6.1.x:** its dates are *earlier* than EAM's. IBM's Control Desk guidance (updated 6 May 2026) says optional Extended Support ran until 30 September 2026, Sustained Support runs until 31 December 2026, and after that date "all IBM support for Control Desk wraps up." Control Desk is easy to forget because it often sits next to Maximo in the same estate and the same contract. It has the shortest runway of anything in this post.

## 🧭 Clock 2: The 30 April 2027 Dual-Support Deadline

Dual support is the mechanism that let customers hold both a MAS entitlement *and* keep running legacy Maximo Asset Management while they migrated. It is the thing that made "buy MAS now, migrate over two years" a legal position instead of a hopeful one.

IBM's support page ("IBM Maximo Application Suite with IBM Maximo Asset Management Dual Support ending 2027", updated 8 July 2026) states the end date plainly: **30 April 2027**. What it means depends on how you bought MAS:

| Your licensing path | What IBM says happens after 30 Apr 2027 | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Perpetual license trade-up to MAS** | You must finish your migration by the end date. "After this date, EAM usage rights expire and continued use would be out of compliance." | Running legacy EAM becomes a **compliance** problem, not just a support problem. Audit and legal exposure begin. |
| **Subscription (upgrade) path** | You stay compliant with your subscription terms, but EAM 7.6.1.x "will no longer be supported." | A **support** problem. You may still legally run it; you have nobody to call. |

IBM's earlier version of the same notice (modified February 2024) already carried the 30 April 2027 date and added that once you have moved to MAS Manage you must stop using EAM. So this is not a new surprise — it is a long-announced date that is now only 196 days out.

The reason this is a *Part 2* topic and not a footnote is that the table above hides an uncomfortable fact: most organisations cannot say, from memory, which row they are in. The answer lives in an entitlement record from the original trade-up, and in a handful of cases it is mixed across business units. Part 2 gives you the contract checklist and the exact questions to put to your IBM representative.

## 🧭 Clock 3: MAS 8.x — Already Over for Some, Extended for Others

If you migrated to MAS early, you are not automatically safe. Version 8 support split into two outcomes on 30 April 2026:

| MAS version | GA date | Status on IBM's supported-versions page | Completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8.7-CD** | 22 Feb 2022 | No longer supported | 30 Apr 2026 |
| **8.8-CD** | 26 Jul 2022 | No longer supported | 30 Apr 2026 |
| **8.9-CD** | 22 Nov 2022 | No longer supported | 30 Apr 2026 |
| **8.10-LTS** | 28 Mar 2023 | Fix availability complete; Extended Support available | Extended to **30 Apr 2030** |
| **8.11-LTS** | 26 Sep 2023 | Fix availability complete; Extended Support available | Extended to **30 Apr 2030** |

Read the 8.10/8.11 rows carefully. "Extended Support available" is not the same sentence as "supported." Fix availability has completed; Extended Support is a separate commercial arrangement with its own terms, and the 2030 date describes how long IBM will offer it, not how long the version is safe to leave alone. The security reality is visible in the current patch train: the October 2026 Core interim releases fixed an unauthenticated path-traversal issue in the login service (CVE-2026-105052) that affected 8.10 and 8.11 too, and for those two versions the remediation was a component-level fix, not a normal release. That is what Extended Support looks like in practice: you get what IBM chooses to ship, not what you ask for.

For 8.7–8.9, there is no extension on offer. If any production environment is still on one of these, the question is no longer "when do we plan the upgrade?" but "what is our exposure while we do it?" Part 3 builds the upgrade clock for this group, including the 9.1-versus-9.2 target decision.

## 🧭 Clock 4: MAS 9.0 — The Date That Was Announced This Month

MAS 9 uses a new policy, which IBM calls **Support Cycle-3** and summarises as **3+1+3**: three years of support, one year of critical-fix extension, and three years of usage and existing fixes. For 9.0.x, IBM's lifecycle page (last updated 9 October 2026) gives:

| Milestone | Date | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| General availability | 25 Jun 2024 | AD24-0483 |
| Transition to Extended Support | **30 Jun 2027** | AD26-0622 |
| Completion of Extended Support | **30 Jun 2031** | — |

The announcement letter for the 2027 transition is dated 2026 — it landed only weeks ago. That pattern matters for planning. IBM issues each transition date in a *later* announcement, so the 9.1 and 9.2 cells in the table above are blank *because IBM hasn't filled them in*, not because we couldn't find them. Resist the temptation to project a date by adding three years to GA. If 9.1 and 9.2 follow 9.0's pattern, their transition dates will be announced roughly one year before they take effect, but that is inference, not information.

Current patch levels, as of IBM's release page on 7 October 2026, show how much is moving on the 9.x line: Core 9.0.33, 9.1.27 and 9.2.8 (all interim releases), and Manage 9.0.32, 9.1.25 and 9.2.4. A 9.0 shop is not on a dead branch — it is on a branch with a 257-day countdown to a lower tier of support. Our existing [MAS 9.2 series](/blog/mas-92-series-index) covers what the move to 9.2 involves, and its [upgrade watch-list](/blog/mas-92-upgrade-watchlist) covers what breaks.

## ⚖️ Five Misreadings That Cost Real Money

After walking through this material, the same five errors keep showing up. If a colleague says one of these in your steering meeting, push back.

1. **"We bought Sustained Support, so we're covered until 2030."** Sustained Support is existence, not support in the sense you had before. It covers existing fixes and usage help. The 2030 date is how long IBM sells it, not a promise about security.
2. **"Dual support is a support issue."** For perpetual trade-up customers, IBM's wording makes it a *usage-rights* issue. The distinction decides who needs to be in the room: procurement and legal, not just IT.
3. **"We're on MAS, so we're fine."** Only if you're on the right MAS. 8.7–8.9 are out of support, 8.10/8.11 are Extended, and 9.0 will be Extended in 257 days.
4. **"Extended Support means supported."** It means a separately negotiated service on a version where regular fixes have completed. The word "available" appears on IBM's page for a reason.
5. **"9.1 and 9.2 will be supported until 2029 and 2030."** That is exactly the arithmetic IBM has not endorsed. Their transition dates are open.

## 🛠️ What To Do This Quarter

You have until the end of December to make four decisions and one calendar entry. The plan is deliberately boring, because boring is what keeps you out of the cliff.

### Week 1–2: Establish the facts

- **Inventory every environment** by product, version and fix pack. Include test, training and the "temporary" instance a vendor stood up three years ago. A single query is a reasonable starting point on any Maximo 7.6.1 estate:

```sql
-- Maximo 7.6.1: current build and installed fix pack
SELECT propvalue
FROM   maxpropvalue
WHERE  propname IN ('mxe.version', 'mxe.build');   -- confirm exact property names in your build

-- Which environments exist and which hold production data is NOT in the database;
-- it lives in your CMDB and your deployment runbooks. Reconcile the two.
```

  For MAS, the equivalent is the Core and application versions shown in the Suite Administration UI. Record the catalog version too.
- **Pull the entitlement record** for the original MAS purchase from Passport Advantage: was it a perpetual trade-up or a subscription? Which business units? This single document decides which row of the dual-support table you are in.
- **Check IBM Control Desk**, if present, against the 31 December 2026 date.

### Week 3–6: Get answers in writing

- Ask your IBM representative to **confirm in writing**: your dual-support end date, your licensing path (perpetual trade-up or subscription), the exact Sustained Support PID dates that apply to you, and whether 7.6.1.3 is required for the offering you hold.
- Ask whether the **9.1 and 9.2 transition dates** have been announced for your agreement. They are open on the public lifecycle page.
- Ask your **security and risk** teams what an unpatched platform means for your cyber-insurance questionnaire and for any regulated-asset audit. This is the angle that moves executives faster than any lifecycle table.

### Week 7–10: Decide

Put three options on the table with costs and dates, for each row you identified:

| Option | When it fits | What it buys you | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Migrate before the date** | You have a funded program and time before 30 Apr 2027 | Compliance and full support on a current MAS version | Program cost and risk compression |
| **Buy time (Sustained / Extended)** | You are eligible and the migration cannot finish | Usage help and existing fixes for a bounded period | Recurring fee, no new fixes, and no help with the *licensing* deadline |
| **Re-platform or exit** | Maximo is no longer the strategic EAM | A clean break | Highest program cost, longest duration |

Notice what the middle option does *not* buy: it does not move the 30 April 2027 licensing date. Buying Sustained Support is a support decision. It leaves the dual-support deadline exactly where it was.

### Week 11–13: Commit

Set a **go/no-go date before 31 December 2026**. Put the 30 April 2027 and 30 June 2027 dates on the executive risk register with named owners. Part 4 gives you the business-case structure to carry that decision through finance.

**Is Maximo 7.6.1 still supported after 30 September 2026?**
Only in a narrow form. Base support ended on 30 September 2025, and the one-year Extended Support window has now closed. Sustained Support, which IBM's lifecycle pages show running to 30 September 2030, gives usage help and fixes that already existed, not new fixes or security patches. You qualify only if you are current on Subscription and Support, on 7.6.1.3 or later, and preparing a migration roadmap.

**What exactly happens on 30 April 2027?**
Dual support under the MAS license for EAM 7.6.1.x ends. Per IBM, perpetual trade-up customers must finish migrating by that date because their EAM usage rights then expire; subscription customers stay compliant but lose support. The date is a legal and audit deadline for one group and a support deadline for the other. Part 2 shows how to work out which group you are in.

**I run MAS 8.9 or earlier. Am I out of support?**
Yes. IBM lists 8.7, 8.8 and 8.9 as no longer supported, with completion on 30 April 2026 and no extension. MAS 8.10 and 8.11 moved to Extended Support on the same day, with completion on 30 April 2030. Extended Support is a separate service, not the full support you had before.

**When does MAS 9.0 stop being fully supported?**
IBM's 9.0.x lifecycle page shows transition to Extended Support on 30 June 2027 (AD26-0622) and completion on 30 June 2031, under the Support Cycle-3 policy IBM describes as 3+1+3. IBM has published GA dates for 9.1 and 9.2 but not their transition dates, so do not project them from GA.

**Which of these dates should I not rely on yet?**
Two items need care. The 30 September 2030 Sustained Support date was confirmed directly on IBM's page for the sibling Spatial Asset Management 7.6.1 product and in IBM-sourced summaries for EAM itself, so confirm your exact PID. And most detail on what Sustained Support excludes (notably "no new security patches") comes from partner write-ups rather than IBM's own text. We label each one in the tables above.

## 🔟 The Ten Rules of the Support Cliff

1. **Find your row before you find your budget.** The table decides the plan.
2. **Treat licensing and support as separate clocks.** The earlier of the two sets your deadline.
3. **Get the entitlement record, not an opinion.** The trade-up paperwork settles perpetual versus subscription.
4. **Never confuse "available" with "supported."** Read IBM's wording on Extended Support literally.
5. **Do not project IBM dates.** Open cells stay open until IBM fills them.
6. **Buying time does not move the dual-support date.** Sustained Support is a support purchase.
7. **Fix pack 7.6.1.3 is a gate.** Check it before you negotiate.
8. **Inventory the forgotten.** Control Desk, training instances and vendor-hosted copies all count.
9. **Put 30 April 2027 on the risk register today** with an owner and a go/no-go date before year end.
10. **Ask IBM in writing.** A call is a conversation; an email is a position.
