# On MAS 8.x or 9.0? Your 2026–2027 Upgrade Clock

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** Maximo owners, platform leads and the executives who fund them, on any MAS 8.x release or on 9.0. You already migrated off 7.6. You are not looking at a cliff, you are looking at a treadmill, and you want to know how fast it is moving under your feet.

*Part 3 of 4 in the MAS Lifecycle series. [Start with the series index](/blog/mas-lifecycle-series-index) for every date on one page. Parts [1](/blog/mas-lifecycle-761-after-extended-support) and [2](/blog/mas-lifecycle-dual-support-deadline) cover the EAM 7.6.1 estates; this part is for everyone already on MAS.*

## 🔥 You Did the Hard Part. The Calendar Did Not Care.

If you are reading this on MAS, you probably finished a hard migration. You rebuilt customisations as automation scripts, retired direct database access, learned OpenShift, and survived an Operator upgrade that took a weekend. It is reasonable to think you bought yourself a few quiet years.

You bought less than you think. MAS follows a continuous-delivery lifecycle, and the lifecycle moves in dated steps. Three of them already sit in your calendar:

- **30 April 2026, already behind you:** MAS 8.7, 8.8 and 8.9 completed support. MAS 8.10 and 8.11 moved to Extended Support.
- **30 June 2027, 256 days from today (17 October 2026):** MAS 9.0.x transitions to Extended Support.
- **30 April 2030 and 30 June 2031:** the far ends of Extended Support for 8.10/8.11 and for 9.0.

Notice what is missing: any date for 9.1 or 9.2. IBM has published their general-availability dates (24 June 2025 and 25 June 2026) and nothing else. We will come back to why that matters, because it is the single most common planning mistake we see right now.

This post does four things. It pins each version to a verified IBM date. It explains what Extended Support actually is, so you can tell a bridge from a destination. It shows the arithmetic of the upgrade path, because IBM's n-1 rule means the number of *hops* matters as much as the target. And it gives you a decision framework for choosing between 9.1 and 9.2.

> 💡 **Key insight:** Part 2 was about what you are *allowed to run*. This part is about what you can *keep running supported*. On MAS the answer is almost never "nothing happens on the date." It is "you move from a plan that includes fixes to a plan that includes less," and the cost of that move lands on your risk register, your insurer and your next audit.

## 🗓️ Find Your Row First

Before any detail, locate yourself. Each row below is a state we see in the field.

| Your estate | Your clock | Status today (17 Oct 2026) | First action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MAS 8.7, 8.8 or 8.9** | Expired 30 Apr 2026 | **Unsupported, no Extended Support offered** | Treat as remediation; plan the multi-hop route this quarter |
| **MAS 8.10 or 8.11** | Extended Support, completion 30 Apr 2030 | Supported on a reduced, paid basis | Confirm Extended Support is on your contract; set a 9.x target and date |
| **MAS 9.0.x** | Extended Support starts 30 Jun 2027 | Fully supported for 256 more days | Plan a move to 9.1 or 9.2 that completes before the transition |
| **MAS 9.1.x** | No transition date published | Supported; check the lifecycle page again | Track IBM's page; consider 9.2 only on readiness |
| **MAS 9.2.x** | No transition date published | Supported; newest release | Keep patched (Core 9.2.8 carries a security fix) |
| **Mixed (several instances)** | One clock per instance | Depends on the worst row | Build one register; run the earliest clock first |

The mixed row is more common than anyone admits. A production instance on 9.0 and a development instance on 8.11 that nobody has touched since the migration is a typical shape. The lowest row sets the risk, and it is usually the one nobody has on a slide.

## 📋 The Dates, Source by Source

Every date below was checked against IBM's own page where we could reach it, and we say where we could not. The label tells you how much weight a date can bear.

| Version | GA date | Key lifecycle date | Source and confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8.7-CD** | 22 Feb 2022 | Completion of support 30 Apr 2026, no Extended Support | **IBM-confirmed** (supported-versions page) |
| **8.8-CD** | 26 Jul 2022 | Completion of support 30 Apr 2026, no Extended Support | **IBM-confirmed** |
| **8.9-CD** | 22 Nov 2022 | Completion of support 30 Apr 2026, no Extended Support | **IBM-confirmed** |
| **8.10-LTS** | 28 Mar 2023 | Extended Support from 30 Apr 2026; completion 30 Apr 2030 | **IBM-confirmed** (supported-versions and 8.10/8.11 lifecycle pages) |
| **8.11-LTS** | 26 Sep 2023 | Extended Support from 30 Apr 2026; completion 30 Apr 2030 | **IBM-confirmed** |
| **9.0.x** | 25 Jun 2024 | Extended Support from 30 Jun 2027 (AD26-0622); completion 30 Jun 2031 | **IBM-confirmed** (page last modified 9 Oct 2026) |
| **9.1.x** | 24 Jun 2025 | None published | **IBM-confirmed absence** (page last modified 13 Feb 2026) |
| **9.2.x** | 25 Jun 2026 | None published | **IBM-confirmed absence** (page last modified 25 Jun 2026) |

Three notes on that table.

**First**, the 8.10 and 8.11 lifecycle page lists the eligible services as Advanced Support and Extended Support, and the lifecycle policy as Continuous Delivery with a minimum of two years of support plus a one-year extension. The page was last updated on 1 May 2026. Other IBM pages we saw in search results list Support and Subscription alongside them. If your support entitlement is S&S, confirm with IBM that Extended Support is actually attached to it, because "eligible" and "purchased" are different words.

**Second**, the 9.0 page describes the policy as "Support Cycle-3: 3 years of support with critical fix extension of 1 year and usage and existing fixes for 3 (3+1+3)". The record of 9.0 shows exactly three years from GA to transition (25 June 2024 to 30 June 2027) and exactly four years from the transition to completion (30 June 2031). It tempts you to apply the same shape to 9.1 and 9.2. Do not. IBM publishes the transition date through a *later* announcement letter: AD26-0622 is a 2026 letter for a 2027 date. Until that letter exists for 9.1 or 9.2, any date you write in a plan is your guess.

**Third**, the IBM supported-versions page does not enumerate 9.x releases at all. That is a listing gap, not a statement that they are unsupported. The authoritative pages for 9.x are the per-release lifecycle pages.

### How to count the days

| From 17 Oct 2026 to | Days | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 30 Apr 2027 | 195 | EAM 7.6.1 dual-support end (Part 2) |
| 30 Jun 2027 | **256** | MAS 9.0.x transitions to Extended Support |
| 30 Apr 2030 | 1,291 | MAS 8.10/8.11 Extended Support completes |
| 30 Jun 2031 | 1,717 | MAS 9.0.x Extended Support completes |

Write the first two numbers on a whiteboard. The 256 will feel generous in October and tight by February, because the first 90 days of any upgrade programme are mostly spent deciding what you are upgrading to.

## ⛔ MAS 8.7, 8.8 and 8.9: Already Past the Date

If this is you, the honest message is short. IBM's supported-versions page lists completion of support for all three on 30 April 2026 and shows no Extended Support transition for them. Partner analysis of IBM's 20 March 2026 customer notice puts it in plain words: IBM will not offer Extended Support for these releases, and after the date there are no patches, security updates or defect fixes. That analysis is a partner's summary, not IBM's text, but it matches what the lifecycle table shows.

What that means in practice:

- **You are not on a delayed clock.** There is nothing to extend. No contract renewal restores a fix stream that IBM has said it will not provide.
- **Vulnerabilities stop being your vendor's problem and become yours.** IBM's login-service security bulletin from October 2026 (CVE-2026-105052, CVSS 7.5) lists 8.10, 8.11, 9.0, 9.1 and 9.2 as affected. Releases before 8.10 are not in it, and the reason is not that they are safe. It is that they are no longer assessed. We are inferring that from the bulletin's version list; IBM does not state it.
- **Your support calls will be redirected.** Expect to be asked to upgrade before IBM will diagnose.
- **Audit and insurance language will catch up.** Part 1 covered the same pattern for 7.6.1: an unsupported platform looks identical to a supported one until a questionnaire asks.

There is a tempting half-measure: hop to 8.10 or 8.11 first, because they are on Extended Support, and plan 9.x later. We would resist it unless a specific constraint forces it (for example, you must move a database or an OS first). Partner guidance puts it as "treat Extended Support as a bridge, not a destination," and an extra hop onto a version that is already in its reduced-support window buys you a smaller timeline than it appears to.

> ⚠️ **If you must stage through 8.11:** write down the exit date on the first day. A bridge with no end date becomes the destination by default, and Extended Support is a paid programme with reduced coverage.

## 🌉 MAS 8.10 and 8.11: A Paid Bridge to 2030

8.10 and 8.11 are the Long Term Support releases of the 8.x line. They moved to Extended Support on 30 April 2026 (announcement AD25-1237, following a July 2025 IBM statement that Extended Support would be available for them), and IBM lists completion of Extended, Sustained and extension availability on 30 April 2030.

A date four years out feels like a lot of room. Read what the programme is first.

### What Extended Support covers

IBM's own pages tell you the dates and the eligible services. They do not set out the coverage in one readable paragraph. The best public description is from a partner (Maxiron), summarising IBM's notice:

| Item | Full support | Extended Support (per partner summary) |
|---|---|---|
| Product usage help | Yes | Yes |
| Patches to existing code | Yes | Yes |
| Fixes for critical defects | Yes | Yes |
| Proactive security fixes | Yes | **Excluded** |
| New functionality | Per release | **Excluded** |
| Cost | In S&S / Advanced Support | **Paid programme** |

Treat that table as a map for your contract conversation, not as the contract. The "excluded" rows are exactly where practical risk sits: security fixes you did not ask for, and capabilities you will want in 2028. If you rely on 8.10 or 8.11 past 2026, read the Extended Support terms attached to your own entitlement and get IBM's answer in writing on what a "critical defect" is for your use.

### A data point on security help

CVE-2026-105052 is a path-traversal weakness in the MAS login service (CWE-22, CVSS 3.1 score 7.5, network-reachable, no privileges, high confidentiality impact). IBM's bulletin, published 3 October 2026 and last modified 14 October, lists 8.10, 8.11, 9.0, 9.1 and 9.2 as affected, with these fixes:

| Release | Fix |
|---|---|
| 9.0 | Core 9.0.33 or later |
| 9.1 | Core 9.1.27 or later |
| 9.2 | Core 9.2.8 or later (IBM's readme is dated 7 Oct 2026) |
| 8.10 | CoreIdp-Login 3.2.201 (workaround, no product upgrade listed) |
| 8.11 | CoreIdp-Login 4.0.179 (workaround, no product upgrade listed) |

Two readings are possible, and both are useful. The optimistic one: IBM did ship a component-level fix for the Extended Support releases, so security help is not nil. The cautious one: the fix for 8.10 and 8.11 is a workaround through a component override, with a note that the `imageTags` override should only be removed after the October 2026 monthly update or later. That is operational work on your side, on a release that is not getting a normal fix. If that same bulletin had been about a flaw that needs a code change deep in Manage, we do not know what the answer would have been. Do not turn a single data point into a policy.

### What to do on 8.10 or 8.11

- **Confirm entitlement.** Ask IBM or your partner which support product (Advanced Support, Extended Support, S&S) is attached to which instance, and file the answer.
- **Set an internal end date earlier than 2030.** Extended Support completion is not the date you can safely run to. It is the date IBM stops. The date your auditors, insurers and add-on vendors stop is usually sooner.
- **Pick a 9.x target now** (see the decision section below) so that every other decision, from OpenShift version to database, is aimed at it.
- **Audit your add-ons.** The 8.x to 9.x journey changes more than Manage. Applications, industry solutions and add-ons each have their own 9.x availability.

## ⏳ MAS 9.0: The Next Date on the Calendar

9.0 is the version most teams on a modern MAS landscape are running, and it has the nearest hard date. IBM's 9.0.x lifecycle page, last modified 9 October 2026, shows:

- General Availability: 25 June 2024 (announcement AD24-0483)
- Transition to Extended Support: **30 June 2027** (announcement AD26-0622)
- Completion of extended, sustained and extension availability: 30 June 2031
- Policy: Support Cycle-3, 3+1+3

The page does not list a separate extended-support start date. The 30 June 2027 transition date is when Extended Support begins.

### What actually changes on 30 June 2027

Nothing breaks. Operators keep reconciling, work orders keep flowing, integrations keep running. What changes is the support relationship:

| Dimension | Before 30 Jun 2027 | After 30 Jun 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| IBM support status | Full support for 9.0 | Extended Support (paid, reduced) |
| New fixes | Normal patch stream (e.g. Core 9.0.33 for the October login fix) | Critical defect fixes and patches to existing code, per Extended terms |
| Security fixes | Included as part of full support | Not proactively provided (per partner summary of Extended Support) |
| New features | None for 9.0 anyway; all new capability lands in 9.1/9.2 | None |
| Compatibility pressure | Add-ons and partners still certify against 9.0 | Partners increasingly certify against 9.1 and 9.2 only |

The last row is the quiet one. Third-party integrations, certified connectors and partner add-ons tend to follow IBM's lifecycle, so the practical squeeze on 9.0 begins before the Extended date, not after.

### Why 256 days is shorter than it looks

An honest upgrade programme has six stages, and most of them are not technical:

1. Inventory every instance, application, add-on, integration and customisation.
2. Choose the target and generate the compatibility report for it.
3. Fix the prerequisites (OpenShift, database, storage, identity).
4. Rehearse the upgrade in a clone of production, at least twice.
5. Schedule the production change in an outage window your business agrees to.
6. Hypercare, then retire the old thing and record the evidence.

If each one is a month, the schedule is already full. Here is a back-plan from 30 June 2027, assuming the aim is to be *on* a later version before the transition date:

| Weeks before 30 Jun 2027 | Approx. date | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| 36 | mid-Oct 2026 | Inventory started; owner named; target shortlist agreed |
| 30 | late Nov 2026 | Target chosen (9.1 or 9.2); compatibility reports generated |
| 24 | early Jan 2027 | Prerequisite work scoped and funded (OpenShift, Db2, storage) |
| 18 | mid-Feb 2027 | First rehearsal upgrade on a production clone completed |
| 12 | late Mar 2027 | Second rehearsal; customisation and integration regression passed |
| 8 | late Apr 2027 | Change window approved; business sign-off |
| 4 | early Jun 2027 | Production upgrade complete; hypercare begins |
| 0 | 30 Jun 2027 | Transition date; evidence filed |

If you also hold EAM 7.6.1 estates, the 30 April 2027 date from Part 2 sits inside this plan and wins any resource conflict. Plan them as one programme, not as two projects arguing over the same people.

## 🪜 The Arithmetic Nobody Does: How Many Hops?

Here is the part of upgrade planning that surprises senior stakeholders. The question "how long does it take to get to 9.2" has no single answer, because the answer depends on where you start. IBM's upgrade documentation states the rule:

> "Maximo Application Suite upgrade policy supports n-1 versions in a cluster, which means that you can upgrade directly from the version just before the current one. For example, if the current version of Maximo Application Suite is 9.1, then you can upgrade directly from 9.0 in a cluster."

Apply it:

| Starting point | Target 9.1 | Target 9.2 |
|---|---|---|
| **9.1** | n/a | **1 step** (direct) |
| **9.0** | **1 step** (direct) | **2 steps** (9.0 → 9.1 → 9.2) |
| **8.11 / 8.10** | Multiple steps (confirm the exact route with IBM) | Multiple steps |
| **8.7 – 8.9** | Multiple steps, possibly via 8.10/8.11 | Multiple steps |

For the 8.x rows, we do not state a single route. IBM's text is about n-1 from the *current* version, and which 8.x releases connect to 9.0 directly depends on the channel history and on what your applications support. Verify the route for your own starting version in IBM's upgrade documentation before you cost it. What we will say with confidence is that "8.x to 9.2" is not one weekend.

### What a hop costs

A hop is not a button. For each one you pay:

- A **rehearsal** on a clone of production, because the failure modes show up on your data.
- An **outage window**, or at least a read-only window, agreed with operations.
- A **regression cycle** for customisations, automation scripts, integrations and reports.
- **Add-on and industry-solution checks**, because an application that does not exist at the next version blocks the upgrade. IBM's guidance is that an application or add-on no longer available for upgrade must be deactivated and deleted first.
- **Platform work** on OpenShift, which constrains the order of everything else. IBM notes that App Connect and Cloud Pak for Data do not support odd-numbered OpenShift Container Platform versions, so if Maximo Collaborate or Predict is deployed you need an even-numbered version.

IBM release notes and the community also contain warnings about specific hops, and these are worth reading before you commit. As reported in IBM's own readmes, a 9.1.21 online upgrade from 9.0.26 could fail because of a missing column, with a fix in 9.1.22, and a 9.0.47 IT patch note warns that moving from 9.0 to 9.1.x is susceptible to failure in some circumstances. We have not reproduced either; treat them as reasons to read the readme for the *exact* patch levels you are moving between, not as general verdicts on 9.1.

> 💡 **Key insight:** A two-hop route does not cost twice as much as a one-hop route. It costs more, because you rehearse and test the intermediate version too, even though you do not intend to stay there. If a programme manager says "9.0 to 9.2, same effort as 9.1 to 9.2," ask them to show the rehearsal plan for the middle step.

### The alternative: build new, migrate across

Some partners prefer to avoid the hop ladder for old estates by building a fresh MAS on the target version and migrating the data and configuration across. It can shorten the calendar and give a cleaner platform, but it is a migration project, with its own data-mapping, integration-cutover and licensing implications, not a shortcut around the n-1 rule. Our [MAS migration playbook](/blog/mas-migration-playbook) covers the phases of that route, and [MAS 9 upgrade gotchas](/blog/mas9-upgrade-gotchas) covers the traps that apply to both.

## 🎯 9.1 or 9.2: Choosing the Target

Which one should you land on? The wrong way to decide is to guess the support dates. The right way is a readiness comparison.

### What we know about each release

| Fact | MAS 9.1 | MAS 9.2 |
|---|---|---|
| GA date | 24 Jun 2025 (AD25-1186) | 25 Jun 2026 (AD26-0673) |
| Published transition date | None | None |
| Lifecycle policy text | Support Cycle-3, 3+1+3 | Support Cycle-3, 3+1+3 |
| Manage runtime | Java 17 | Java 25 |
| User and group management APIs | Deprecated | **Removed** (replaced by newer user management APIs) |
| User and group data | As per 9.1 model | Stored in the Manage relational database; the 9.1→9.2 upgrade migrates authentication mappings and SCIM fields |
| Collaborate (ex-Assist) | Suite add-on | Moves toward a Manage add-on |
| Db2 | Per 9.1 compatibility | Db2 12 supported; the 11→12 upgrade needs opt-in and a licence file |
| Patch train (as of Oct 2026) | Core 9.1.27 | Core 9.2.8; Manage 9.2.4 |
| Maturity | A year of patches | Four months of GA, but with interim cores through 9.2.8 |

The first practical conclusion is symmetrical. Because IBM has not published transition dates for either, the support calendars are equally unknown. The second is about *direction*: 9.2 is where new capability and the fix stream will concentrate, so a 9.1 landing is likely to be an intermediate one for any estate with a multi-year horizon.

### A decision table

| If this is true for you | Lean toward | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You are on 9.1 already | **9.2**, when your readiness checklist is clean | One direct step; avoids a later forced move |
| You are on 9.0 and have until June 2027 | **9.2 via 9.1**, with the 9.1 stop as a short pause | Two rehearsals, one production sequence |
| You are on 9.0 and cannot take identity or API changes this year | **9.1** | Less change per hop; accept a second upgrade later |
| You rely on user-management APIs for provisioning | **9.1 first** | 9.2 removes the deprecated APIs; fix the callers before the platform |
| You use add-ons without a confirmed 9.2 version | **9.1 (or wait)** | A missing application blocks the upgrade |
| You run Oracle or non-Db2 databases | Check compatibility report | Do not assume the Db2 12 story applies to you |
| You are on 8.x and facing multiple hops anyway | **Go for the latest viable target** | You are already paying for the hops; landing early wastes them |
| Your change windows are rare and expensive | **9.2** | You can only afford one more destination |

### 9.2 prerequisites that bite

This is a summary; the full ranked list is in [the MAS 9.2 upgrade watch-list](/blog/mas-92-upgrade-watchlist), and [the version comparison](/blog/mas-92-vs-91-vs-90) shows what each release adds.

**Db2 12 and its licence gate.** MAS 9.2 supports Db2 12. We found no IBM statement that makes Db2 12 mandatory for 9.2, so do not assume it is. If you choose to move Db2 from 11 to 12 as part of the programme, IBM's upgrade prerequisites state that it needs explicit opt-in (the `db2_v12_upgrade` flag) and a valid Db2 Warehouse licence file, and that without one Db2 12 runs on a 30-day trial licence that causes production outages on expiry. New Db2 12 installations do not need the file, so a greenfield test environment will not expose this. The simplest policy: decide separately whether the Db2 move happens in this window, and never bundle it in by accident.

**OpenShift versions.** The IBM CLI catalog page lists catalogs v9-260924 and v9-261007 as supporting OpenShift 4.18 through 4.22, with the Cloud Pak for Data range shown as 7.0–8.0 and MAS CLI 5.3.1 (our knowledge base records CPD as 5.3.1, a discrepancy we flag below). The same page lists OCP 4.18–4.22 end of service as 3 August 2027, while a lifecycle note on the page says 4.18 extended support ends on 25 February 2027, and the page does not reconcile them. What it means: catalog support is not the same as "9.2 requires this". IBM deliberately does not publish inline version floors for MAS 9.2 and directs you to the Software Product Compatibility Report for your chosen version and applications. Use the report as the source of truth, and put your own OpenShift lifecycle dates (Red Hat's, not just IBM's) into the plan, because your cluster may need to move twice in this window: once for MAS, once for Red Hat.

**Removed user-management APIs.** If anything in your estate provisions users through the APIs that were deprecated in 9.1, it breaks on 9.2. Find the callers before the upgrade, not after.

**Identity migration.** User and group data moves into the Manage database in 9.2. A pre-upgrade job (`datamodel-migration`) updates authentication mappings and synchronises SCIM fields, including `externalId`. IBM's 9.2 Core patch train also contains a fix for an upgrade defect where MAXUSER fields were overwritten when MASISSUER was null (DT501598, Core 9.2.6). Land on a current patch, not on the first build.

**Java 25.** Manage 9.2 moves to Java 25 from Java 17 in 9.1. Customisations and libraries that assumed an older runtime are a regression-test line item. The community guide to the 9.1 move already warns about Java 17 and the removal of Nashorn, so if you are skipping from 9.0 you inherit both lots.

**Add-on parity.** Not every 9.0 application has the same availability at 9.2. Check each activated application and industry solution against the target before you commit.

### A quick inventory you can run

These commands sketch how to collect the facts the compatibility report needs. Adjust names to your instance IDs, and treat them as a starting point rather than a verified runbook for your cluster.

```bash
# OpenShift cluster version (the platform side of the plan)
oc get clusterversion version -o jsonpath='{.status.desired.version}{"\n"}'

# MAS core channel and installed version, per instance (replace inst1)
oc get subscription.operators.coreos.com ibm-mas -n mas-inst1-core \
  -o jsonpath='{.spec.channel}{"\n"}'
oc get suite inst1 -n mas-inst1-core \
  -o jsonpath='{.status.versions.reconciled}{"\n"}'

# Which application workspaces are activated (add-on parity input)
oc get manageapp,manageworkspace -A

# Which catalog the cluster is using (compare to the CLI catalog page)
oc get catalogsource ibm-operator-catalog -n openshift-marketplace \
  -o jsonpath='{.spec.image}{"\n"}'
```

Put the output of those commands, per instance, into the same register as your lifecycle dates. A register that says "9.0, Extended Support from 30 Jun 2027, OCP 4.16, Db2 11, Collaborate active" is the document that finds the problem in week one, not week twenty.

## 🚫 Mistakes That Turn a Plan Into an Emergency

| Mistake | Why it bites | Better |
|---|---|---|
| **Adding three years to a 9.1 or 9.2 GA date** | IBM has not published those dates; the 9.0 transition was announced separately | Track the lifecycle page; plan on readiness |
| **Treating Extended Support as "supported"** | It is a reduced, paid programme; security and features are excluded per partner summaries | Read your Extended terms; set an earlier exit |
| **Counting versions instead of hops** | n-1 means 9.0 to 9.2 is two steps | Cost each hop with its own rehearsal |
| **Letting OpenShift lag** | Catalog support is a moving window; your Red Hat lifecycle moves too | Put cluster lifecycle dates in the same register |
| **Bundling Db2 12 in by accident** | The 11→12 upgrade needs opt-in and a licence file; the failure shows 30 days later | Make the Db2 decision explicit and separate |
| **Testing 9.2 on a greenfield cluster only** | A clean install does not reveal upgrade-path defects or licence gates | Rehearse on a production clone |
| **Ignoring development and test instances** | The oldest instance sets the risk row | One register, all instances |
| **Waiting for TechXchange or a 9.3 announcement** | A new release will not shorten your hops; it may add one | Plan against what is shipped |
| **Assuming partners will keep certifying 9.0** | Ecosystem follows IBM's lifecycle | Ask each add-on vendor for its 9.x roadmap in writing |

On that last row about timing: an Oct 2026 code change in IBM's open-source CLI (release 23.14.0) hints that a 9.3 is in preparation, with an internal certificate issuer behaving differently on 9.3 and later. There is no announcement, Feature Channel or date, and any mid-2027 guess is our inference. For planning purposes, there is no reason to stand still waiting for it.

## 📣 What We Hear in Steering Meetings

> *"We are on LTS, so we are fine until 2030."* — Extended Support, not fine. The date is IBM's last day, not yours.

> *"9.0 is the latest we deployed, so we are current."* — 9.2 went GA in June 2026. Current is a moving target, and 9.0 has a date.

> *"We will skip 9.1 and go straight to 9.2."* — From 9.0 that is not what IBM's n-1 policy describes. Ask how the route works.

> *"Why not wait for 9.3?"* — Because the clock on your current version runs regardless.

> *"Our integrator says it is a six-week project."* — For which starting version, how many hops, and how many rehearsals?

> *"Reliabilityweb says 73% of users plan to upgrade within a year."* — That is a survey claim reported by a trade body; useful for context, not for your risk register. Your plan stands or falls on your own dates.

## 🔟 The Ten Rules of the Upgrade Clock

1. **Find your version's row first.** 8.7–8.9, 8.10/8.11, 9.0, 9.1, 9.2 each have a different clock.
2. **Use IBM's lifecycle page, not a summary.** Dates move when announcement letters arrive.
3. **Never infer a date IBM has not published.** 9.1 and 9.2 have none today.
4. **Count hops, not versions.** n-1 decides the length of the road.
5. **Extended Support is a bridge.** Write the exit date on day one.
6. **Pick the target on readiness.** Compatibility reports, add-ons, API callers, Java and identity.
7. **Rehearse on a clone of production.** Every hop, not just the last.
8. **Decide Db2 12 explicitly.** Never inherit the licence trap by accident.
9. **Plan the cluster lifecycle too.** OpenShift dates are part of the MAS plan.
10. **File the evidence.** Dates, reports, rehearsals and decisions go in one register.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Is MAS 8.9 or earlier still supported?**
No. IBM's supported-versions page lists 8.7, 8.8 and 8.9 with completion of support on 30 April 2026 and no Extended Support transition. Partner analysis of IBM's March 2026 notice says no Extended Support is offered, so there are no patches, security updates or defect fixes. Treat the upgrade as remediation of an unsupported production platform.

**What does Extended Support on 8.10 and 8.11 actually give me?**
IBM shows transition to Extended Support on 30 April 2026 and completion on 30 April 2030, with Advanced Support and Extended Support as eligible services. A partner summary describes usage help, patches to existing code and critical defect fixes, but no proactive security fixes or new functionality. IBM did publish a CoreIdp-Login workaround for CVE-2026-105052 on 8.10 and 8.11, which shows help is possible but is not a promise.

**When does MAS 9.0 lose full support, and what happens then?**
IBM's 9.0.x page, last modified 9 October 2026, shows transition to Extended Support on 30 June 2027 (AD26-0622) and completion on 30 June 2031. From 17 October 2026 that is 256 days. After the transition you are on the reduced, paid programme, so an estate that wants full support must be on a later version before the end of June 2027.

**Can I jump straight from 8.10 or 9.0 to MAS 9.2?**
IBM's policy supports n-1 versions in a cluster. 9.1 to 9.2 is direct, 9.0 to 9.2 goes through 9.1, and 8.x to 9.2 takes multiple steps. A new build with data migration is the alternative for older estates, but it is a project in its own right. Count the hops before you promise a date.

**Should I target 9.1 or 9.2?**
Usually 9.2, if your add-ons and integrations are ready, because it is the newest release and landing on 9.1 typically means a second upgrade. Choose 9.1 when you depend on APIs 9.2 removes, an add-on lacks a 9.2 version, or you cannot absorb the Java 25, identity and Db2 changes now. IBM has published no transition dates for either, so decide on readiness.
