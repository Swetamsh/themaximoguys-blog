# Building the Upgrade Business Case Against the Clock

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** The Maximo owner, platform lead or reliability executive who now has to stand in front of finance and explain why an upgrade needs money this fiscal year. You have read the dates. You need the numbers, the risks and the plan that go with them.

*Part 4 of 4 in the MAS Lifecycle series. [Start with the series index](/blog/mas-lifecycle-series-index) for every date on one page. Part [1](/blog/mas-lifecycle-761-after-extended-support) covers 7.6.1 Sustained Support, Part [2](/blog/mas-lifecycle-dual-support-deadline) the licence deadline, and Part [3](/blog/mas-lifecycle-8x-9-0-upgrade-clock) the MAS version clocks. This part prices all of it.*

## 🔥 The Meeting You Are About to Walk Into

You know how this goes. You have a slide with a date on it. The date is real. The finance partner across the table nods, says "so nothing breaks on that day?", and you have to answer honestly: no, nothing breaks on that day.

That honest answer is the whole difficulty. Every deadline in this series is a support or licensing boundary, not a failure boundary. Operators keep reconciling. Work orders keep flowing. The cost of crossing the line arrives slowly, in four different shapes, and none of them looks like an invoice. A business case that says "IBM says so" loses to a business case that says "this costs us less than the alternative." Finance does not fund calendars. It funds comparisons.

> *"We upgraded a few years ago. Why are we doing this again?"* — The most common first question, and the right one. The answer is that MAS is a continuous-delivery platform and the lifecycle moves in dated steps.

> *"Can we just pay for Extended Support and revisit in 2029?"* — A legitimate option, and one of the three you should price. It is only a bad option when nobody has priced it.

> *"What does this give us?"* — Careful. If your answer is a list of AI features, you have answered a different question. The question is "what does it cost us not to?"

This post gives you the structure for answering that. It does five things:

1. Pins the case to the **dates IBM has actually published**, and labels the ones it has not.
2. Prices the **cost of staying** in four buckets, and tells you which numbers only IBM can supply.
3. Prices the **cost of moving** in hops, with a worked example whose assumptions are labelled as ours, not IBM's.
4. Builds the **risk register**, the **phased plan** and a **one-page executive summary**.
5. Ends with the **questions you put to IBM in writing**, because a business case built on verbal assurances is built on sand.

> 💡 **Key insight:** The goal of a business case is not to prove the upgrade is good. It is to make the cost of every option visible, so the decision the executive makes is a decision between priced options, not between a price and a fear.

### The one date you must not invent

IBM publishes the transition date for a release through a *later* announcement letter. AD26-0622 is a 2026 letter that fixed a 2027 date for 9.0. Neither the 9.1 nor the 9.2 lifecycle page carries one. Every business case we see that adds "three years" to a GA date is writing an IBM announcement that IBM has not made. Put "not yet published" in the plan and a review date next to it. That is stronger than a guess, not weaker, because it shows the reader you know the difference.

## 💸 Part One: The Cost of Staying

Staying is a decision, and decisions have costs. The mistake is to think the cost of staying is zero until the date and infinite after it. The real shape is a slope that starts before the date. We split it into four buckets because each has a different owner, a different source of numbers and a different level of confidence.

| Bucket | What it is | Who owns the number | Confidence | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Support premium** | What you pay for Extended or Sustained Support instead of full support | Procurement / IBM | Low until quoted | Written IBM or partner quote |
| **2. Unpatched exposure** | Expected loss from vulnerabilities, audit findings and insurance consequences | CISO / Risk | Medium (estimate) | Your risk model, labelled as such |
| **3. Ecosystem drag** | Cost of add-ons, connectors and skills no longer certified or available for your version | Platform lead | Medium | Vendor roadmaps obtained in writing |
| **4. Compounding delay** | The upgrade gets bigger and costlier the longer you wait | Programme manager | Medium | Your own hop and rehearsal model |

### Bucket 1: the support premium

**IBM does not publish an Extended Support price.** We searched for one while writing this post and found only vendor-adjacent commentary, some of which quotes percentage uplifts for Extended Support on 7.6.1 and some of which conflicts with other sources on end dates. We have not carried any of those figures into this post, and we recommend you do not carry them into yours. The lifecycle pages say what Extended Support is called and when it starts. Part 3 sets out what partner summaries say it covers: usage help, patches to existing code and fixes for critical defects, with no proactive security fixes and no new functionality. That description is not IBM's contract language.

What you can do about bucket 1 is concrete:

- Ask IBM, or your business partner, for a **written quote** for each version and entitlement you hold.
- Ask what the **term** of the quote is. A premium that is fixed for a year and then repriced is a different number from one fixed to the end date.
- Ask what **conditions** attach. For Sustained Support on 7.6.1, partner sources report an eligibility gate of 7.6.1.3 or later and a documented migration plan (see Part 1). If similar conditions attach to your MAS Extended Support, the cost of not having a plan is also a bucket-1 cost.

If you cannot obtain the figure before the budget cycle, put a **named assumption** in the case: "Extended Support premium modelled at X pending written quote, owner: Procurement, due: 30 November." That is a legitimate number. A figure without an owner is not.

### Bucket 2: unpatched exposure

This is the bucket that gets argued about, so be disciplined about it. An unpatched platform is not a certain loss. It is a probability of a loss with a distribution of impacts. Your security team can estimate that, and your finance team will respect it more if it is presented as a range with a stated basis.

The most useful evidence you can give them this quarter is a current, IBM-sourced example of how the support state changes your options. IBM's security bulletin for **CVE-2026-105052**, a path traversal in the MAS login service (CWE-22, CVSS 7.5, vector `AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N`), initially published on 3 October 2026 and last modified on 14 October 2026, lists affected versions 8.10, 8.11, 9.0, 9.1 and 9.2. The fixed versions are:

| Release line | Fix path per IBM's bulletin |
|---|---|
| 9.0 | Core 9.0.33 or later |
| 9.1 | Core 9.1.27 or later |
| 9.2 | Core 9.2.8 or later |
| 8.10 | No full fix listed; workaround is a `spec.settings.imageTags.coreidplogin` override on the Coreidp CR, CoreIdp Login 3.2.201 |
| 8.11 | No full fix listed; same override, CoreIdp Login 4.0.179 |

Read that table as a business-case exhibit, not as a security lesson. An estate on a fully supported release takes a Core update. An estate on 8.10 or 8.11 gets a workaround that carries an instruction from IBM: remove the override only *after* updating to the October 2026 monthly update or later. IBM did help the older releases here, and that is worth saying plainly. But a workaround you must remember to remove is operational debt, and an estate on 8.9 or earlier is not listed at all, because no assessment is made of releases outside support. The bulletin does not say what IBM will do for the next vulnerability. It shows what the support state looks like *this time*.

For the 7.6.1 estates, the exposure story is different and Part 1 covers it: Sustained Support, as described by partner sources, means existing fixes and no new ones, so exposure grows with every disclosure against the product or its underlying components, and your compensating controls become the only lever you have. A probability-times-impact estimate for that is a reasonable thing for your security team to build, as long as the case calls it an estimate.

### Bucket 3: ecosystem drag

You do not run MAS alone. You run it with identity providers, certified connectors, partner add-ons, reporting tools, mobile devices and a cluster platform with its own lifecycle. All of those follow IBM's lifecycle with a lag. The practical result is that the squeeze on a version begins *before* its Extended Support date, not after it.

Ask each dependency owner for three things in writing:

1. Which MAS versions the vendor certifies today, and which it will certify in 12 months.
2. Whether the vendor charges differently for supporting a version IBM has moved to Extended Support.
3. The latest platform prerequisites (OpenShift, Db2, MongoDB) the vendor supports.

Also price your **skills** risk. A team trained on 8.x deployment tooling, with an unpatched cluster and no one who has run a 9.x upgrade rehearsal, is carrying a key-person risk that does not appear in any IBM document. It will appear in your plan as a contractor line.

### Bucket 4: compounding delay

This is the bucket that most surprises executives, and it is the most valuable one to model, because it flips the framing from "cost of upgrading" to "cost of upgrading later."

An upgrade gets more expensive with time for four independent reasons:

- **More hops.** IBM's n-1 policy supports upgrading directly from the version just before the current one. If you stay on 9.0 while 9.1 and 9.2 are both out, you owe two hops to reach 9.2. If a 9.3 appears, you may owe three. Each hop has its own rehearsal, outage window and regression cycle.
- **More drift.** Every month, customisations, integrations and reports are added by people who assume the platform will stay still. A year of drift adds regression scope.
- **More compatibility debt.** Add-ons you adopt now are the ones you will have to re-certify later.
- **Less leverage.** A migration planned eight months before a hard date has fewer options than one planned two years before it: fewer weekends, fewer vendor slots and a poorer negotiating position on contractors.

On the question of whether to wait for MAS 9.3: the open-source ibm-mas CLI release 23.14.0 carries a code change that behaves differently on "MAS 9.3+" (a namespace-scoped internal certificate issuer). That hints that a 9.3 is in preparation. There is no announcement, no Feature Channel and no date. Any timing is inference. A new release does not shorten your hop count; it may lengthen it. It is not a reason to stand still.

> 💡 **Key insight:** The cost of staying is rarely one big number. It is four small numbers that grow together. Put them side by side and the picture changes: bucket 1 is a price, buckets 2 and 3 are estimates, and bucket 4 is a trend line. Finance can work with a trend line.

## 🪜 Part Two: The Cost of Moving

Costing the move is where most business cases get either fanciful or fatal. Fanciful means a single number from an integrator's sales deck. Fatal means a single number that grows by 60 percent after discovery. The fix is to cost the move as **hops**, each with named components, and to give a range.

### Count your hops first

From Part 3, applying IBM's stated n-1 rule:

| Starting point | To 9.1 | To 9.2 | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **9.1** | n/a | **1 hop** | Plan the direct upgrade |
| **9.0** | **1 hop** | **2 hops** (via 9.1) | Decide whether you pay for the intermediate rehearsal or land on 9.1 |
| **8.10 or 8.11** | Multiple hops (confirm route with IBM) | Multiple hops | Ask IBM for the supported route in writing; consider build-new and migrate |
| **8.7 to 8.9** | Multiple hops | Multiple hops | Treat as remediation of an unsupported platform |
| **EAM 7.6.1** | Migration, not an upgrade | Migration, not an upgrade | Use the migration playbook; this is a different cost model |

For 7.6.1 estates the right comparator is not an upgrade at all but a migration, and the cost components are different: data preparation, rationalisation of customisations and integration redesign dominate. Our [7.6 to MAS migration playbook](/blog/mas-migration-playbook) lays out the six phases, and its timeline of roughly 60 weeks end to end for a full migration is the reason the 30 April 2027 date is already tight for anyone who has not started. The playbook is a methodology, not a promise for your estate, so take the phase structure and replace its durations with your own.

### The components of one hop

Every hop carries the same cost lines. Naming them is what stops a case from being a guess.

| Component | What you are paying for | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery and inventory** | Applications, add-ons, integrations, automation scripts, reports, extensions | Platform lead |
| **Compatibility report** | Per-target checks of applications, add-ons and industry solutions | Platform lead / IBM |
| **Prerequisite work** | OpenShift, Db2, MongoDB, storage and identity upgrades that must precede the hop | Infrastructure |
| **Rehearsal 1** | Upgrade of a production clone, with data, to find platform and data issues | Platform + DBA |
| **Rehearsal 2** | Second upgrade with fixes applied, ideally timed against the real window | Platform + DBA |
| **Regression test** | Business process, integration, report and extension testing | Business + QA |
| **Outage window** | Production downtime or read-only period, plus the cost of lost throughput | Operations |
| **Cutover and hypercare** | Execution, support and fixes in the weeks after | Platform + vendor |
| **Evidence** | Documentation for audit, insurers and the next budget cycle | Programme manager |

Two items on that list are routinely missing from first-draft budgets. The first is the **second rehearsal**, because everyone assumes the first will pass. The second is the **outage cost**. If your plant loses a shift of work-order throughput, someone in operations can put a number on it, and it belongs in the case.

IBM's own description of its SaaS upgrade service is a useful reference for who carries which cost. In IBM's customer documentation for hosted Maximo, the provider installs the new version and upgrades the source database, while the customer is responsible for regression testing and remediation of customised screens, reports, integrations and Java extensions, and the process requires planned outages. That is documentation for a managed service, not for self-managed MAS on OpenShift, but the split is the same one you will see in any programme: the platform mechanics are the smaller line, and your own regression scope is the larger one.

### A model you can copy

Here is a deliberately simple cost model. Put it in a spreadsheet. The point is that every input has a name, an owner and a confidence, so the case can be revised in a meeting without being rebuilt.

```yaml
# Upgrade cost model, per estate. ALL values below are placeholders.
# Replace each with your own number, owner and confidence before use.
estate:
  name: "Plant fleet, 3 environments"
  current_version: "9.0"
  target_version: "9.2"
  hops: 2                       # n-1 policy: 9.0 -> 9.1 -> 9.2
  environments: [DEV, TEST, PROD]

assumptions:
  rehearsals_per_hop: 2         # owner: platform lead, confidence: medium
  regression_person_days: TBD   # owner: business process owners, confidence: low
  outage_hours_per_hop: TBD     # owner: operations, confidence: low
  outage_cost_per_hour: TBD     # owner: operations/finance, confidence: low
  prerequisite_work_days: TBD   # owner: infrastructure, confidence: medium
  contractor_day_rate: TBD      # owner: procurement, confidence: high once quoted
  extended_support_premium: TBD # owner: procurement, source: WRITTEN IBM QUOTE ONLY

formula:
  effort_days: >
    hops * (rehearsals_per_hop * rehearsal_days
            + regression_person_days
            + cutover_days)
    + prerequisite_work_days
  cost_to_move: >
    effort_days * blended_day_rate
    + hops * outage_hours_per_hop * outage_cost_per_hour
    + licence_and_tooling_changes
  cost_to_stay_per_year: >
    extended_support_premium
    + expected_unpatched_loss          # CISO estimate, labelled as such
    + ecosystem_workarounds
    + deferred_hop_growth              # added effort if the upgrade slips a year

output: "Show low / expected / high. Never a single number."
```

Notice what the model does *not* contain: any IBM price. That is deliberate. IBM does not publish Maximo Application Suite prices publicly, and the licence entitlement is yours to read, not ours to guess.

### A worked example, with the assumptions on the table

To show the shape of the reasoning, here is an illustrative estate. **The inputs are ours, chosen for the example, and are not benchmarks, IBM figures or any customer's data.**

| Input | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Starting version | MAS 9.0 | Known |
| Target | 9.2 | Decision pending |
| Hops (n-1) | 2 | IBM policy |
| Environments | DEV, TEST, PROD | Known |
| Rehearsals per hop | 2 | Assumption |
| Regression scope | 38 automation scripts, 11 integrations, 24 reports | Inventory needed |
| Blended effort per hop | 60 to 110 person-days | Assumption (range) |
| Prerequisite work | 20 to 45 person-days (cluster and database) | Assumption (range) |

Then the model gives:

| Scenario | Effort calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | 2 hops × 60 days + 20 prerequisite days | **140 person-days** |
| **Expected** | 2 hops × 85 days + 32 prerequisite days | **202 person-days** |
| **High** | 2 hops × 110 days + 45 prerequisite days | **265 person-days** |

Now apply the single most useful sensitivity test: **what if the estate lands on 9.1 instead?** One hop, fewer rehearsals, the same inventory.

| Scenario | Effort calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | 1 hop × 60 days + 20 prerequisite days | 80 person-days |
| **Expected** | 1 hop × 85 days + 32 prerequisite days | 117 person-days |
| **High** | 1 hop × 110 days + 45 prerequisite days | 155 person-days |

That is roughly a 40 percent reduction in effort on paper. But there is a catch, and it is the reason the model needs a bucket-4 line: 9.1 has no published transition date either, and landing there leaves you one hop from 9.2 for the next time. If the estate will be forced to take that second hop within three years, the 9.1 route is not the cheaper one. It is the same cost, paid in two instalments, with a second outage and a second regression cycle. Whether the hop count saves you money depends on how long you can honestly say you will stay.

> 💡 **Key insight:** A model like this does not tell you the answer. It tells you which question to argue about. In the example, the argument is not "9.1 or 9.2?" It is "how many years do we really intend to stay on whatever we land on?"

### Hidden lines that belong in the cost of moving

| Hidden cost | Why it appears | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Db2 12 licence decision** | 9.2 supports Db2 12; the 11 to 12 move needs an opt-in and a licence file. See the [9.2 upgrade watch-list](/blog/mas-92-upgrade-watchlist) | Make the Db2 decision explicit and separate; cost it separately |
| **Removed user-management APIs** | 9.2 removes user and group management APIs that 9.1 deprecated | Inventory callers; cost the rewrite |
| **Java 25 runtime** | Manage on 9.2 runs Java 25, which affects custom Java extensions | Include extension rebuild and test in regression |
| **OpenShift lifecycle** | The ibm-mas catalog support window moves (v9-260924 and later list OCP 4.18 to 4.22; v9-260827 was the last with 4.16) | Put cluster dates in the same register as MAS dates |
| **Add-on parity** | An add-on with no version for your target blocks the upgrade | Ask each vendor in writing before the target decision |
| **Licensing changes** | Entitlement may change with consolidation or product restructuring | Review AppPoints position with IBM; see below |
| **Knowledge transfer** | Few teams have rehearsed a 9.x upgrade end to end | Budget a rehearsal as training, not only as testing |

The 9.2-specific items are covered in depth in [MAS 9 upgrade gotchas](/blog/mas9-upgrade-gotchas) and the watch-list, so we will not repeat them. What matters for the business case is that they are *line items with names*, not a generic "technical risk" contingency.

## 🧾 The Licence Line: Where Money Can Come Back

A cost-of-moving section that only has costs in it is half a case. The other half is the licence position, because this is the one place where an upgrade or migration can *reduce* a bill rather than add one.

MAS 9 restructured the packaging of some modules. As [our licensing post](/blog/mas-features-licensing-free-paid) sets out, Calibration, Linear Assets, Maximo Mobile, Scheduler and Reliability Strategies are described as included in base Manage with no additional AppPoints, where legacy contracts may have carried separate charges. The post's central advice is that IBM does not proactively audit your contract and tell you what you might be overpaying. We have not re-verified every packaging statement in that post against IBM's current entitlement documents for this series, so treat it as a prompt for the conversation, not as a quote. The sound approach is:

1. Take your current entitlement documents (proofs of entitlement, renewal quotes).
2. List every SKU line against the capability it covers.
3. For each, ask IBM in writing whether the capability is now included, still separately charged, or priced differently under AppPoints.
4. Record the answer, with the date and the person who gave it.

The same exercise applies to user tiers. Our licensing post describes Base, Premium and Authorized patterns, and the general principle, that not every user needs the most expensive tier, is worth testing against your actual access patterns. Whether that saves money in your contract is an empirical question that an AppPoints usage review answers; it does not belong in a business case as an assumed saving.

There is also a more pointed use of this section for EAM 7.6.1 estates. Part 2 sets out IBM's stated position on dual support: perpetual trade-up customers lose the right to use EAM after 30 April 2027, and subscription customers keep their subscription rights but lose support. Those two outcomes have very different cost shapes. For a perpetual trade-up customer, the cost of staying after the date includes a **compliance** exposure, which is a different class of risk from a support gap. Make sure the case says which of the two you are.

> 💡 **Key insight:** The licence review costs almost nothing and can change the denominator. Do it in Phase 0, before the numbers go to finance, not after.

## 🎲 The Risk Register

A business case without a risk register is a promise. A register turns the promise into a set of owned, dated conditions. Here is a register we would put in front of a steering committee for an estate that includes MAS 9.0 and some residual EAM 7.6.1. Each row has a trigger (how you will know), an owner and a date. Adjust the likelihood and impact to your own estate, and remove rows that do not apply.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Trigger | Mitigation | Owner | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Dual-support date missed for 7.6.1 estate** (30 Apr 2027) | Medium | High (compliance for perpetual trade-up) | No approved cutover plan by 31 Jan 2027 | Treat as hard date; resource ahead of other work | Programme sponsor | 31 Jan 2027 |
| R2 | **9.0 estate still on 9.0 at 30 Jun 2027** | Medium | Medium to High | First rehearsal not complete by mid-Feb 2027 | Back-plan from date; hold schedule buffer | Platform lead | 15 Feb 2027 |
| R3 | **Extended Support premium higher than modelled** | Medium | Medium | Written quote exceeds the assumption | Obtain quote in Phase 0; re-run model | Procurement | 30 Nov 2026 |
| R4 | **Extended Support coverage narrower than assumed** | Medium | High | Contract text excludes security fixes or has conditions | Read terms; compare to partner summaries; escalate to IBM | Procurement + CISO | 30 Nov 2026 |
| R5 | **Unpatched vulnerability on an out-of-support release** | Medium | High | New IBM bulletin lists your release as unassessed or workaround-only | Compensating controls; shorten exit date | CISO | Ongoing |
| R6 | **First rehearsal fails on production-clone data** | Medium | Medium | Rehearsal overruns or aborts | Fund a second rehearsal; read readmes for exact patch levels | Platform + DBA | Per rehearsal |
| R7 | **Add-on or industry solution has no target-version release** | Medium | High | Vendor written roadmap shows gap | Ask in Phase 0; choose target on parity | Platform lead | 30 Nov 2026 |
| R8 | **Removed API (9.2 user management) breaks integrations** | Medium | Medium | Caller inventory finds dependent integrations | Inventory callers; schedule rewrite before the hop | Integration lead | 31 Dec 2026 |
| R9 | **OpenShift or Db2 prerequisite slips** | Medium | High | Cluster upgrade not complete before the MAS hop | Put platform dates in register; schedule first | Infrastructure | 31 Jan 2027 |
| R10 | **Db2 12 licence decision made by accident** | Low to Medium | Medium | Db2 upgrade proceeds without licence file | Make decision explicit and separate | DBA + Procurement | 31 Dec 2026 |
| R11 | **Outage window not approved** | Medium | High | No business sign-off 8 weeks before the date | Early engagement with operations; plan read-only options | Operations | 30 Mar 2027 |
| R12 | **Key people unavailable** (rehearsals, regression) | Medium | Medium | Team commitments overlap with plant outages | Name backups; fund contractor cover | Programme manager | 31 Dec 2026 |
| R13 | **Scope creep from "while we are in there" upgrades** | High | Medium | Change requests that add features to the upgrade | Separate upgrade from enhancement; park backlog | Sponsor | Weekly |
| R14 | **Partner or IBM dates change** | Low | Medium | IBM lifecycle page changes | Re-verify dates monthly; log changes | Programme manager | Monthly |

Three practical notes on using this register.

**Likelihood and impact are yours.** We have put plausible labels in the columns so the structure is clear. You know your estate. If you have a single plant on 9.0 and a clean customisation inventory, R6 may be Low. If you have 200 automation scripts and a three-person team, it may be High.

**Triggers are the valuable column.** A risk with no trigger is a worry. A risk with a trigger (a date, a document, a test result) is an early warning, and it is what lets a steering committee make a decision before the cost lands.

**Rows can be closed.** The register's reward is seeing rows go green. R3 and R4 close when the written quote and contract text are in the file. R7 closes when every add-on vendor has answered in writing. Closing risks is progress that executives can see, and it turns the programme from a cost into a reduction in exposure.

### Quantifying the register without false precision

If your finance team wants numbers, resist the temptation to multiply a guessed probability by a guessed impact and report the product to three digits. Use bands.

| Band | Likelihood | Impact (illustrative, define your own) |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Under 10% | Under one week of delay, small cost |
| Medium | 10 to 40% | One to four weeks of delay, material cost |
| High | Over 40% | More than four weeks, or a compliance or safety consequence |

A register using bands is honest about how much you know. The numbers will be revised as rehearsals complete, and the revisions are themselves evidence that the programme is learning.

## 🗺️ The Phased Plan

The plan has five phases, backed up from the two hard dates. The phases are deliberately small at the front. Phase 0 is cheap, fast and makes every later number better, which is exactly why it should be funded first.

| Phase | Window | What happens | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0: Facts** | 17 Oct to 31 Dec 2026 | Inventory every instance, version and add-on; collect written quotes and contract text; review licence position; decide target; build the register | Approved target, written quotes in file, funded plan |
| **1: Foundations** | Nov 2026 to Feb 2027 | Prerequisite platform work (OpenShift, Db2, storage, identity); regression scope frozen; contractor and vendor slots booked | Prerequisites complete or dated; test plan signed |
| **2: Rehearse** | Jan to Mar 2027 | First rehearsal on a production clone per hop; fix; second rehearsal; business regression | Second rehearsal passes within the planned window |
| **3: Cut over** | Mar to Jun 2027 | Approved outage window; production hops; hypercare | Production on target before the relevant date; evidence filed |
| **4: Close out** | Jul to Sep 2027 | Retire old environments; remove temporary overrides; update register; lessons learned | Old estates decommissioned; register closed or reassigned |

Overlay the two hard dates:

| Date | Days from 17 Oct 2026 | What must be true by then |
|---|---|---|
| 31 Dec 2026 | 75 | Phase 0 complete: target chosen, quotes in hand, plan funded |
| 30 Apr 2027 | **195** | Every EAM 7.6.1 estate cut over to Manage, or a written position from IBM on your licence path |
| 30 Jun 2027 | **256** | Every MAS 9.0 estate on 9.1 or 9.2, or an explicit, priced decision to take Extended Support |

The last row is the one that makes the business case honest. **Taking Extended Support is a legitimate outcome, as long as it is a decision.** Some estates have good reasons: a vendor add-on that will not certify on 9.2 until later, a plant shutdown calendar that makes the spring window impossible, a platform prerequisite that cannot be met in time. In those cases the plan should say so, price the premium and name the new exit date. What is not acceptable is arriving at 30 June 2027 on 9.0 because nobody put the date in the plan.

### Three options, priced side by side

Every steering committee should see the same three options in the same table. Here is the template.

| | **A: Upgrade before the date** | **B: Pay for Extended Support, upgrade later** | **C: Do nothing** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it means** | Land on 9.1 or 9.2 before 30 Jun 2027 | Stay on 9.0 on Extended Support; schedule an exit | Stay and do not plan an exit |
| **Support position** | Full support | Reduced, paid support | Reduced support, no end date for staying |
| **One-off cost** | Hop effort, rehearsals, outage, prerequisites | Planning only (smaller) | Nil |
| **Run cost** | Normal | Extended Support premium (written quote) | Premium, plus rising ecosystem and exposure costs |
| **Risk position** | Falls as rehearsals pass | Held flat, rising with ecosystem drift | Rises |
| **Bucket 4 effect** | Stops compounding | Defers it; may add a hop | Compounds |
| **Best when** | You can be ready and the add-ons are certified | Dependencies genuinely are not ready, and the exit is dated | Almost never |

The value of the table is not that it picks A. It is that B has to be defended *with a dated exit and a number*, and C has to be defended at all. A committee that sees all three priced together is making a decision rather than reacting to one large number.

## 📄 The Executive One-Pager

This is the artefact that goes in the pre-read. Keep it to one page. It is a template, with every placeholder in square brackets. Replace the placeholders with your own numbers, and remove any line you cannot back up.

```text
UPGRADE DECISION: [Organisation], [Maximo / MAS estate]            Date: [date]

THE ASK
Approve [£/$ range] and [N] person-days to move [estate] from [version]
to [target version] by [date], ahead of IBM's published support dates.

WHY NOW (all dates from IBM pages, re-checked [date])
• 30 Apr 2027  EAM 7.6.1 dual support ends                [195 days]
• 30 Jun 2027  MAS 9.0.x moves to Extended Support        [256 days]  (AD26-0622)
• MAS 8.7-8.9  already out of support since 30 Apr 2026
• MAS 9.1 / 9.2  no transition date published yet

WHAT STAYING COSTS (per year)
1. Support premium    [quote / TBD - owner, date]
2. Unpatched exposure [range - CISO estimate]
3. Ecosystem drag     [range - vendor letters on file / TBD]
4. Delay growth       [added effort if we slip 12 months]

WHAT MOVING COSTS
Hops: [n] (IBM n-1 policy)   Rehearsals: [n]   Outage: [hours]
Effort: [low] / [expected] / [high] person-days
Prerequisites: [OpenShift / Db2 / identity - dated]

OPTIONS
A Upgrade before the date    B Extended Support + dated exit    C Do nothing
[one line each: cost, risk, recommendation]

TOP 3 RISKS (owner, trigger, date)
[from register]

WHAT WE NEED FROM THE COMMITTEE
1. Fund Phase 0 now ([amount]) - facts by 31 Dec 2026.
```

The most important line is the last section of the "ask." **Fund Phase 0 now.** It is the smallest request in the document and the one with the highest return. Phase 0 buys the quotes, the inventory and the target decision. With them, every other number is firmer. Without them, the committee is asked to fund an unknown.

## ❓ The Questions to Put to IBM in Writing

A business case is only as good as its weakest assumption, and the weakest assumptions are the ones only IBM or your partner can settle. Put these in an email, name a person at IBM, and attach the replies to the register.

| # | Question | Why it matters | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the Extended Support price and term for [versions] under our entitlement? | IBM publishes no price | Bucket 1 |
| 2 | What exactly does Extended Support include and exclude for security fixes? | Partner summaries are not contract language | R4, bucket 2 |
| 3 | Does the end date on our contract match IBM's lifecycle page? | Dates differ between sources | Dates table |
| 4 | What is the supported upgrade route and patch level for each hop from [current] to [target]? | Hop warnings exist in readmes | Cost of moving |
| 5 | For EAM 7.6.1: what are the dual-support terms for our licence path after 30 Apr 2027? | Perpetual and subscription differ | R1 |
| 6 | Will our AppPoints position change if we retire instances or consolidate? | Licence line | Licence review |
| 7 | Is a transition date planned for 9.1 and 9.2, and when will it be announced? | None is published | Target choice |
| 8 | Which add-ons and industry solutions in our estate have no 9.2 equivalent? | Blocks the hop | R7 |

If IBM or the partner cannot or will not answer, that is itself information. Record the non-answer, the date and the person, and carry it as an open assumption with an owner.

## ⚠️ Mistakes That Sink a Business Case

| Mistake | Why it bites | Better |
|---|---|---|
| **A single number** | Cannot survive discovery | Low, expected and high, with named assumptions |
| **Costing one hop when n-1 gives two** | Effort is short by design | Cost each hop with its own rehearsals |
| **Using partner percentages as IBM prices** | IBM publishes none; sources conflict | Written quote or a labelled assumption |
| **Inventing a 9.1 or 9.2 date** | IBM has not published one | "Not yet published", with a review date |
| **Leading with AI features** | Answers the wrong question | Lead with deadline and cost of staying; show features as bounded upside |
| **Hiding the licence review** | The one line that can reduce cost | Do it in Phase 0 |
| **Ignoring the outage cost** | Operations will raise it later | Price lost throughput up front |
| **No owner on assumptions** | Open items rot | Every assumption has an owner and a date |
| **Treating Extended Support as a plan** | It is a bridge | Date the exit and price the premium |
| **Combining upgrade and enhancement** | Scope creep eats the buffer | Separate the programmes |

## 🔟 The Ten Rules of the Business Case

1. **Anchor every date to an IBM page.** If it is partner-sourced, say so.
2. **Never invent a date IBM has not published.** 9.1 and 9.2 have none.
3. **Price four buckets of staying, not one.** Premium, exposure, drag and compounding delay.
4. **Cost the move in hops.** Rehearsals, outage and regression per hop.
5. **Show ranges with named assumptions.** One number is a promise you cannot keep.
6. **Put an owner and a date on every assumption.** Open items need names.
7. **Present three options side by side.** Upgrade, Extended Support with a dated exit, and do nothing.
8. **Fund Phase 0 first.** Facts before projects.
9. **Do the licence review early.** It can change the denominator.
10. **Put every answer from IBM in writing and file it.** Evidence outlives memory.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I put a dollar figure on staying on an old Maximo release?**
Split the cost of staying into four buckets and price each separately: the support premium you pay for Extended or Sustained Support, the expected loss from unpatched security and compliance exposure, the productivity drag of an ecosystem that no longer certifies against your version, and the compounding cost of a bigger, later upgrade. IBM does not publish an Extended Support price, so the first bucket must come from a written quote. The second is an estimate your security and risk teams own, labelled as an estimate.

**What is the single biggest mistake in an upgrade business case?**
Costing the upgrade as one event when IBM's n-1 policy makes it a ladder. A 9.0 estate heading for 9.2 owes two hops, and each carries its own rehearsal, outage window and regression cycle. A case that prices one hop will be far short on effort before the first environment is touched. The second most common mistake is presenting one number instead of a range with named assumptions.

**Should we present the business case as risk avoidance or as value creation?**
Lead with risk and deadline, because that is what the dates support, then show value as a bounded upside. The verified facts are dates: 30 April 2027 for EAM 7.6.1 dual support and 30 June 2027 for MAS 9.0 moving to Extended Support. Value claims depend on your adoption, and executives discount inflated upgrade ROI. Structure it as what must be done and by when, the cheapest compliant route, and what the new platform allows afterwards.

**How much contingency should the plan carry?**
Hold contingency against specific risks in the register, not as a flat percentage, and keep a separate schedule buffer in front of each hard IBM date. A flat percentage hides which risk it protects against and is usually spent in the first quarter. Typical candidates are a failed first rehearsal, an extra outage window and a prerequisite slip such as an OpenShift or database upgrade. Tie each to a trigger and an owner, and release it when the matching rehearsal passes.

**What do we ask IBM or our business partner for before the case goes to finance?**
Ask for five things in writing: the Extended Support entitlement and price for your exact versions, confirmation that your contract end date matches IBM's lifecycle page, your AppPoints position, the supported route and patch levels for each hop, and the dual-support terms for any EAM 7.6.1 estate. Verbal assurances change; a written answer is evidence for the audit file. If a number cannot be obtained, record it as an open assumption with an owner and a due date.
