# The 30 April 2027 Dual-Support Deadline: Perpetual vs Subscription Rights

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** Executives, procurement, legal, software-asset managers and Maximo owners who run EAM 7.6.1.x and hold (or are buying) a MAS entitlement. If you cannot say from memory whether your MAS entitlement is a perpetual trade-up or a subscription, this post is for you.

*Part 2 of 4 in the MAS Lifecycle series. [Start with the series index](/blog/mas-lifecycle-series-index) for every date on one page, and read [Part 1](/blog/mas-lifecycle-761-after-extended-support) for the support side of 7.6.1.*

## 🔥 The Deadline That Isn't an Outage

Most end-of-support dates are boring in the best way. Support ends, the software keeps running, and the pain arrives later in the form of an unpatched vulnerability. The 30 April 2027 date is different, and for one group of customers it is not about support at all.

IBM's dual-support notice is titled *IBM Maximo Application Suite with IBM Maximo Asset Management Dual Support ending 2027*. We read the live page on 17 October 2026; it was last modified on 8 July 2026. It states that effective 30 April 2027, dual support under the MAS license for EAM 7.6.1.x is no longer available. Then it splits customers into two groups:

- **Perpetual license trade-up to MAS:** you must finish your migration by the end date. IBM's wording: after this date, EAM usage rights expire and continued use would be out of compliance.
- **Subscription upgrade to MAS:** you remain in compliance with your subscription terms, but EAM 7.6.1.x will no longer be supported.

Read those two sentences again, slowly. In one group, on 1 May 2027 the system you run production on is, per IBM's published position, no longer licensed to run. In the other, it is licensed but orphaned.

Nothing will break on the day. Work orders will still be created. Preventive maintenance will still generate. That is the trap. As we said in Part 1, an unsupported platform looks identical to a supported one right up to the day it matters. A platform whose licence basis has lapsed looks identical too, until an auditor, an acquirer, or IBM's licensing team asks.

> 💡 **Key insight:** Part 1 was about what you are *covered for*. Part 2 is about what you are *allowed to run*. They have different owners (IT and risk vs procurement and legal), different dates and different consequences, and the second one is the one most organisations have not put on a calendar.

From 17 October 2026 to 30 April 2027 is **195 days**. We will use that number a lot.

## 🧭 What Dual Support Actually Is

Dual support is a transitional licensing arrangement. IBM's page describes it in plain terms: Maximo Asset Management (EAM) clients can trade up their current Asset Management licence to an IBM Maximo Application Suite licence. After the trade-up, they may run EAM and MAS in any deployment combination, up to the total number of MAS entitlements purchased, for as long as dual support for EAM 7.6.1.x is offered. And, critically, the earlier version of the same notice (last modified 12 February 2024) says: once the client has completed their technical migration to MAS Manage, they are required to discontinue their use of EAM.

That is the whole mechanism. Three elements:

| Element | What IBM says | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Run both** | EAM and MAS may be used "in any deployment combination" up to the MAS entitlements purchased | You can split users and environments across old and new while you migrate |
| **Cap** | The ceiling is the number of MAS entitlements you bought, not the number of EAM licences you had | An undersized MAS entitlement limits how much you can run, on either side |
| **Stop** | Once the technical migration to MAS Manage is complete, discontinue EAM | Dual support was never a licence to keep EAM forever alongside MAS |
| **Sunset** | Dual support for 7.6.1.x ends 30 April 2027 | After this, the "run both" permission is gone |

Two consequences are worth pausing on.

**First**, the "stop" obligation is not new. It was in the February 2024 version of the page. The 30 April 2027 date was also already there, as one of two dates the 2024 page lists alongside the 30 September 2025 end of base support. What the July 2026 revision added, in our reading, is the explicit split between perpetual and subscription customers and the sentence about compliance. The notice has become more specific as the date approached, which is normal, and it is also a signal that IBM expects the question to be asked.

**Second**, "up to the number of MAS entitlements purchased" means the trade-up entitlement is also the capacity you are allowed to move to. If your MAS entitlement was sized for a partial migration, you may not be entitled to run your whole workload on Manage. We come back to that in the checklist.

## 🧩 Perpetual vs Subscription: The Two Groups

The reason the notice splits into two groups is the legal nature of what you hold.

A **perpetual licence** is bought once, with support paid annually through Subscription and Support. You own the right to use that version indefinitely. When IBM offered a trade-up path, a perpetual EAM customer exchanged that perpetual right for a MAS entitlement, and dual support was the bridge during which the old right and the new one overlapped. The key phrase in IBM's notice is that on 30 April 2027 the old right *expires*, so the bridge ends and only the new entitlement remains.

A **subscription** (term) licence is rented for a period. A customer who came to MAS on a subscription path never held a perpetual right that needs to expire; their entitlement is governed by their subscription terms. That is why the notice says they stay in compliance. What they lose is IBM support for the old product.

| Dimension | Perpetual trade-up | Subscription upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| **What you held before** | Perpetual EAM licence plus Subscription & Support | Typically a term-based entitlement or an upgrade into term-based MAS |
| **After 30 Apr 2027: licence** | EAM usage rights expire; continued use out of compliance (IBM) | Remains compliant with subscription terms (IBM) |
| **After 30 Apr 2027: support** | Not applicable as a usable position, because use is out of compliance | EAM 7.6.1.x no longer supported (IBM) |
| **Nature of the risk** | Audit, legal, financial: true-up, back-licensing, contractual dispute | Operational: no IBM help for defects, security, integration failures |
| **Owner of the problem** | Procurement, legal, SAM, CFO | IT, security, operations |
| **Can Sustained Support help?** | Not shown to restore usage rights; confirm with IBM in writing | May retain some legacy help, subject to eligibility; does not change the date |
| **Worst case** | Unlicensed production system discovered in an audit | An outage or breach on a system nobody will support |

A word of caution about the labels. These categories describe IBM's public summary. Your own agreement may contain terms that differ: a negotiated extension, a mixed deal, a bespoke addendum. Treat the table as a map for asking the right questions, not as legal advice or a substitute for your contract.

> ⚠️ **Honesty note on evidence:** The licensing consequences above are quoted from IBM's own page, so they are as authoritative as public sources get. The partner commentary about what an auditor will actually do on 1 May 2027 is not. We found no public IBM statement describing an enforcement mechanism or an audit schedule tied to this date. Planning on the assumption that nobody will check is a bet, not a position.

## 🔍 Step 1: Find Out Which Group You Are In

Most organisations cannot answer this from memory. The deal was struck years ago, maybe by a predecessor, maybe through a business partner, and the paperwork lives in three systems. Here is how to reconstruct it.

### Where the evidence lives

| Source | What it tells you | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|
| **IBM Passport Advantage** entitlement records | Part numbers, licence type (perpetual trade-up vs term), quantities, dates, site | Procurement / SAM |
| **Original MAS order or quote** | Whether the line was a trade-up from an existing licence or a new term purchase | Procurement |
| **Trade-up or upgrade letter / addendum** | The specific legacy entitlements exchanged and any special conditions | Legal / procurement |
| **IBM Subscription & Support renewal invoices** | Whether S&S is still being paid on the legacy EAM and on which parts | Finance |
| **Business partner contract** | Services, but also sometimes licence reseller terms | Procurement |
| **Internal licence register / SAM tool** | What you believe you own; compare to the above | SAM |

### The reconciliation questions

Work through these in order and write the answers down.

1. For each MAS part number you hold, is it a **trade-up** from a perpetual licence or a **new or upgraded subscription**?
2. Which **legacy EAM licences** (Authorized User, Concurrent, Limited Use, industry add-ons) were traded, and which, if any, were *not* traded and remain perpetual in their own right?
3. Which **business units, legal entities or sites** are covered by each entitlement? Mixed estates are common after mergers.
4. What is your **MAS entitlement quantity** (for example AppPoints) and does it cover the full workload you intend to run on Manage after migration?
5. Is the legacy **Subscription & Support** still current, and on which parts?
6. Are there any **IBM Flexible Contract Terms (FCT)** agreements? IBM's EOS page says FCT customers must move to Passport Advantage because of end of support, so check that move was completed.

> 💡 **Key insight:** The "mixed estate" is the case that surprises people. A group that acquired a company, or that bought MAS in tranches, can be perpetual trade-up in one business unit and subscription in another. The deadline then means different things to different parts of the same organisation, and a single "we're fine" statement from IT is wrong for at least one of them.

### A starting-point inventory worksheet

Capture one row per entitlement. This is a worksheet design, not an IBM format; adapt the columns to your SAM tool.

```text
entitlement_id | PA part number | licence_type (trade-up|term) | legacy EAM licences traded
quantity (AppPoints etc.) | covered entities/sites | S&S current (Y/N, to date)
dual-support end date quoted by IBM | evidence file (contract, PA export, letter)
owner | last verified (date) | verified by (name)
```

The last two columns matter. A licence position nobody has verified since 2023 is a hypothesis.

## 📋 Step 2: The Contract and Entitlement Checklist

Once you know your group, test the details. This checklist is what we would walk through in a steering meeting with procurement and legal in the room. Items marked ◆ are the ones most often missed.

### Licence basis

- ☐ Group confirmed (perpetual trade-up, subscription, or mixed) for **every** entitlement and business unit
- ☐ ◆ The **exact wording** of the dual-support clause in *your* agreement or order, not only the public notice
- ☐ Dual-support end date stated in your paperwork matches 30 April 2027 (or documents a different negotiated date)
- ☐ Any **special terms or amendments** (extended dual-use period, carve-outs, regional variations) identified and filed
- ☐ ◆ Whether any legacy EAM licences were **not** traded and so remain perpetual on their own terms

### Scope after the deadline

- ☐ Position on **non-production** use (test, training, development, sandbox) of EAM after the date
- ☐ ◆ Position on **disaster recovery and standby** EAM copies
- ☐ Position on a **read-only or archive** EAM instance for historical data access, if you want one
- ☐ Whether third-party systems that call EAM APIs or integration endpoints are covered by the same rule

### Capacity

- ☐ ◆ **MAS entitlement quantity** is sufficient to run the *whole* workload on Manage once EAM is off
- ☐ User-type mapping (Authorized vs Concurrent vs Limited, and administrative users) understood under AppPoints
- ☐ Industry solutions and add-ons you use on EAM have entitlement equivalents in MAS
- ☐ Any extra AppPoints needed for add-ons (mobile, AI, scheduling) budgeted before cutover

### Support and risk

- ☐ Position on **Sustained Support**: whether you hold it, whether you are eligible (see Part 1), and what it does and does not cover
- ☐ Internal **risk register** entry with named owner for the licence date and a separate entry for the support date
- ☐ Insurance and regulator implications reviewed for running a platform that is unsupported or unlicensed
- ☐ Board or audit committee informed of the exposure in writing

### Process

- ☐ ◆ **Written confirmation from IBM** (see the template below) filed in your audit folder
- ☐ Go/no-go date set with at least a quarter of contingency before 30 April 2027
- ☐ Exceptions (rollback environments, pilot users still on EAM) logged and time-boxed

A note on capacity, because it is the most expensive miss. The public IBM text caps dual running at the MAS entitlements you purchased. Our knowledge base notes that the shape of consumption under AppPoints differs from named-user licensing, that organisations with many light or occasional users can see costs rise, and that one third-party provider flags a possible 35% increase on like-for-like migrations. That figure is a vendor claim, not an IBM number, and your result depends on your user mix. Build your own AppPoint model with IBM's licence documentation before accepting any cost narrative, in either direction.

## ✉️ Step 3: Getting It Confirmed by IBM, in Writing

Public pages describe policy. Your contract describes your rights. The gap between them is closed by a written statement from IBM that names *you*. Ask for it now, because replies take time and your cutover plan depends on them.

IBM's own pages point you to your IBM sales representative for entitlement migration and to Expert Labs for migration planning help. Use the representative as your first channel, copy your business partner, and escalate through your account team if you get a verbal answer only.

Here is wording you can adapt. Replace the bracketed fields and have legal review it before sending.

```text
Subject: Written confirmation of dual-support terms – [Company], customer no. [###]

Re: IBM Maximo Application Suite entitlements [part numbers / PA order refs]

We are running IBM Maximo Asset Management 7.6.1.x under the dual-support
arrangement described at https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/node/7274656
(last modified 8 July 2026). Please confirm in writing, for each entitlement
and legal entity listed in the attached schedule:

1. Whether each MAS entitlement is a perpetual license trade-up or a
   subscription upgrade.
2. The date on which dual support ends for us, and the source of that date
   in our agreement.
3. Whether, after that date, any use of EAM 7.6.1.x is permitted for
   (a) production, (b) non-production, (c) disaster recovery/standby,
   (d) read-only access to historical data. Please state yes/no for each.
4. What is IBM's position if our technical migration to MAS Manage is not
   complete by that date, and who is our contact to discuss it.
5. Whether the MAS entitlement quantity we hold is sufficient, in IBM's
   view, to run our current EAM workload on MAS Manage, and what the
   conversion basis is.
6. Whether purchasing Sustained Support for EAM 7.6.1.x changes any of the
   above.

Please reply by [date, at least 4 weeks out] so we can complete our
cutover plan.
```

> ⚠️ **What a good answer looks like:** It names your entitlements, states a date, answers each numbered point separately, and comes from IBM or an authorised representative in writing. "Don't worry, we'll sort it out" is not an answer. Neither is a link back to the public notice.

If IBM's reply conflicts with the public page or with your contract, the conflict is itself valuable. Put it in front of legal immediately rather than reconciling it informally.

## ⏱️ Step 4: Back-Plan From 30 April 2027

If you are a perpetual trade-up customer with production still on EAM, the schedule is not "finish by April". It is "finish *before* April, with the cutover complete and stable". Here is a back-plan from the deadline, built on the conservative assumption that the technical cutover must be finished and EAM switched off by 30 April 2027.

| Weeks before 30 Apr 2027 | Approx. date | Milestone | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28 | mid-Oct 2026 | Licensing path confirmed internally; request to IBM sent | Replies take weeks; capacity and scope answers shape the plan |
| 24 | mid-Nov 2026 | IBM written reply received; entitlement gap (if any) quantified | Time to negotiate extra AppPoints before budget year-end |
| 20 | mid-Dec 2026 | Go/no-go on the migration path; programme funded | Before year-end freezes; also where Part 4's business case lands |
| 16 | mid-Jan 2027 | Data migration approach proven in a rehearsal environment | Customisations, scripts and integrations are the usual long pole |
| 10 | early-Mar 2027 | User acceptance testing complete; training under way | A cutover fails on people at least as often as on data |
| 6 | late-Mar 2027 | Final rehearsal and rollback test; freeze window agreed | Leaves one failed attempt's worth of contingency |
| 4 | early Apr 2027 | Production cutover | Four weeks of hypercare before the date |
| 1 | late Apr 2027 | EAM shut down and licences retired; evidence filed | Proves the "stop using EAM" obligation was met |
| 0 | 30 Apr 2027 | Deadline | |

This is deliberately aggressive, and it is the shape you must achieve if you cannot get an extension. If your estate is large, regulated, or has heavy customisation, the honest answer may be that 195 days is not enough. In that case the most valuable thing you can do is learn that now, from your own rehearsal results, and take it to IBM as an informed request. The worst version of this conversation is asking for flexibility in April.

For the migration itself, our [MAS migration playbook](/blog/mas-migration-playbook) covers the phases, and the [MAS 9.2 upgrade watch-list](/blog/mas-92-upgrade-watchlist) covers what changes if you land on 9.2.

### Worked example: a mixed estate

Consider a mid-size utility with three business units. We have composed this example from patterns we see; it is not a named customer.

| Business unit | Legacy estate | MAS entitlement | Group | Position on 1 May 2027 if nothing changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Generation | EAM 7.6.1.3, 400 users | Perpetual trade-up, 2022 | Perpetual | EAM usage rights expired, out of compliance |
| Distribution | EAM 7.6.1.3, 650 users | Term subscription, 2023 | Subscription | Compliant, unsupported |
| Telecom (acquired 2024) | EAM 7.6.1.1 | Not yet entitled | Neither | No dual-support right; also below the 7.6.1.3 gate from Part 1 |

The IT team's summary is "we're on the dual-support plan, deadline April 2027". It is true for none of the three rows in the same way. Generation needs a licence-driven cutover. Distribution needs a risk-accepted or accelerated plan. Telecom needs a conversation about entitlement before anything else. One steering-group slide that says "April 2027" hides all three.

## 🚫 Where Plans Go Wrong: Edge Cases and Traps

These are the cases that cause surprises. For each, the safe move is to ask IBM, not to assume.

| Trap | Why it bites | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **"Cutover done" but EAM still running somewhere** | The obligation is to discontinue EAM use once migration is complete. A forgotten test or reporting instance is still use | Inventory every EAM instance, including DR and vendor-hosted ones, and retire each with evidence |
| **Read-only EAM kept "just for history"** | Not clear from the public notice whether this is allowed after the date | Include it in the written request; if IBM says no, migrate history to Manage or an archive |
| **Entitlement sized for pilot, not production** | Dual running is capped by MAS entitlements bought; full migration needs full capacity | Model AppPoints for the whole user base before cutover, not after |
| **Integrations still pointing at EAM** | Other teams' systems call EAM endpoints and keep it "in use" | Catalogue integration framework endpoints and retarget them as a workstream |
| **Reports and BI on 7.6.1 tables** | Direct database reports keep a reason to run EAM | Rebuild on Manage or an analytics layer; do not leave a "temporary" EAM database |
| **Contractor or vendor-hosted instance** | A third party runs EAM on your behalf and you forget it | Add it to the register and give the vendor the same date |
| **Sustained Support treated as a licence fix** | It is a support product; the date is a licence term | Read Part 1; get written confirmation of any interaction |
| **Multiple legal entities, one contract** | The entitlement may cover entities unevenly after acquisitions or divestments | Map each entity to an entitlement before the date |
| **Unknown pre-7.6.1 instance** | An old 7.5 or 7.6.0 box nobody maintains | We found no IBM extended option for pre-7.6.1; confirm with IBM |
| **Assuming IBM will "grandfather" you** | No public statement says so | Treat any extension as nonexistent until it is in writing |

### What if you cannot make the date?

Be honest with yourself early. If a perpetual trade-up estate cannot complete by 30 April 2027, there are only a few levers, and none are within your control alone:

1. **Negotiate with IBM.** Ask for a written short extension of the dual-use right, with a defined end date and any fee. Come with a credible plan: a rehearsal result, a funded programme, a date. Our reading of IBM's Sustained Support eligibility conditions (Part 1) is that a documented roadmap is what IBM wants to see, so the same artefact helps both conversations. Whether IBM would grant such an extension is unknown; we found no public sign of one.
2. **Reduce the scope that must move.** Retire modules and sites you do not need, so that less has to cut over. A smaller programme finishes sooner.
3. **Accept and document the exposure.** If neither of the above is available, the decision to continue past the date belongs to a named executive with legal advice, in writing, with the cost of true-up or dispute estimated. It should never be a default that nobody decided.

What you should not do is rely on a third party's assurance that "IBM never enforces this". The only authoritative answer is IBM's, about your contract.

## 💬 What We Hear in Steering Meetings

> *"We bought MAS in 2022, so we're covered."* — Covered for what? Dual support is the thing that expires.

> *"It's a support date, we've done Sustained Support."* — Only true for the subscription group, and Sustained Support does not change the date even for them.

> *"IBM will extend it."* — Maybe. It is not in the public text. Ask in writing, with a plan attached.

> *"We'll cut over in April."* — With a deadline of 30 April, April is the month you are *out*, not the month you start.

> *"Legal says the contract is fine."* — Which clause? Ask to see the dual-support wording and the entitlement quantity, not a summary.

> *"Procurement handles the licences."* — Then procurement needs to be in the programme board, because the migration schedule is now a licensing control.

## 🔟 The Ten Rules of the Dual-Support Deadline

1. **Know your group.** Perpetual trade-up, subscription, or mixed. Per entitlement, per entity.
2. **Use IBM's wording, not a summary.** Quote the page, then read your own contract.
3. **Treat it as a licence date first and a support date second.**
4. **Bring legal and procurement to the programme board.**
5. **Size the entitlement for the whole workload.** Dual running is capped by what you bought.
6. **Find every EAM instance.** Test, DR, hosted and forgotten ones all count.
7. **Get answers in writing.** Verbal reassurance has no audit value.
8. **Don't confuse Sustained Support with licence rights.** Different contracts, different dates.
9. **Plan the cutover to finish a month early.** Hypercare and rollback live in that month.
10. **Record the evidence.** When EAM is retired, file proof. Future-you will be asked.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**What exactly happens on 30 April 2027 if I traded up a perpetual license to MAS?**
IBM's dual-support notice, last modified 8 July 2026, says perpetual trade-up customers must finish migrating to MAS by 30 April 2027 and that after that date EAM usage rights expire and continued use would be out of compliance. The system will not switch itself off, which is why it is dangerous: production keeps running while the licence basis is gone. That turns a technical project into an audit and legal exposure, so procurement, legal and software-asset management must be in the planning.

**I'm on a subscription path. Am I safe after 30 April 2027?**
Safe on licence, exposed on support. IBM says subscription-upgrade customers remain in compliance with their subscription terms, but EAM 7.6.1.x is no longer supported after the date. That is no IBM help for a platform whose base support ended on 30 September 2025 and whose Extended Support window closed on 30 September 2026. It can be a legitimate position, but make it a documented decision, and confirm the wording in your own contract.

**How do I find out whether I'm perpetual trade-up or subscription?**
Start with the original MAS order in Passport Advantage: the part numbers and licence type on the entitlement record tell you. Cross-check against the signed contract or trade-up letter. If records disagree, or you hold both across business units, ask your IBM representative for a written statement of your licensing path and dual-support end date. Do not rely on memory.

**Can I buy Sustained Support to get past 30 April 2027?**
Not for the licensing clause. Sustained Support is a support purchase on the legacy product; the dual-support end is a licensing term under your MAS entitlement. For a subscription customer it may retain some help, subject to eligibility. For a perpetual trade-up customer, nothing in IBM's public notice suggests that buying support restores usage rights. Get any combination confirmed by IBM in writing.

**What should I put in the request to IBM?**
Ask for five things in writing: your licensing path per entitlement and business unit; the dual-support end date that applies to you; whether EAM may be used for production, test, DR or read-only archive after that date; what happens if migration slips and who you negotiate with; and whether your MAS entitlement is large enough to run the full workload on Manage. File the reply with your audit records.
