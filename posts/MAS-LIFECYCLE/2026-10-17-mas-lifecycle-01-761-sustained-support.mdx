# Maximo 7.6.1 After Extended Support: What Sustained Support Does and Doesn't Cover

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** Executives, risk and compliance owners, and asset-management leaders who run Maximo 7.6.1 and need a straight answer to one question: "what are we actually covered for now, and what are we exposed to?" Technical readers will find the hardening plan in the second half.

*Part 1 of 4 in the MAS Lifecycle series. [Start with the series index](/blog/mas-lifecycle-series-index) if you want every date on one page.*

## 🔥 The Day After the Window Closed

On 30 September 2026 the last optional year of IBM Extended Support for Maximo 7.6.1.x closed. Seventeen days later, nothing on your plant floor looks different. Work orders still get created. Preventive maintenance still generates. The reports still run. That is exactly why this deadline is dangerous: **an unsupported platform looks identical to a supported one right up until the day it matters.**

You have probably heard three versions of the story by now.

- The vendor version: *"You can buy Sustained Support through 2030, so you have four more years."*
- The IT version: *"It's an on-prem system behind the firewall, so it's fine."*
- The finance version: *"We'll absorb it in next year's budget."*

Each of those contains something true and something that will hurt you. This post separates them. We will go through what IBM actually states, what only partners state, what the "no new fixes" reality means for security, how auditors and insurers tend to see an unsupported platform, and what you can do in the next 90 days to keep the risk bounded while you plan the move to MAS.

> 💡 **Key insight:** Sustained Support is a bridge sold by the same vendor that is asking you to cross it. It exists to give a documented migration plan time to execute. If your plan is "stay on Sustained Support until 2030," you have misread what the product is for, and you may also have missed that a licensing date lands before that.

## 🔬 What We Can and Cannot Prove

Our brief for this series is to avoid stating a date or a coverage claim as fact until we have seen it on an IBM page. Here is how that played out for this post, because the labels matter later when you decide what to rely on.

| Claim | Where we saw it | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Regular base support for Maximo Asset Management 7.6.1.x ended 30 Sep 2025 | IBM end-of-support announcement page (last modified 4 Jun 2024) | **IBM-confirmed** |
| IBM announced the end of support on 12 Apr 2022 under announcement letter 922-024 | Same IBM page | **IBM-confirmed** |
| Customers may purchase one year of Extended Support and up to five years of Sustained Support | Same IBM page | **IBM-confirmed** |
| Eligibility: Subscription and Support current, 7.6.1.3 or later, unable to upgrade before 30 Sep 2025, preparing an upgrade roadmap | Same IBM page | **IBM-confirmed** |
| Extended Support ends 30 Sep 2026 | IBM Community post (dated 3 Jun 2024) and partner posts; date is "one year" from IBM's 30 Sep 2025 | **IBM-derived** |
| Sustained Support completes 30 Sep 2030 | IBM lifecycle entries for the 7.6.1 family and IBM-sourced summaries; confirm the PID page for your product | **IBM-derived** |
| Dual support under the MAS license for EAM 7.6.1.x ends 30 Apr 2027 | IBM support page, last modified 8 Jul 2026 | **IBM-confirmed** |
| Sustained Support = usage/how-to help plus existing fixes, no new security patches | Naviam (Maximo partner) | **Partner-sourced** |
| Expanded support is "typically defect-based"; may carry a surcharge | Origina (a third-party maintenance competitor) | **Partner-sourced, conflicted** |

Two things jump out. First, IBM's public page for 7.6.1 carries the *terms* but not an *itemised list of what Sustained Support includes*. The itemised description exists, but it comes from partners. Second, one of the partner sources sells independent maintenance for a living and has an obvious incentive to describe IBM's programme unflatteringly. We use it for questions to ask IBM, not as evidence.

> ⚠️ **Discrepancy worth knowing:** One search summary cited a 7.6.1 fix level later than .3 as the Extended Support requirement. IBM's own end-of-support page, which we read directly, says "7.6.1.3 or later." We use IBM's wording. If your entitlement letter names a different level, the letter wins.

## 🧱 The Four Stages of 7.6.1 Support, Plainly

Most confusion in the market comes from using "support" for four different things. Here is the ladder as IBM's terms describe it, with the partner-sourced detail marked.

| Stage | Dates | What it is | Status today (17 Oct 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base support** | Ended 30 Sep 2025 | Regular fixes, patches and technical support under Subscription and Support | Over |
| **Extended Support** | One year, to 30 Sep 2026 | Separately purchased continuation for eligible customers on the latest fix level | **Closed 17 days ago** |
| **Sustained Support** | Up to five years, availability to 30 Sep 2030 | Separately purchased; partner sources describe usage/how-to help and existing fixes only | Available only if you qualify and buy it |
| **No coverage** | Any time you lack the above | You run the software you have; nobody at IBM is obliged to help | Where an unprotected estate sits now |

The 2030 date is the one that gets quoted in budget meetings. One Maximo partner puts it more accurately than most: the date "is not an entitlement." It describes how long IBM will *offer* the option, subject to conditions, not a promise that your installation is covered through that day. Eligibility can be lost, for instance if your Subscription and Support lapses or your migration roadmap stops being credible.

## 🚪 The 7.6.1.3 Gate and the Roadmap Condition

IBM's page is specific about who may buy the extended options. In IBM's words, the customer must be current on IBM Subscription and Support, be on the latest version (7.6.1.3 or later), be unable to upgrade before 30 September 2025, and show that they are preparing an upgrade roadmap. Four conditions, and each one is a way to be ineligible without realising it.

| Condition | How a customer fails it | What to check this week |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscription and Support current** | A lapsed renewal, a business unit nobody renewed, a Flexible Contract Terms agreement not moved to Passport Advantage | Renewal status per entitlement; IBM states FCT customers must move to Passport Advantage to receive End of Support |
| **7.6.1.3 or later** | Production on 7.6.1.0, .1 or .2, or a mismatch between environments | Fix level per environment, including the forgotten ones |
| **Unable to upgrade before 30 Sep 2025** | A reviewer asks why the upgrade was not done earlier | Keep a documented reason on file; "other priorities" is a weak answer |
| **Upgrade roadmap in preparation** | No document exists, or it is two years old and stale | A dated roadmap with owner, scope and milestone dates |

The practical consequence of that last row is something we hear repeatedly from practitioners: IBM can ask you to prove it. A partner source reports customers being told IBM would not provide support without evidence of active plans. We cannot verify that claim, but it matches the logic of the condition. If you buy Sustained Support, you are signing up to produce a credible migration roadmap, and the roadmap needs to keep moving.

> 💡 **Key insight:** The eligibility conditions and the 30 April 2027 dual-support date are linked. A roadmap that ends after April 2027 may be a roadmap that ends after your EAM usage rights do. Part 2 covers that clause.

### If you are below 7.6.1.3

Your first problem is not buying Sustained Support. It is getting to a level where you can. IBM's page describes 7.6.1.3 as the latest version for this purpose and describes 7.6.1 as the last 7.6.x release. Moving up a fix level on a legacy estate is a real change: it needs a regression cycle, integration tests and a rollback plan. Treat it as a small project with a date, not a favour you ask your DBA to do over a weekend. If IBM confirms in writing that a given fix level does not qualify, record the answer and the date.

## 📦 What Sustained Support Does and Doesn't Cover

This is the section most executives skip to. We will be explicit about the evidence level.

### What IBM states

- Customers can buy one year of Extended Support and up to five years of Sustained Support.
- Regular base support, meaning fixes, patches and technical support under the normal agreement, is no longer provided for 7.6.1.x and its components and add-ons since 30 September 2025.
- The path forward IBM names is the Maximo Application Suite.

That is the whole of IBM's public statement on the page we read. It does not say "Sustained Support includes X and excludes Y."

### What partners describe

Naviam, a Maximo services firm, describes the Sustained Support package as:

- **Included:** support for usage and how-to questions; access to existing fixes that were available before the end-of-support date.
- **Not included:** updates, patches or enhancements issued after that date; and, in their words, "no new security patches will be available."

Blue Mountain, another partner, is more careful and, we think, more useful. It says the page "doesn't list covered services," that Sustained Support is "not automatic continuation of the previous support arrangement," and that exclusions, response commitments and responsibilities "must be confirmed in the governing terms."

### Our recommended working assumption

For planning, assume the Naviam description is accurate. If you assume more and you are wrong, you will have a security gap nobody budgeted for. If you assume less and you are wrong, the cost is that you were conservative. Then convert the assumption into an answer by asking IBM in writing.

| Capability | Base support (before 30 Sep 2025) | Sustained Support (partner description, to be confirmed) | Your risk if you assume wrongly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask "how do I..." questions | Yes | Yes (usage/how-to) | Low |
| Report a defect and get a new fix | Yes | **No new fixes** | A known bug stays a known bug |
| Receive a new security patch for a newly disclosed vulnerability in the product | Yes | **No** | Exposure window is open-ended |
| Download existing fixes | Yes | Yes (those available before the cut-off) | Low, if you can find them |
| Severity-1 production outage response | Defined by agreement | Unclear; confirm | High if you assume a 24x7 response that is not there |
| Help from IBM with third-party component problems | Defined by agreement | Unclear; confirm | Medium to high |
| Product enhancements | Roadmap-driven | No | Expected |

The two rows in bold are the ones that matter. A support model without new fixes is, in security terms, a model where the product's vulnerability list only grows.

### Twelve questions to put to IBM in writing

Calls are conversations; emails are positions. Send these to your IBM representative and file the replies in your contract record.

1. What exactly does Sustained Support for Maximo Asset Management 7.6.1.x include and exclude? Please send the governing terms.
2. Will IBM issue new security fixes for the product under Sustained Support? If not, does IBM publish security bulletins for 7.6.1.x vulnerabilities at all?
3. What is the required fix level for our entitlement, and does it differ for add-ons and industry solutions?
4. What severity levels and response targets apply to Sustained Support cases?
5. Does Sustained Support cover the bundled middleware we deploy with Maximo, or is each component's vendor relationship separate?
6. What does the pricing look like for years one through five, and is there a surcharge over the annual support fee?
7. What roadmap evidence will IBM accept, and how often must we refresh it?
8. What causes eligibility to be withdrawn?
9. How do the 30 April 2027 dual-support terms interact with a Sustained Support purchase?
10. Which of our IBM products (Control Desk, Spatial, industry solutions) have different dates?
11. Can we purchase Sustained Support for part of the estate, and what are the consequences for the remainder?
12. What is the process and timeline to transfer to MAS entitlement if we decide to move earlier?

## 🛡️ Security: What "No New Fixes" Actually Means

Executives hear "no new security patches" and nod. Here is what it means mechanically.

Every piece of software ships with vulnerabilities nobody has found yet. After an end-of-support date, three things change at once:

1. **The vendor stops fixing newly discovered flaws.** Disclosed vulnerabilities remain open for the life of the installation unless you mitigate them yourself.
2. **Disclosure still happens.** Researchers, scanners and attackers keep looking. A flaw that gets published is now a recipe, and the product is not going to change.
3. **Your scanners keep flagging it.** Your vulnerability management team sees findings that cannot be closed by patching and must be handled as accepted risks or compensating controls.

The risk is not uniform. Your exposure depends on what is reachable. Here is a way to think about the layers underneath a Maximo 7.6.1 estate:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Layer 5  Integrations & users                            │
│          REST/OSLC/MIF endpoints, SSO, mobile, 3rd-party │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 4  Maximo application (7.6.1.x)                    │
│          NO NEW FIXES under Sustained Support (partner)  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 3  Application server and Java runtime             │
│          Vendor lifecycles are SEPARATE - check each     │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 2  Database                                        │
│          Db2 / Oracle / SQL Server - own support dates   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 1  Operating system and network                    │
│          Your patch cadence, your segmentation           │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
        Only Layer 4 is directly governed by this post's dates.
        Layers 1-3 can fall out of support on their OWN schedule.
```

The point of the diagram is the last line. People assume the Maximo date is the only date. It is not. The Java runtime, the application server and the database each have their own support lifecycle, and they may already have expired or may expire inside your Sustained Support window. We did not find a single source that tells you whether the bundled middleware receives fixes under Sustained Support, so that is question five in the list above, and you should not assume the answer.

### A quick, honest exposure audit

You can do this in an afternoon. The shell commands below are standard tooling on a WebSphere-based deployment; adjust paths to your installation.

```bash
# 1. Java runtime used by the application server profile
<WAS_HOME>/java/8.0/bin/java -version

# 2. Application server version and installed fix packs
<WAS_HOME>/bin/versionInfo.sh -long

# 3. Operating system level (record it, then check the OS vendor's lifecycle page)
cat /etc/os-release

# 4. What can reach Maximo from outside the plant network? (run from a perimeter host you control)
nmap -Pn -p 80,443,9060,9043,9080,9443 <maximo-host-or-vip>
```

Record each result in a table with three columns: component, version, and the date its vendor stops providing security fixes. If any row has already passed its date, that row is a finding in its own right, regardless of what happens to Maximo itself.

### What an attacker actually needs

For an unsupported business application, exposure typically comes through five routes:

| Route | Example | Typical control |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct internet exposure** | Maximo or its SSO page reachable from the public internet | Remove it. Place it behind a VPN or reverse proxy with authentication |
| **Integration endpoints** | An old REST, SOAP or flat-file integration account with a shared password | Rotate credentials, restrict by source address, review scopes |
| **Weak administrative access** | A shared `maxadmin` password, no MFA on the identity provider | Named admin accounts, MFA through your identity provider |
| **Vulnerable middleware** | An unpatched application server component | Isolate, or replace the component independently of Maximo |
| **Credential reuse and phishing** | A user's stolen password works in Maximo | SSO with MFA; session limits; alerting |

You will notice that none of those five is "a new Maximo vulnerability." Most real-world compromises of business applications come through exposure and credentials, not zero-days. That is the good news: **the controls that matter most are the ones you can implement without IBM.**

## ⚖️ The Audit, Regulatory and Insurance Angle

We need to be careful here because this is the part of the conversation where claims run ahead of evidence. Here is what we found and what we did not.

### What we can say

- IBM's own wording on the dual-support page makes continued EAM use after 30 April 2027 "out of compliance" for perpetual trade-up customers. That is a **licence** statement from the vendor, and it is the single hardest audit exposure in this series.
- A partner source argues that losing active vendor support can lead to "bad IT practices or a violation of regulatory terms requiring active support," depending on the industry. That is a vendor-adjacent claim, and it is true *only if* your regulator or contract actually requires active support.
- CISA has stated that using unsupported software in service of critical infrastructure is dangerous. Separately, in February 2026 CISA issued Binding Operational Directive 26-02 on end-of-support edge devices for federal civilian agencies, and encouraged non-federal organisations to adopt similar practices. That directive targets edge devices, not business applications like Maximo, and it does not bind private operators. We cite it as direction of travel, not as a rule that applies to you.

### What we could not find

- We found **no source** saying a cyber insurer excludes or surcharges Maximo 7.6.1 specifically.
- We found **no source** saying a named regulator requires a vendor-supported Maximo version.
- We found **no source** quantifying audit findings for unsupported EAM platforms.

If someone tells you "the auditors will fail you," ask which auditor, under which standard, and on what evidence.

### How to find out for yourself

| Stakeholder | Question to ask | Why it matters | Who asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **External auditor** | Does our control framework treat an application with no vendor security fixes as a finding? What compensating evidence do you accept? | Pre-empts a surprise at year-end | CFO / Internal Audit |
| **Cyber insurer or broker** | Does our application questionnaire ask about unsupported software? What happens at renewal if we answer yes? | Premium, sublimits and exclusions are in the policy wording | Risk Manager |
| **Regulator or licensing body** | Is any asset-management system we run mentioned in a requirement for supported software? | Nuclear, utilities and transport operators often have specific rules | Compliance |
| **Customers and contracts** | Do any contracts or SLAs require us to run supported versions of systems of record? | A contract clause can bind you even if law does not | Legal |
| **Internal security** | Is there a documented exception for unsupported software, and who signs it? | Without a signed exception, the risk is invisible | CISO |

The principle is simple. **Turn a vague worry into a signed risk acceptance.** If the answer is that your risk is acceptable with compensating controls, that decision needs an owner, a review date and an exit date. An exception that nobody signed is an incident waiting to be found.

## 🛠️ Interim Hardening: The 90-Day Plan

None of these steps replaces migration. They keep the exposure bounded while you execute it. We have ordered them by value for effort.

### Days 1 to 30: Know what you have

- **Fix the facts.** Record the fix level for every environment (production, test, development, training, any vendor-hosted copy). Confirm Subscription and Support status per entitlement.
- **Write down what covers you.** One page: which IBM agreement applies, whether you bought Sustained Support, the gaps, the date. Include the answers to the twelve questions above as they arrive.
- **Build the component table.** Java, application server, database and OS, each with version and vendor end-of-security-fix date.
- **Find the front door.** Which URLs for Maximo are reachable from outside the plant or corporate network? Include the mobile endpoints, integration endpoints and the SSO redirect.
- **Name an owner and a risk-register entry.** The owner is an executive, not a DBA.

### Days 31 to 60: Shrink the attack surface

- **Remove direct internet exposure.** If Maximo is published to the internet, put it behind a VPN, a zero-trust gateway or a hardened reverse proxy with authentication.
- **Enforce SSO with MFA.** Move interactive users to your identity provider. Where the 7.6.1 configuration uses application-server security, confirm it is enabled rather than relying on the legacy database login (the `mxe.useAppServerSecurity` property is the relevant switch in `maximo.properties`; test in a non-production environment first).
- **Clean up accounts.** Disable users who have left, remove shared accounts, rotate every integration credential, and limit integration users to the objects they need.
- **Restrict network paths.** Allow only required ports between the web tier, application tier and database, and only from known sources.
- **Back up and rehearse a restore.** A restore test that has actually been run beats a backup policy that exists on paper. Include the attachments store, not just the database.

### Days 61 to 90: Detect, decide, commit

- **Add monitoring.** Send application-server logs, authentication events and administrative changes to your SIEM. Alert on repeated failures, new admin grants and off-hours integration activity.
- **Run a vulnerability scan with an expiry date.** Document accepted findings with owner and review date, and do not let accepted risks roll over silently.
- **Decide Sustained Support: buy, don't buy, or defer.** Use the decision table below.
- **Fix the roadmap.** A dated migration plan with milestone dates that finish before 30 April 2027 if you are on a perpetual trade-up.
- **Take the go/no-go to the steering committee** before the calendar year ends.

### Should we buy Sustained Support?

| Your situation | Likely answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| On 7.6.1.3+, Subscription and Support current, credible roadmap, migration finishing in more than six months | **Probably yes, for a defined term** | Gives you usage help and a vendor relationship during the build; confirm cost and scope first |
| On 7.6.1.3+, migration go-live within the next quarter | **Possibly not** | Cost may outweigh the benefit; weigh the risk of delays |
| Below 7.6.1.3 | **Fix level first** | You are not eligible as stated until you reach the gate |
| Perpetual trade-up customer with a post-April-2027 migration plan | **Re-plan** | Sustained Support does not extend EAM usage rights past 30 Apr 2027 |
| No roadmap and no budget | **You cannot qualify** | The roadmap is a stated condition; start by writing one |
| Considering independent third-party maintenance | **Get legal review** | Check how it interacts with your IBM licence and with the dual-support terms before signing |

## 🚫 Six Myths We Keep Hearing

1. **"We bought Sustained Support, so we have vendor security cover."** Not as far as partner descriptions go: no new patches. Confirm in writing.
2. **"It's inside the firewall, so it doesn't matter."** Internal does not mean unreachable. Credentials, integrations and flat networks are routes.
3. **"IBM will support us until 2030."** IBM has said it will *offer* a paid option until then, conditionally. The date is not an entitlement.
4. **"Sustained Support fixes the April 2027 problem."** It does not. Dual support is a licensing clause with its own date.
5. **"We can wait for MAS 9.x to settle."** The 9.x line is shipping monthly patch catalogs now. Waiting is a decision with a cost, and the calendar is not waiting with you.
6. **"If nothing has broken yet, nothing will."** Unsupported systems do not fail on a schedule; they fail when the combination of exposure and an unfixed flaw turns up.

## 💰 What Staying Actually Costs

Cost conversations usually compare the migration budget against nothing. A fairer comparison lists what staying costs even if no incident occurs.

| Cost of staying on 7.6.1 | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sustained Support fees | Direct, recurring | Pricing varies by contract; one partner mentions a possible surcharge over annual support, unverified |
| Compensating controls | Direct, one-off plus run | VPN or gateway, MFA integration, logging, scanning, testing |
| Extra audit and risk work | Indirect | Exception documentation, review cycles, auditor questions |
| Forgone capability | Opportunity | No enhancements, no mobile or AI improvements that ship only on MAS |
| Skills risk | Indirect | Fewer practitioners want to maintain legacy; contractor rates and turnover |
| Licence exposure | Contingent | For perpetual trade-up customers, continued EAM use after 30 Apr 2027 is out of compliance per IBM |
| Incident risk | Contingent | Unfixable flaw plus exposure; the size of this term depends on your controls |

You will notice the biggest lines are contingent. That is why the first job of Part 4 in this series is a business-case model that prices them rather than ignoring them.

## 🔟 The Ten Rules of Staying on 7.6.1

1. **Treat Sustained Support as a bridge.** It supports a migration; it is not a destination.
2. **Read IBM's wording, not a summary of it.** Terms live in the governing agreement.
3. **Get inclusions and exclusions in writing.** Ask the twelve questions.
4. **Assume no new security fixes.** Design controls as if you have none.
5. **Reach 7.6.1.3 before you negotiate.** The gate is a precondition.
6. **Keep the roadmap alive.** A stale plan is an eligibility risk.
7. **Check the layers underneath.** Java, application server, database and OS have their own dates.
8. **Shrink exposure first.** Internet exposure and weak credentials cause most real incidents.
9. **Sign the risk acceptance.** An unowned risk is not managed.
10. **Remember 30 April 2027.** It is 195 days away and it is not moved by any support purchase.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Sustained Support for Maximo 7.6.1 include new security patches?**
Based on what we could verify, no. IBM's end-of-support page gives the terms (one year of Extended Support, up to five years of Sustained Support) but does not itemise inclusions. The itemised description comes from partner write-ups: Naviam states that Sustained Support covers usage and how-to questions plus existing fixes available before the end-of-support date, and that no new security patches will be available. Treat that as the planning assumption and get the exact coverage in writing from IBM.

**Do I need Maximo 7.6.1.3 to qualify for Sustained Support?**
IBM's page says eligible customers must be current on IBM Subscription and Support, on 7.6.1.3 or later, unable to upgrade before 30 September 2025, and able to show they are preparing an upgrade roadmap. If you are on 7.6.1.0, .1 or .2 you fall below the stated gate. Ask IBM which fix level your entitlement letter names, because one secondary source cited a different level.

**Does buying Sustained Support move the 30 April 2027 dual-support deadline?**
No. Sustained Support is a support purchase on the legacy product, while dual support is a licensing arrangement under the MAS entitlement. IBM's notice, last modified 8 July 2026, says dual support ends on 30 April 2027, and that for perpetual trade-up customers EAM usage rights expire after that date. Part 2 covers how to confirm your licensing path.

**Will an auditor or insurer fail us for running Maximo 7.6.1 on Sustained Support?**
We found no source saying so, and we would rather say that than invent one. Unsupported software is commonly raised as a risk in security reviews, and CISA has called unsupported software in critical infrastructure dangerous. Whether it is a finding, an exclusion or a premium factor for you depends on your auditor, your policy wording and your regulator. Ask each of them in writing before renewal, not after an incident.

**What should I do in the next 30 days if I am on 7.6.1?**
Confirm in writing which IBM agreement covers you and what it excludes; confirm your fix level and eligibility; inventory Java, application server, database and OS with their vendor dates; remove direct internet exposure and tighten integration and admin access; and put a dated decision on the risk register tied to 30 April 2027.
