<aside>

🎯 **Who this is for:** Maximo and EAM architects and development leads deciding whether an AI coding agent belongs in their customization and integration work, platform and security teams who will be asked to approve it, consultancies who would put it on client code, and IT decision-makers who have just heard "IBM has an AI coding partner, and the Maximo team says it saved 69%." If you want to know what is real, what is IBM-reported, and what nobody has verified yet, start here.

## 📖 Why This Series Exists

You have been through this before. A new tool shows up with a slide that says "days to hours," your director forwards it with a one-line question, and you are the person who has to answer it. You know what a Maximo customization actually involves: an automation script that touches three objects and a crossover domain, a Java MBO nobody has opened since 7.5, an application XML change that looks harmless until it breaks a signature option in production. You also know that "it generated the code" and "it is safe to promote" are two very different sentences.

IBM Bob lands in exactly that gap. IBM calls it an "AI SDLC partner," and the release notes since April 2026 read like a platform maturing in public: a V2 rebuild in June, premium packages and analytics in July, a shell rewrite and group policies in August, self-hosted deployment on OpenShift in September. Maximo shops are asking about it for three specific reasons, and each one deserves a straight answer rather than a brochure:

1. **IBM itself pointed at Maximo.** The Bob GA announcement includes a claim from the IBM Maximo development team. We will show you the sentence and the reasons you should not plan a budget around it.
2. **MAS 9.2 made Maximo agent-addressable.** The suite now ships a native Maximo MCP server, and Bob is an MCP client. The pieces fit, but the end-to-end story is younger than the marketing suggests.
3. **Your Java estate is a modernization target.** IBM publishes Bob skills that convert legacy Java MBO classes into automation scripts and review existing scripts for security and performance. That is real, testable work, and exactly where an agent can help or hurt.

This series is not IBM sales copy dressed up as Maximo content, and it is not a takedown either. It draws one consistent line: **vendor numbers are labeled IBM-reported, independent views are labeled independent, and anything that could not be verified goes onto a checklist you can take to IBM.** Our source base is IBM's own documentation, IBM newsroom releases, the Bob blog, and independent press and analyst coverage, and where those disagree we show both readings instead of picking the flattering one.

<aside>

💡 **Key insight:** The organizing question of this series is not "is Bob good?" It is **"which Maximo tasks can an agent safely do under your controls, how will you know it worked, and what does it cost per completed task?"** A tool that is excellent at a task you never do is worthless; a tool that is mediocre but governable on a task you do weekly can pay back. Everything in the five parts serves that question.

</aside>

## 🔑 What Makes This Series Different

Three ideas run through every part. Hold onto them and the series reads as one argument instead of five product tours.

**Every vendor number wears a label.** You will see "[IBM-reported]" and "[independent]" attached to figures throughout, and the labels are not decoration. IBM says 45% average productivity gain from a survey of its own users, 69% time savings for its own Maximo team, 70% for its own Instana team. Those are internal, self-reported results, and the press noticed: The Register described IBM as the latest vendor to lean on its own staff to "prove" AI efficacy, and IDC's analyst paper (which IBM published) states that "the external evidence base remains limited at GA." We carry IBM's numbers because they are the numbers your executives will quote. We attach their provenance because you are the person who has to defend the budget.

**Control is the product, not the model.** The most useful independent framing we found, from VentureBeat, is that the difference between Bob and its rivals "is not about capabilities but about control." Approval gates on edits, commands, MCP calls and skills; rollback; workspace trust; `.bobignore`; group policies pushed by MDM or GPO; enforced hooks; activity logs in CADF format. For a Maximo shop, those are the features that decide whether an AI-authored change to a production customization can survive your change advisory board. The model underneath will keep changing (on SaaS you cannot even choose it); the control surface is what you can design around.

**Agents go through the API layer, never the database.** This is the Maximo-specific rule that survives every product comparison. The MAS 9.2 MCP server turns Manage objects, scripts and API routes into tools that run under Manage's own authorization and validation. A TheMaximoGuys MCP server does the same for earlier versions. Either way, the safe posture is a dedicated least-privilege API key, read-only first, and no agent with direct database credentials. Parts 3 and 4 show how to enforce that centrally.

<aside>

💡 **Key insight:** If you remember one sentence from the index, make it this: **label the number, scope the key, measure your own task.** Skip the first and you quote marketing as fact; skip the second and an agent holds more access than a junior admin; skip the third and you never learn whether the tool paid for itself.

## 🧪 One Concrete Example: What "Governed" Looks Like

The series stays grounded in real configuration rather than slideware. Here is the kind of control the governance and security parts build on: a Linux group policy file that stops anyone on a workstation from auto-approving the riskiest action classes in the Bob IDE. IBM documents group policies for macOS (`com.ibm.bob`), Windows (`Software\Policies\IBM\Bob`) and Linux (`/etc/bob/policy.json`, root-owned), and lists `DisabledAutoApprovalGroups` among the notable policy keys; check the live group-policies page for exact syntax before you deploy, because key names and value formats are the sort of detail that changes between releases.

```json
{
  "DisabledAutoApprovalGroups": ["edit", "execute", "mcp"],
  "UpdateMode": "manual"
}
```

Why these three groups? In January 2026, PromptArmor researchers showed that the beta Bob Shell could be tricked, through a malicious README and an "always allow" setting on `echo`, into chaining commands that ran without approval and downloaded malware (reported by The Register; IBM said it was unaware of the issue and pledged fixes before GA). The lesson that outlives the specific bug is that **auto-approve is the attack surface**. Locking `edit`, `execute` and `mcp` pushes every file change, shell command and MCP tool call back through a human, which on a workstation that can reach a client's Maximo repository or an MCP endpoint is the right default.

And here is the matching `.bobignore` idea for a Maximo working copy. IBM is explicit that `.bobignore` "does not create a system-level sandbox," so treat it as a convenience that keeps secrets out of context, not as a security boundary:

```text
# .bobignore: keep credentials and environment config out of agent context
.env
*.jks
*.p12
maximo.properties
**/secrets/**
```

Part 3 explains where each of these controls stops, because "we have a policy file" and "we have an audit trail" are different claims. Part 2 documents the open question about what the SaaS activity log does and does not capture.

## 💡 Key Themes Across the Series

**IBM's numbers are directional marketing signals, not benchmarks.** Across the product material you will meet 45%, 69%, 70%, "10x," "100% accuracy" and "30 days to 3 days." Each is IBM-reported or appears in an IBM-published customer story, and none has been independently audited. The series quotes them accurately, labels them, and then shows you how to produce comparable figures for your own work mix.

**Plan, then act.** IBM's own guidance says to start unfamiliar or high-surface-area work in Ask or Plan mode and switch to Agent only once the work is clear, and its blog states that "the code is the cheap part." For Maximo, a plan attached to a change record is the cheapest control you can add. The general version of this argument lives in the Enterprise Vibe Coding series; here it is applied to Manage customizations.

**Review capacity is the real bottleneck.** IBM's own Think content says that "review capacity becomes the limiting factor," and it cites a third-party report in which 85% of DevSecOps professionals say AI moved the bottleneck from writing code to reviewing it. A Maximo shop that generates ten times the scripts but reviews them at the same speed has not gained ten times anything. Part 5's pilot measures review hours for exactly this reason.

**SaaS and self-hosted are different quality and control tiers.** Self-hosted Bob gives you sovereignty and air-gapped operation. It also means one core model, no Bobalytics at GA, and no in-place upgrades, and IBM's own guidance says frontier models deliver the highest reasoning quality on complex legacy work. If your constraint forces self-hosted, plan for a pilot that measures the model you will actually run, not the one in the demo.

**Context quality decides output quality.** The one independent Maximo evaluation we found (a Sopra Steria consultant's tests on SQL, BIRT, application XML, log analysis and Java to Jython conversion) concluded that results depend heavily on the context you provide. That is why this series keeps returning to rules, `AGENTS.md`, standards files and accurate API catalogs: they are the cheapest quality lever you own.

## 🔮 What We Do Not Know Yet

An honest index lists its own gaps. These are the items our source base could not settle, and each is revisited in the parts noted:

- **Self-hosted availability date.** IBM's September release notes say it was generally available as of September 24, 2026; the newsroom announcement is dated October 1 and does not use the phrase "GA." We use "GA per release notes September 24; announced October 1." (Part 1)
- **Audit logging on self-hosted deployments.** IBM's September blog says the self-hosted offering includes audit logging and usage metering, while the solution-architecture documentation says those services are not included in the initial release. Resolve this with IBM before you promise anyone an audit trail. (Parts 2 and 3)
- **SaaS model versions and routing policy.** IBM names model families, not versions, and does not show which model handled a task. (Part 2)
- **Tokens per Bobcoin and self-hosted pricing.** Neither is published. (Part 3)
- **How the 69% was measured.** One sentence, no method. (Part 4)
- **Bob calling the MAS 9.2 MCP server in production.** No published end-to-end walkthrough exists that we could find, so treat it as a sandbox experiment. (Part 4)
