# Governing Bob: Admin Controls, Audit, Bobcoins, and CAB-Style Change Control for AI-Authored Maximo Changes

<aside>

🎯 **Who this is for:** Maximo platform owners, security and compliance leads, change managers, and engagement leads who have to answer one question before an AI agent touches a Maximo customization: "Who approved this, what exactly did it change, and what does it cost us?" You do not need to be an AI enthusiast. You need to know which controls are real, where each one stops, and how to fill the gaps with your own process.

</aside>

**Series:** *IBM Bob for Maximo Teams* | **Part 3 of 5** | **Read time:** ~28 minutes
**Previous:** [Part 2, Models, Security, and Self-Hosted](/blog/mas-bob-02-models-security-self-hosted) | **Next:** [Part 4, Bob Meets Maximo](/blog/mas-bob-04-bob-and-maximo)

## 🔥 The Change Request Nobody Knows How to Classify

It is Tuesday, and the CAB agenda has a new kind of line item.

*"CHG-20418: Update WOCOMPLETE autoscript to validate failure class before closing. Author: A. Developer (with Bob). Risk: Medium."*

Somebody on the board asks the obvious question: "With Bob? What does that mean? Did a person write this?" The developer says Bob drafted it, they reviewed it, and it works in dev. A second member asks which model wrote it. Nobody knows. A third asks whether there is a record of what Bob was allowed to do on that workstation while it worked. The developer says they clicked "approve" a lot.

That meeting is the reason this part exists. In [Part 1](/blog/mas-bob-01-what-is-ibm-bob) we covered what Bob is. In [Part 2](/blog/mas-bob-02-models-security-self-hosted) we covered what IBM says about models, data, and indemnity. This part is about the other half of adoption: **the controls you operate after you say yes**. That means the admin console, the policy files, the analytics, the logs, the bill, and, most importantly, how you fit all of it into a change process you already run.

Two principles run through everything below.

**First, a control is only as good as the claim it lets you make.** "We have a policy file" and "we have an audit trail" are different claims. Throughout this part, every control gets a plain statement of what it proves and what it does not.

**Second, an AI agent is an author, never an approver.** Your CAB does not need a new religion. It needs to treat Bob-authored changes the way it treats contractor-authored changes: a named human is accountable, the evidence is attached, and the people who approve are not the people who benefit from speed.

<aside>

💡 **Key insight:** Bob gives you decent device-level and identity-level governance. It gives you almost nothing that says what the agent did to your Maximo estate. That evidence comes from your own process, and the sooner you design it, the less painful the first audit.

</aside>

## 🗺️ The Control Map: Four Layers, Four Different Stopping Points

Bob's governance surface is easier to reason about if you stop treating it as one thing. It is four layers, each answering a different question.

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  LAYER 1: DEVICE POLICY      "What can the agent do on this box?" ║
║  Group policies (MDM / GPO / policy.json)         [Bob IDE only] ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  LAYER 2: IDENTITY           "Who is this person?"               ║
║  SSO (SAML / OIDC), domain verification, teams    [Enterprise]   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  LAYER 3: USAGE ANALYTICS    "How much and how well is it used?" ║
║  Bobalytics, Bobcoin spend, budgets         [SaaS Enterprise]    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  LAYER 4: AUDIT              "What happened, and can we prove it?"║
║  CADF activity log, SIEM (Splunk), EnforcedHooks   [Enterprise]  ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
         None of these four layers records what Bob did
         inside your Maximo environment. That is Layer 5, and you build it.
```

| Layer | Answers | Proves | Does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Device policy | Can auto-approve be turned on? | A setting is locked on managed machines | That a developer used a managed machine; that the agent behaved |
| 2. Identity | Who signed in? | Authentication through your IdP, on a verified domain | What that person asked the agent to do |
| 3. Analytics | How much is Bob used and how much code is Bob's? | Adoption, spend, and share of committed lines | Whether the code was right, reviewed, or valuable |
| 4. Audit | When did identity and admin events occur? | Sign-ins, user and seat changes | Prompts, tool calls, edits, or model choice |
| 5. Maximo-side evidence (yours) | What changed in the estate? | The API calls, the diff, the approvals | Nothing about the agent's reasoning, which is why Plan mode matters |

Keep this table handy. Every section below slots into one row.

## 🔒 Lock Auto-Approval First

If you do one thing from this entire series, do this.

In Part 2 we covered the PromptArmor finding from January 2026, where a prompt injection hidden in a README steered Bob toward downloading and running malware once command auto-approval was in play. The lesson was not "Bob is unsafe." The lesson was that **the approval gate is the control, and anything that lets users switch it off is a hole**. Group policies are how you close it.

### What IBM documents

IBM's group policy page lists the policy names and their types. These are the ones that matter for governance:

| Policy | Type | What it does (IBM's description) |
|---|---|---|
| `DisabledAutoApprovalGroups` | string | "Permanently disables auto-approval for one or more tool-permission groups." Comma-separated group IDs: `read`, `edit`, `execute`, `mcp`, `skill`, `todo`, `subtask`, `subagent`, `mode` |
| `UpdateMode` | string | Controls automatic update behavior. Allowed values: `default`, `start`, `manual`, `none` |
| `GatewayUrl` | string | Enforces the backend gateway URL, for example `https://bob.gateway.example.com` |
| `EnforcedHooks` | string | "Must be a JSON-encoded string that matches the hooks configuration schema." Runs before user-defined hooks and cannot be overridden |

DOC16 also records `RequiredExtensions` (added in the September release) and endpoint distribution for self-hosted. We did not re-fetch those two on the live page, so treat them as carried from our knowledge base.

### Where the policy lives

| Platform | Source | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| macOS | `com.ibm.bob` managed preferences | MDM configuration profile |
| Windows | `Software\Policies\IBM\Bob` registry key | Group Policy (GPO), Intune via ADMX/ADML, or registry edit |
| Linux | `/etc/bob/policy.json` | Configuration management tool or manual edit |

IBM states that locked settings show as read-only in the product, and DOC16 records that on Linux the file should be root-owned with mode 644.

> **Scope warning, in IBM's own words:** group policies are documented as **Bob IDE only** at the time of writing. That has direct consequences for Bob Shell, CI runners, and any scripted `bob run` usage. See "Open items" below.

### What each approval group means for Maximo work

IBM's page describes the groups by tool coverage: `read` covers file reads, directory listing, and search; `edit` covers file write, create, and delete; `execute` covers terminal and shell command execution. The others (`mcp`, `skill`, `todo`, `subtask`, `subagent`, `mode`) cover the remaining operational areas. Here is how we translate that into Maximo terms:

| Group | What it lets an agent do without asking | Maximo-flavored risk | Our recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `read` | Read files, list directories, search | Reads `maximo.properties`, `.env` files, exported XML with real data | Leave auto-approved only if `.bobignore` and workspace hygiene are in place; otherwise lock |
| `edit` | Write, create, delete files | Overwrites an autoscript, deletes a migration file, edits app XML | **Lock** |
| `execute` | Run terminal commands | Runs `curl` against a Maximo endpoint, runs a script that posts to REST | **Lock, always** |
| `mcp` | Call MCP tools | Calls a Maximo MCP tool that updates a work order | **Lock, always** |
| `skill` | Run installed skills | Runs a skill that contains shell steps | Lock on client-code machines |
| `subagent`, `subtask` | Spawn delegated work | Fans out edits you did not individually approve | Lock where `edit` and `execute` are locked, since delegated work inherits the problem |
| `todo`, `mode` | Update task lists, switch modes | Low | Leave open |

### An illustrative policy

The key names below are IBM's. The exact file shape, quoting, and the way the registry value must be typed are **illustrative, not verified against a live install**. Check IBM's current page before deploying.

```json
{
  "DisabledAutoApprovalGroups": "edit,execute,mcp,skill,subagent,subtask",
  "UpdateMode": "manual",
  "GatewayUrl": "https://bob.gateway.example.com"
}
```

```text
# Windows (illustrative): push via GPO or Intune ADMX/ADML rather than by hand
HKLM\Software\Policies\IBM\Bob
    DisabledAutoApprovalGroups  (REG_SZ)  edit,execute,mcp,skill,subagent,subtask
    UpdateMode                  (REG_SZ)  manual
```

Why `UpdateMode: manual`? Because of Part 2's point about SaaS routing: you cannot pin the model, but you can at least stop the client from changing underneath a regulated change window. That is a judgment call, so write down why you picked it.

### Prove it is locked

A policy is a claim until you check it. Add a one-line verification to your workstation baseline:

```bash
# Linux (illustrative): confirm the policy file exists, is root-owned, and contains the lock
stat -c '%U %a %n' /etc/bob/policy.json
grep -o '"DisabledAutoApprovalGroups"[^,}]*' /etc/bob/policy.json
```

Then do the human test: ask a developer to try to enable auto-approve for `execute` in settings. The control should appear read-only. Screenshot it for the audit file.

<aside>

💡 **Key insight:** Auto-approval is not a convenience setting. It is the point at which the human stops being the control. Your policy file is the CAB's first line of evidence that the human is still in the loop.

</aside>

## 🪝 EnforcedHooks: The Closest Thing to an Agent Action Log

The activity log, as we will see, does not record agent actions. The mechanism IBM does give you for that is **enforced hooks**. `EnforcedHooks` is a policy whose value is a JSON-encoded string matching Bob's hooks configuration schema. IBM states these hooks run before user-defined hooks and cannot be overridden by the user. DOC16 adds that hook handlers can be HTTPS endpoints, and that the GA release says Bob Shell "records agent actions as they happen."

That combination is the design lever for CAB evidence: a hook that fires on tool use and posts a record to a service **you** own, outside the developer's control.

### What a Maximo-shaped hook should capture

We have not seen IBM's hook schema fields in this run, so the table below describes the **evidence you want**, not field names. Map each row onto whatever events the schema exposes.

| Evidence | Why the CAB wants it | Where it should land |
|---|---|---|
| Who (SSO identity) and which workstation | Accountability | Your logging service, tagged with the change ID |
| Timestamp | Sequence of events | Same |
| Tool type (edit, execute, mcp) | Classifies the action | Same |
| File path or command or MCP tool name | What was touched | Same |
| Approval decision (user approved or denied) | Proves the human gate | Same |
| Repository and branch | Ties to the diff | Same |

### Guardrails for the hook itself

- **Fail closed or fail loud.** If the hook endpoint is unreachable, decide in advance whether the action is blocked or merely flagged. Our default for production-adjacent repositories is flag-and-alert, because blocking every developer on an endpoint outage will push people toward workarounds.
- **Do not log secrets.** A hook that records command lines will capture tokens passed in headers. Redact before storage.
- **Test it on Bob Shell and the IDE separately.** IBM documents group policies, including `EnforcedHooks`, as IDE-scoped. If your pipeline calls Bob Shell, you may have no enforced hook at all there.

## 🪪 Identity: SSO, Domains, Teams, and What Is Not Documented

IBM's identity provider page states that Bob supports **SAML** and **OIDC**, with verified OIDC providers **Auth0, Keycloak, Okta, and PingOne**. Domain ownership is proven with a DNS TXT record in the form `bob-verify=<verification-code>`, and the status flips to "Verified" once Bob sees it.

| Item | What IBM documents | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Protocols | SAML and OIDC | Covers Entra ID and most enterprise IdPs through SAML; verify yours explicitly |
| Verified OIDC providers | Auth0, Keycloak, Okta, PingOne | Keycloak matters if you already run Red Hat build of Keycloak beside MAS |
| Domain verification | TXT record `bob-verify=<code>` | A DNS change request; plan lead time |
| Removing an IdP | Delete under Administration, Authentication; "removes the SSO configuration for all associated domains" | Deleting an IdP can lock out everyone on it; make it a CAB-controlled admin change |
| SCIM | **Not mentioned** on the page we fetched | DOC16 says "contact IBM"; ask directly |

### The deprovisioning question

DOC16 records that deprovisioning follows the IdP. For a consultancy, the question to ask is practical: when a contractor leaves the engagement and their IdP account is disabled, how fast does their Bob seat stop working and stop consuming Bobcoins, and is that event in the activity log? We could not find that stated. Put it on your vendor questionnaire.

### Teams and budgets

Per IBM's enterprise documentation, an admin can add and remove team members, form teams, assign seats, allocate Bobcoins from a shared pool, and set individual spending limits. That makes **team** the natural unit for governance. We suggest aligning Bob teams to your Maximo delivery boundaries, such as "Manage customizations," "Integrations," and "Reporting," so that spend, Bobalytics, and your CAB categories line up.

## 📊 Bobalytics: What It Measures and What It Cannot

Bobalytics is Bob's analytics view for admins. IBM states it is available to **Enterprise** plan users, is reached from the Admin console's Bobalytics tab, and, per the self-hosted known-limitations page, is **not supported on self-hosted Bob at GA**.

### The three KPIs

| KPI | IBM's definition | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| **Adoption rate** | Average daily active users divided by total licensed seats | Seat waste detector. Low adoption means you are paying for shelfware |
| **Bob factor** | Percentage of committed lines of code that Bob created (lines by Bob divided by total lines committed) | How much of your committed code is agent-written |
| **Bobcoin spend** | Total Bobcoins consumed over a selected period | Cost, by period and team |

DOC16 adds two details from IBM's documentation: a user counts as "active" after accepting at least one code completion or completing a chat conversation, and commits with zero Bob lines are excluded when the total exceeds 40,000 lines of code, to filter out templates and fixtures. We did not re-confirm those two on the live page.

### IBM's interpretation guide

IBM pairs the metrics: **high Bob factor with low spend** indicates effective use; **high spend with low Bob factor** suggests Bob is being used for research, explanation, or exploration rather than code. That second pattern is not a problem. It is a different job, and you should decide whether you are funding it.

### Exports and privacy

Available tables are Bob factor compared with team, Bob's repository impact, most frequently used modes, and Bob's code contribution by language. Each exports as a separate CSV in a ZIP, which is easy to feed to Power BI, Tableau, or Looker. On privacy, team charts anonymize users as "User 1, User 2," and IBM states that "admins cannot access a specific user's data from any team-level chart." Individual users can see their own data.

That privacy design is good for developer trust. It is also a limit: **you cannot use Bobalytics to investigate an individual's behavior in an incident.** For that you need the SIEM and your own hooks.

### The measurement trap

Bob factor counts lines authored. It does not count defects, rework, review time, or whether the lines were the right ones. IBM's own Think content says token consumption "is a cost signal, not a value metric," and the same logic applies to lines. If you report Bob factor to executives as productivity, you are about to be asked what the defect rate is, and Bobalytics will not have the answer.

| Metric | Source | Why you need it beside Bob factor |
|---|---|---|
| Bob factor | Bobalytics | Share of code the agent wrote |
| Defect escape rate on AI-authored changes | Your ticketing and CAB records | Quality |
| Review hours per change | PR tooling, CAB timestamps | The real bottleneck (the GitLab survey IBM cites says 85% of DevSecOps professionals see AI moving the bottleneck from writing to reviewing) |
| Rollbacks and emergency changes | Change records | Operational impact |
| Bobcoins per completed Maximo task | Bobalytics spend divided by closed tickets | The unit economics IBM does not publish |

We build the pilot scorecard around exactly this table in [Part 5](/blog/mas-bob-05-pilot-playbook).

## 🧾 What the Audit Trail Does and Does Not Prove

This is where most governance conversations go wrong, so we will be literal.

### What IBM documents

The Enterprise **activity log** is a downloadable record of security-relevant events in the Bob Enterprise organization. IBM lists two categories:

| Category | Events | Storage |
|---|---|---|
| Authentication | User sign-in and sign-out; token renewals | Stored globally |
| Admin activity | User creation, updates, and removal; team creation, updates, and deletion; team membership changes; seat assignments | Stored per region |

Each file covers a one-hour window identified by date and hour. Files are JSON in the **CADF** (Cloud Auditing Data Federation) format. The documented fields are `action`, `outcome`, `eventTime` (UTC), `initiator.id` (the email address of the user who performed the action), and `attachments[].content.subscription_id`, `.instance_id`, and `.category`. The page does not specify a retention period and gives no example JSON.

DOC16 records example `action` values such as `admin.user.create` and `bob-authn.session.authenticate`. We did not see those on the live page, so treat them as illustrative.

### The SIEM feed

IBM's SIEM integration page says you **cannot configure SIEM integration yourself** on the Administration page. You open a case with IBM Support, and "in most cases, onboarding takes a few days." You provide:

- Subscription ID(s)
- Splunk HEC endpoint
- Splunk HEC token
- Splunk index name
- CA certificate, if you use a self-signed certificate

"Splunk is the tested example today. Bob can also route your logs to other SIEM backends that use Vector integrations," with coordination required. Critically: "The events forwarded to your SIEM are the same audit events available in the activity log."

### Evidence matrix: what a CAB or auditor will ask, and whether Bob can answer

| Auditor question | Answered by Bob's audit log? | Where the answer actually lives |
|---|---|---|
| Who had a Bob seat on date X? | **Yes** (seat assignments, admin events) | Activity log, SIEM |
| Who signed in during the change window? | **Yes** (authentication events) | Activity log, SIEM |
| Was a user added or removed, and by whom? | **Yes** | Activity log, SIEM |
| What prompt produced this autoscript? | **Not documented** | Developer's local task history (default 14 days, per IBM's FAQ via DOC16), your own hook records |
| Which files did the agent edit? | **Not documented** | Git history; your hook records |
| Which commands did the agent run? | **Not documented** | Your hook records; terminal history |
| Which Maximo records did it touch through MCP or API? | **No** | Maximo logs, API key usage, Max_mcp or native MCP server logs |
| Which model wrote it? | **No** (not even IBM discloses it on SaaS) | Unavailable; record Bob version only |
| Did a human approve each action? | **Not in the activity log** | Hook records, if configured |

DOC16 flags this explicitly as an open question: it is "unclear whether prompts, tool calls, and file edits reach Splunk SIEM," and recommends verifying the SIEM event schema. Our reading of the pages we fetched is that the answer is no, because the SIEM feed is stated to be the same events as the activity log. Confirm with IBM before you promise anyone otherwise.

### Illustrative detection queries

Once the feed lands in Splunk, there are still genuinely useful detections. The index name, field names, and `action` strings below are **placeholders based on the documented CADF fields**; adjust to the real schema after your first test file arrives.

```text
# Seat or user changes outside the approved admin group (illustrative SPL)
index=bob_audit action="admin.*" outcome="success"
| where NOT match(initiator.id, "^(bob-admin1|bob-admin2)@example\.com$")
| table eventTime, initiator.id, action, outcome

# Sign-ins from accounts that should have been offboarded (illustrative SPL)
index=bob_audit action="*authenticate*" outcome="success"
| lookup offboarded_users email AS initiator.id OUTPUT left_on
| where isnotnull(left_on)
| table eventTime, initiator.id, left_on

# Seat assignment bursts (illustrative SPL)
index=bob_audit action="admin.*seat*"
| bin _time span=1h
| stats count by _time, initiator.id
| where count > 10
```

### Self-hosted: the audit conflict, one more time

Part 2 documents this in detail, so here is only the governance consequence. IBM's September release blog says the self-hosted backend includes "audit logging, and usage metering," while the solution-architecture page says the Audit Logs and Metering services are "Not included in the initial release; planned for a future release," and the overview says security event logging is managed at the OpenShift platform level. Until IBM resolves that in writing, **a self-hosted CAB design must assume audit evidence comes from OpenShift logging and your own hooks**, and Bobalytics is not available.

## 🏛️ CAB-Style Change Control for AI-Authored Maximo Changes

Now we put the layers to work. The goal is a change process where an AI-authored change is **no harder to approve than a human-authored one, and no easier**.

### Step 1: Classify by blast radius, not by author

Do not create an "AI change" category that triggers a separate bureaucracy. Add one field to your existing change record, `Authoring method`, and let your existing risk tiers do the work.

| Change class | Examples in Maximo | Default CAB tier | Bob mode and approvals | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Analysis only** | Explain an autoscript, summarize a crontask, draft a Job Plan description | None (no change) | Ask mode; read-only | None beyond workstation policy |
| **B. Dev and test artifacts** | New or changed autoscript in dev, app XML in a sandbox, BIRT draft | Standard or pre-approved | Plan then Agent; `edit` approved per action | Plan output, diff, test result |
| **C. Production-bound code** | Autoscript promoted via Migration Manager, Java extension, integration object structure or publish channel change | Normal change, CAB review | Plan mode output attached; no agent access to production credentials | Plan, diff, code review by a second human, test evidence, rollback plan |
| **D. Data or configuration change in production** | Bulk update of work orders, domain value changes, security group changes | High-risk change, CAB approval plus business owner | Agent **drafts** the change script or payload only; a human runs it through your normal path | Plan, payload, dry-run results, named executor |
| **E. Security and access** | New API keys, security group edits, Bob admin changes | CAB plus security approval | Agent does not perform | Normal security process |

Notice class D. For anything that mutates production data, our recommendation is that the agent writes the change but never holds the credential that applies it.

### Step 2: Make Plan mode the review artifact

Bob has Plan mode, and IBM's own line is that "the plan is the review artifact." (Part 1 covers the modes.) For a CAB that is the most useful sentence in Bob's documentation, because it moves the review upstream of the code. Attach the plan to the change record for classes B through D, and require the reviewer to check three things in the plan itself:

1. **Scope:** Are the files and Maximo objects listed the ones the change request names, and no others?
2. **Side effects:** Does the plan call any MCP tool, API, or shell command that writes?
3. **Rollback:** Does the plan say how to undo it?

### Step 3: Define the evidence package

Here is a change record template. It is not an IBM artifact; it is a suggested shape. Keep it in whatever your ITSM tool supports.

```yaml
change_id: CHG-20418
title: "WOCOMPLETE autoscript: validate failure class before close"
authoring_method: ai-assisted            # human | ai-assisted | ai-drafted
change_class: C
accountable_human: a.developer@example.com
code_reviewer: b.reviewer@example.com    # must differ from accountable_human
ai_tooling:
  tool: IBM Bob (SaaS)
  seat_type: Enterprise                  # indemnity depends on this
  client_version: "record from About dialog"
  model: "not disclosed by IBM (SaaS auto-routing)"
  mode_used: [Plan, Agent]
  policy_locked_groups: "edit,execute,mcp,skill,subagent,subtask"
evidence:
  plan_output: attachments/CHG-20418-bob-plan.md
  diff: "git: feature/wocomplete-failureclass @ <commit>"
  approval_log: attachments/CHG-20418-hook-records.json
  test_results: attachments/CHG-20418-dev-tests.md
  maximo_api_key_scope: "key TMG-DEV-AUTOSCRIPT-RW, group BOB_DEV_SCRIPTS"
rollback: "Migration Manager package v-prev; documented in plan step 6"
```

Three fields are worth explaining. `model` is deliberately filled in with "not disclosed," because writing down what you do not know is better than leaving it blank. `seat_type` matters because indemnity depends on it. `code_reviewer` must differ from the accountable human, which is separation of duties.

### Step 4: Scope the Maximo-side credential

This is Layer 5, and it is the part only you can build. Bob will happily operate through whatever credential you give it. In Maximo Manage, API keys are created in the **API Keys** application, which registers the key to a user; IBM's documentation notes an API key is displayed only once and cannot be retrieved later, and that API key authentication requires the `mxe.oslc.webappurl` system property to point at your Manage server. A user's reach is then defined by the security group of the user the key belongs to.

| Use case | Key owner (service user) | Security group scope | Object structures (examples) | Environment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis and read-only Q&A | `BOB_READONLY` | Read on specific apps and object structures | `MXAPIWODETAIL`, `MXAPIASSET` read | Non-production data only, or masked |
| Autoscript development | `BOB_DEV_SCRIPTS` | Autoscript and script-deploy signature options; **no production** | Autoscript object structure | Dev only |
| Work order drafting | `BOB_WO_DRAFT` | Create and change on draft work orders; no status change to approved states | `MXAPIWODETAIL` | Test |
| Production execution | None for Bob | Human service accounts via your normal path | n/a | Production |

Three rules:

1. **One key per use case, never per developer, never shared.** When something goes wrong, the key name tells you what the agent was doing.
2. **No production key on a workstation that runs Bob.** `.bobignore` is not a sandbox (Part 2), and an agent with command execution can read environment variables.
3. **Rotate on a schedule and on every offboarding.** Keys are shown once, so rotation is a re-issue, not a lookup.

If you use the native MAS 9.2 MCP server or TMG's Max_mcp (agent-neutral, proprietary, 175 tools; see [Part 4](/blog/mas-bob-04-bob-and-maximo)), mark which tools are destructive and keep them out of the key's reach through the security group, not just through the tool list.

### Step 5: Separation of duties

| Role | Can do | Cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| Developer | Drive Bob, approve agent actions in dev | Approve own CAB item; hold production credentials |
| Code reviewer | Review diff and plan | Be the developer |
| CAB approver | Approve or reject | Be author or reviewer |
| Bob admin | Assign seats, set budgets, view Bobalytics | Alter group policy without a change record |
| Maximo security admin | Create API keys and security groups | Use Bob's agent to do it |

IBM's own measure of where AI moves the bottleneck is worth quoting for your CAB charter: the GitLab 2026 AI Accountability Report, cited by IBM, found 85% of DevSecOps professionals say AI shifted the bottleneck from writing code to reviewing it. If you add Bob without adding review capacity, you will make the CAB the slowest thing in the pipeline.

### Step 6: Run a periodic control review

| Frequency | Check | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Per change | Plan, diff, approval log, key scope attached | Change record |
| Weekly | New Bob seats versus HR roster; overage spend; failed or denied approvals | Activity log, Bobalytics CSV |
| Monthly | Policy file hash on a sample of workstations; API key inventory | Baseline scan |
| Quarterly | Bob factor versus defect escape rate; seat mix; open items with IBM | Scorecard |
| On every Bob release | Re-read the changelog for policy, audit, and model changes | Release notes |

## ⚠️ Pitfalls and Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A developer says auto-approve "still works" | The user is on Bob Shell or a non-managed install; group policies are documented for the IDE only | Inventory install types; confirm Shell coverage with IBM; restrict Shell in CI until confirmed |
| Policy change not visible | MDM, GPO, or config management has not propagated; Windows policy under the wrong hive | Check the exact location (`com.ibm.bob`, `Software\Policies\IBM\Bob`, `/etc/bob/policy.json`); restart the client |
| Settings appear editable | Policy not read, or keys misspelled | Verify key names against IBM's page and match them exactly |
| SIEM shows nothing | Onboarding is a support case and "takes a few days"; HEC token or index wrong; self-signed CA not supplied | Re-open the case with all five items; test HEC separately |
| SIEM shows sign-ins but no agent actions | By design, the feed equals the activity log | Add EnforcedHooks to your own endpoint |
| Bobalytics Bob factor is low despite heavy use | Heavy research or exploration (high spend, low Bob factor), or commits excluded by the 40,000-line rule | Read IBM's interpretation guide; do not penalize teams for it |
| Bobalytics empty on self-hosted | Not supported at GA | Plan your own metrics |
| Overage appeared unexpectedly | Individual overage was enabled; it resets to disabled each cycle, but a pool admin may have raised the Enterprise limit | Review overage limits per team monthly |
| Pack Bobcoins vanished | Packs expire one year after purchase | Track purchase dates; buy to usage, not to discount |
| Contractor still has access after leaving | Seat tied to an IdP account or a non-SSO login still active | Reconcile seats against the IdP weekly; ask IBM how deprovisioning reaches seats |
| Auditor asks which model wrote the code | SaaS does not disclose model per task | State that plainly; rely on diff and review, not on model identity |

## 🧭 What to Take Away

### The Ten Commandments of Governing an AI Agent in a Maximo Estate

1. **Lock auto-approval centrally** for edit, execute, mcp, and skill before anyone opens client code.
2. **Verify the lock** with a screenshot and a file check, not a policy export alone.
3. **Never say "audit trail"** until you can name the system that records agent actions.
4. **Use enforced hooks** to send agent-action records to a service you own.
5. **Buy Enterprise for client work** because indemnity, Bobalytics, and the activity log live there.
6. **Never read Bob factor as productivity.** Pair it with defect escape rate and review hours.
7. **Budget from Bobcoins per completed task**, measured in a pilot, never from a formula.
8. **Make Plan mode the review artifact** and attach it to the change record.
9. **Give the agent keys for drafting, never for production execution.**
10. **Write down what you do not know**, including the model, on every change record.
