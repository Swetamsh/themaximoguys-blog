# MAS 9.2: The Grounded Series — What Shipped, What Breaks, and Whether You Qualify

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** Maximo owners, administrators, developers and decision-makers who need to know what MAS 9.2 actually contains — not what the launch material implies it contains — and whether any of it applies to them.

## 📖 Why This Series Exists

IBM announced Maximo Application Suite 9.2 on 25 June 2026 with a clear message: asset-first AI, embedded in the flow of work, with agentic capabilities across reliability, maintenance, field service and safety.

That message is doing a job, and the job is not to help you plan an upgrade.

What we found when we went to the documentation instead of the announcement is a release with a **different centre of gravity than its marketing**. The AI is real and some of it is architecturally interesting. But the most consequential change in MAS 9.2 is that **user and group data has been consolidated out of a synchronisation model and into the Manage relational database**, with user synchronisation removed entirely and three families of user management APIs deleted. That is an identity architecture change, it is the item most likely to break your upgrade, and it appears nowhere in the launch narrative.

This series exists to close that gap.

<aside>💡 <strong>Key insight:</strong> A vendor announcement argues why you should want a release. It is not designed to tell you whether you can have it, or what it will cost you to take it. Those are the questions this series answers.</aside>

## 🔬 How This Series Was Researched

Every factual claim in these seven posts traces to a primary IBM source.

That was harder than it sounds. IBM Documentation is a JavaScript application: fetch a page normally and you get an empty shell, and standard fetchers receive HTTP 403. We worked around this through IBM's own content API with a browser user-agent, pulled the relevant topics, and **cached them locally** so every claim remains auditable after IBM's continuous-delivery docs move on.

Those sources were then distilled into a fact base of roughly one hundred numbered, individually-cited facts, each carrying a source URL, an access date, and a confidence level:

| Confidence | Meaning | How it appears in the posts |
|---|---|---|
| **CONFIRMED** | Read directly from a primary IBM page | Stated plainly, with confidence |
| **REPORTED** | Secondary source only | Attributed, with the source named |
| **UNCONFIRMED** | Could not be verified | Explicitly flagged, never asserted |

Writers worked under one hard rule: **no fact base ID, no claim.** Nothing was written from memory or inference. Where a post could not reach depth on sourced material, the instruction was to come up short rather than pad.

## 🗺️ The Seven Parts

### [Part 1 — What MAS 9.2 Actually Is](/blog/mas-92-what-it-actually-is)
*Release identity, the Feature Channel, and the lifecycle clock you're now on.*

Before features, structure. What "a new MAS version" means in the 9.x era versus the 8.x LTS and CD era, the monthly Feature Channel cadence running alongside the base release, the Support Cycle-3 (3+1+3) lifecycle policy that applies from 9.0 onward, and the confirmed fact that every MAS 8.x stream reached completion on 30 April 2026. Also teaches the Software Product Compatibility Report method, because IBM deliberately does not publish inline version floors.

### [Part 2 — MAS 9.0 vs 9.1 vs 9.2: The Honest Comparison](/blog/mas-92-vs-91-vs-90)
*Five dimensions, three versions, side by side.*

Platform and UI, AI, Manage applications, prerequisites and runtime, and support lifecycle. Includes the Java arc (9.1 to Java 17, 9.2 to Java 25) and two threads that explain most of the confusion in the 9.x line: the **product-identity arc** — Maximo Assist renamed to Collaborate at 9.1, then deprecated as a suite add-on at 9.2 and moved into Manage — and the **centralisation arc**, in which user data and security configuration progressively migrate from Manage up to the suite.

### [Part 3 — What's New Inside Maximo Manage 9.2](/blog/mas-92-manage-features)
*Tickets, Alerts, AI insights, and a calibration engine that finally checks itself.*

The largest post in the series. Two new applications, Collaborate arriving as a Manage add-on with a Single Node OpenShift restriction, work order attachments and failure reporting, six new time zone processing rules, and automated calibration accuracy validation against ISO/IEC 17025 and ISO 15189 with configurable enforcement. Plus a full section on Maximo Optimizer, including the Large Neighborhood Search algorithm and natural-language what-if analysis.

### [Part 4 — What's New Outside Manage](/blog/mas-92-suite-applications)
*Monitor, Health, Predict, Visual Inspection, Maximo IT, Real Estate and Facilities, HSE, AIP.*

Led by **Maximo Monitor**, which removes its dependency on the IoT Platform entirely and becomes a stand-alone system of record — the single biggest architectural change in the release. Also covers Visual Inspection's framework transition and the removal of NVIDIA Pascal GPU support, Maximo IT's watsonx Orchestrate agent, and RAG-based lease abstraction in Real Estate and Facilities.

### [Part 5 — The AI Layer in MAS 9.2](/blog/mas-92-ai-features)
*What IBM documented, what it only announced, and what nobody can verify yet.*

An evidence-tiered audit of the release's headline theme. Documented capabilities — the agentic Maximo Assistant, the Maximo MCP server, AI-generated insights with confidence scores, AppPoint tracking — are separated from announcement-only capabilities such as Maximo Condition Insight and conversational scheduling. The MCP server gets the most attention: an EAM vendor shipping one is a meaningful admission about how customers actually buy AI.

### [Part 6 — The MAS 9.2 Upgrade Watch-List](/blog/mas-92-upgrade-watchlist)
*Eight things ranked by how badly they bite.*

The practical post. The Db2 12 licence trap that can take production down on trial expiry, three removed user management API families, the add-on parity problem in IBM's own words, the SCIM and authentication migration, two settings that cannot be changed after the fact, Java 25, and Visual Inspection's retroactive hardware removal. Ends with a pre-upgrade verification checklist.

### [Part 7 — MAS 9.2 — FOMO?](/blog/mas-92-fomo-reality-check)
*Who actually qualifies, who doesn't, and what you're really missing.*

The finale. Separates whether you **can** move from whether you **should**, runs both through eight sequential gates, maps eight real customer profiles to five verdicts, and closes with a reality check naming what is overhyped and what is genuinely urgent. If you read one post, read this one.

## 📊 The Release in One Table

| Area | The headline change |
|---|---|
| **Manage** | Two new applications — Tickets and Alerts — plus ISO/IEC 17025 and ISO 15189 calibration accuracy validation |
| **Monitor** | IoT Platform dependency removed; Monitor becomes a stand-alone system of record |
| **Suite platform** | User synchronisation removed; user and group data consolidated into the Manage relational database |
| **AI** | Agentic Maximo Assistant, a Maximo MCP server for customer-supplied agents, AI insights with confidence scores |
| **Optimizer** | Large Neighborhood Search for thousands of work orders; natural-language what-if analysis |
| **Visual Inspection** | TensorFlow and PyTorch only; Caffe and Darknet removed; NVIDIA Pascal GPUs unsupported |
| **Runtime** | Java 25; Db2 12; Cloud Pak for Data 5.2; Python 3.12 |
| **Architecture reach** | s390x (LinuxONE) and ppc64le (Power) for a named set of add-ons |
| **Biggest upgrade hazard** | The Db2 11 to 12 licence requirement, which can cause production outages if missed |

## 🗓️ The Lifecycle Picture

Verified from IBM's product lifecycle pages (PID 5737-M66), because this is the table most readers actually came for:

| Version | General Availability | Announcement | Transition to Extended Support | Extended completion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MAS 9.0.x** | 25 Jun 2024 | AD24-0483 | **30 Jun 2027** (AD26-0622) | **30 Jun 2031** |
| **MAS 9.1.x** | 24 Jun 2025 | AD25-1186 | Not yet published | Not yet published |
| **MAS 9.2.x** | 25 Jun 2026 | AD26-0673 | Not yet published | Not yet published |
| **MAS 8.11-LTS** | 26 Sep 2023 | 223-0230 | Fix availability completed **30 Apr 2026** | Extended Support available |
| **MAS 8.10-LTS** | 28 Mar 2023 | 223-043 | Fix availability completed **30 Apr 2026** | Extended Support available |

Two readings matter. **The annual late-June cadence is real** — three consecutive releases, three consecutive Junes. And **the blank cells are not missing information**: IBM issues each transition date in a *later* announcement letter, which is why 9.0's 2027 date arrived under a 2026 letter. Do not fill those blanks by adding three years to GA.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**What is MAS 9.2 and when was it released?**
IBM announced Maximo Application Suite 9.2 on 25 June 2026, positioned around asset-first AI embedded into reliability, maintenance, field service and safety workflows. It is a substantial release: two new Manage applications, a re-architected Monitor, an MCP server, and a long list of deprecations and removals that make it a genuine upgrade project rather than a routine refresh.

**Which post should I read first?**
If you are deciding whether to upgrade at all, start with Part 7 and work backwards into the parts that apply to you. If the upgrade is already committed, start with Part 6. For the feature picture, Part 3 covers Manage and Part 4 covers everything else. Part 1 is the right entry point if release channels and the lifecycle policy are unclear to you.

**Does this series repeat the MAS 9 features series?**
No. The 25-part MAS FEATURES series covers the general MAS 9 landscape and the MAS NUCLEAR series covers 9.2 for nuclear operators. This series is strictly the 9.2 delta — what changed, what breaks, and who is eligible. It assumes you already know what MAS is.

**How was this series researched?**
Primary-source first. IBM Documentation returns HTTP 403 to ordinary fetchers and renders client-side, so topics were pulled through IBM's own content API and cached locally, then distilled into a fact base of roughly one hundred individually-cited facts. Every claim traces to an IBM documentation page, IBM Support page, or IBM's own announcement, all accessed in August 2026.

**What could this series not verify?**
Four things, each flagged in place rather than omitted: published end-of-support dates for MAS 9.0, 9.1 and 9.2; confirmed GA dates for 9.0 and 9.1; whether Feature Channel builds carry production entitlement; and the widely-repeated claim that the AI Service replaced IBM Granite with OpenAI GPT-OSS-120B in January 2026.
