# What MAS 9.2 Actually Is: Release Identity, the Feature Channel, and the Lifecycle Clock You're Now On

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** The person who has to answer "should we move to 9.2, and when?" to a steering committee — asset directors, Maximo platform owners, and the architects who will be asked to justify the answer in writing.

**Series:** Part 1 of 7 — *MAS 9.2: An Honest Read* | **Read time:** 19 minutes

---

## 📖 The Question Nobody Asks First

Something predictable happens whenever IBM ships a new Maximo Application Suite version. A feature list circulates. Someone forwards the announcement blog. A sales conversation starts. Within a week your team is arguing about whether the agentic Maximo Assistant is worth the upgrade, and nobody has yet asked the question that determines whether the argument even matters:

**What kind of release is this, structurally?**

Not "what's in it." *What is it.* How long will it be supported. What channel does it come down. Whether the thing your integrator is demoing is the same artifact you'd be allowed to run in production. Whether the release you're on right now still has a support contract behind it.

Those questions have concrete, checkable answers, and they change the upgrade decision far more than any single feature does. A release that supports you until 2029 is a different proposition from one that supports you until 2027, even if the feature lists are identical. A build you can only run in a sandbox is a different proposition from one you can run in production, even if the screenshots match.

This post answers the structural questions and nothing else. Features start in Part 2. If you're reading the series in order, treat this one as the frame everything else hangs on — and if you're the person who has to write the business case, this is the part your steering committee will actually interrogate.

One promise about method up front: everything below is traced to a primary IBM page — IBM's announcement, IBM Documentation, or an IBM Support lifecycle page. Where IBM has not published something, we say so explicitly rather than filling the hole with a plausible guess. There is a section near the end devoted entirely to what we could not verify. Read it. It's the most honest part of the post, and probably the most operationally useful.

## 📅 The Release, Stated Plainly

IBM publicly announced Maximo Application Suite 9.2 in a post published **25 June 2026**, authored by IBM's VP of Asset Lifecycle Management Product Engineering. IBM followed the announcement with a webinar on **30 June 2026**.

The positioning is **"asset-first AI"** — IBM's framing for embedding AI directly into reliability, maintenance, field service, safety and operations workflows, rather than bolting a chatbot onto the side of an EAM system. The announcement also leans on **"agentic workflows"**, which in 9.2 is not purely marketing vocabulary: the Maximo Assistant genuinely changes shape in this release, and we take that apart properly in Part 5.

That's the release identity. Now the structural picture, which is where it gets interesting.

MAS 9.2 arrives on what has become a firmly annual June cadence. IBM's own product lifecycle pages record **MAS 9.0 reaching general availability on 25 June 2024** under announcement letter AD24-0483, and **MAS 9.1 on 24 June 2025** under announcement letter AD25-1186. A 25 June 2026 announcement for 9.2 lands exactly where the pattern predicts.

That regularity is worth internalising, because it's a planning input. If IBM holds the cadence, a 9.3 lands around June 2027. If your upgrade programme takes nine months from business case to production cutover — and for a multi-site Maximo estate, nine months is optimistic rather than pessimistic — then you are never upgrading to the newest release. You are always upgrading to the previous one while the next one is being announced. Plan for that reality instead of being annoyed by it.

| Version | General availability | Announcement letter |
|---|---|---|
| MAS 9.0 | 25 June 2024 | AD24-0483 |
| MAS 9.1 | 24 June 2025 | AD25-1186 |
| MAS 9.2 | Announced 25 June 2026 | Webinar 30 June 2026 |

<aside>
💡 **Key insight:** The June cadence is the single most useful planning fact in this post. Anchor your upgrade calendar to it. Budget cycles that assume "we'll upgrade when the new one comes out" quietly commit you to a permanent state of being one version behind — which is a perfectly defensible strategy, but only if you choose it deliberately rather than discovering it.
</aside>

## 🔀 Channels: The Part Everyone Gets Wrong

Here's where most MAS 9.2 conversations go sideways, and it's worth slowing down.

**MAS versions are upgraded by subscribing to a channel.** That's IBM's own phrasing in the upgrade documentation: you keep MAS up to date by subscribing to a channel, after completing the prerequisite tasks. There is no installer you download and run against an existing deployment. The operator watches a channel, and the channel determines what lands in your cluster.

Alongside the base release, IBM runs a **monthly Feature Channel**. You'll see it referenced in IBM Support readmes with names like "Maximo Application Suite 9.2.x — Feature Channel November Release." The cadence is real and it is genuinely monthly.

The question that matters — and the one we've heard asked wrong in three separate client conversations — is: **can you run Feature Channel builds in production?**

IBM answers this directly on its *Software Support Lifecycle Policy for Versions 9.0+* page. The language is worth quoting exactly, because paraphrases of it have been drifting:

> "IBM provides non-production components exclusively through a continuous delivery (CD) stream know as Feature Channel. Delivered as part of our regular Operator Catalog updates, this channel introduces new features, developed for the next release, allowing early access for non-production environments. This is offered alongside and in parallel with our normal maintained releases."

Three things fall out of that sentence, and each one has an operational consequence:

1. **"Non-production components exclusively."** The Feature Channel is not a faster production track. It is explicitly scoped to non-production. If someone on your team has been treating it as "the same product, just earlier," that assumption needs correcting before it becomes an audit finding.
2. **"Developed for the next release."** This is the part people miss. The Feature Channel is a *forward* preview stream. Features arriving in the Feature Channel today are being built for the *next* version, not backported into your current one. It's a window into 9.3, not an accelerator for 9.2.
3. **"Alongside and in parallel with our normal maintained releases."** The Feature Channel does not replace or interrupt your maintained stream. Both exist simultaneously. You subscribe to one for production and, if you want early sight of what's coming, you stand up a separate non-production environment on the other.

IBM's published release table bears this reading out. Under the "MAS v9.2" heading, IBM lists monthly Feature Channel releases running from a September release (published 6 October 2025) through October, November, December 2025, then January, February, March and April 2026 — all *before* 9.2 became generally available. Then comes "9.2.0 and June Feature Channel" on 25 June 2026, and MAS Core **9.2.1 on 30 July 2026**.

Read that timeline again. The Feature Channel spent nine months delivering 9.2's features into non-production environments *before* 9.2 shipped. That is exactly what a next-release preview stream looks like in practice. It is not what a production hotfix train looks like.

### What this means for your environments

The practical shape of a correctly-configured MAS estate under this model:

| Environment | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Production | Maintained release channel | Supported, entitled, audit-defensible |
| Pre-production / staging | Same maintained channel as production | Validates the upgrade you will actually perform |
| Innovation / evaluation sandbox | Feature Channel | Early sight of next-release features, non-production only |

The mistake we see is collapsing rows two and three — using a single non-production environment subscribed to the Feature Channel and treating it as both the upgrade rehearsal *and* the feature preview. That environment is now running code your production cluster will never run in that form, which makes it worthless as an upgrade rehearsal. If you can only afford one non-production environment, it must track your production channel. The Feature Channel sandbox is the one you cut.

<aside>
💡 **Key insight:** "We tested it in the sandbox" means nothing if the sandbox is on the Feature Channel. Your upgrade rehearsal environment must be on the same maintained channel as production, or you have rehearsed a performance nobody is going to stage.
</aside>

## ⏳ Support Cycle-3: The Lifecycle Model Changed Under You

This is the structural change that most 8.x veterans have not fully absorbed, and it reshapes how you think about version selection.

**From version 9.0 onward, MAS moved to IBM Standard Lifecycle Policy Support Cycle-3.** IBM states the policy on the 9.0.x and 9.1.x lifecycle pages as: *"Support Cycle-3: 3 years of support with critical fix extension of 1 year and usage and existing fixes for 3 (3+1+3)."*

Unpacked, Support Cycle-3 provides defect and non-defect technical support for at least three years from general availability. When a release announces a lifecycle transition, an option may exist to purchase Extended Support for limited coverage up to an additional four years. For an eligible product's final version, Sustained Support may extend limited coverage up to a further five years.

IBM also spells out the versioning consequence, and this one catches people: MAS numbering follows semantic versioning principles, but **the lifecycle follows IBM's VRMF standard, where a change to the MINOR number resets the product lifecycle**. Moving 9.1 → 9.2 is not a patch. It starts a new support clock.

Two further commitments from the same policy page are worth having in your back pocket for planning conversations. First, IBM does not commit to a specific release schedule — the June cadence is an observed pattern, not a contractual promise. Second, and more usefully, **lifecycle transition announcements are made at least six months before they take effect.** That six-month floor is your minimum warning period, and it's the number your risk register should use.

### Why this replaced the LTS/CD fork

In the 8.x era you made a choice. You took a **Long Term Support** release like 8.10 or 8.11 and got a long, stable runway with slower feature delivery. Or you took a **Continuous Delivery** release like 8.7, 8.8 or 8.9 and got features sooner on a much shorter support horizon. Two tracks, two philosophies, one decision to defend to your CIO.

That fork is gone. From 9.0 there is one lifecycle policy applied to every minor version, and the "do I want features or stability" tension is resolved differently — through the channel model rather than the release model. Want stability? Stay on the maintained channel of your chosen minor version. Want early sight of features? Stand up a non-production environment on the Feature Channel. You no longer trade support duration for feature velocity; you separate them into different environments.

Here is the contrast in the form most useful for a steering-committee slide:

| Dimension | 8.x era (LTS / CD) | 9.x era (Cycle-3 + Feature Channel) |
|---|---|---|
| Release tracks | Two: LTS and CD | One maintained lifecycle per minor version |
| The core decision | Support duration **vs.** feature velocity | Which channel each **environment** subscribes to |
| Support model | LTS long runway; CD short runway | Support Cycle-3 (3+1+3) for every 9.x minor |
| Lifecycle clock | Set by track choice | Reset by every MINOR version bump |
| Early feature access | Take a CD release into production | Feature Channel, non-production only |
| Feature Channel content | n/a | Features for the **next** release |
| How you upgrade | Installer / channel depending on release | Subscribe to a channel |
| Transition warning | Per release announcement | ≥ 6 months before effective |

<aside>
💡 **Key insight:** The old question was "LTS or CD?" The new question is "which channel does each of my environments subscribe to?" It's a better question — it's answered per environment rather than once for the whole estate, and it stops you trading away support duration to see a feature early.
</aside>

## 🚨 The 8.x Deadline Has Already Passed

If you're still on 8.x, this section is the whole post.

IBM's *Supported Versions for Maximo Application Suite* page — current as of 1 May 2026 — records the following, and every date in it is now in the past:

| Version | GA | Announcement letter | Key date | Status at that date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.11-LTS | 26 Sep 2023 | 223-0230 | **30 Apr 2026** | Fix Availability Completion; Extended Support available |
| 8.10-LTS | 28 Mar 2023 | 223-043 | **30 Apr 2026** | Fix Availability Completion; Extended Support available |
| 8.9-CD | 22 Nov 2022 | 222-285 | **30 Apr 2026** | Completion of Support |
| 8.8-CD | 26 Jul 2022 | 222-117 | **30 Apr 2026** | Completion of Support |
| 8.7-CD | 22 Feb 2022 | 222-011 | **30 Apr 2026** | Completion of Support |

Every supported and CD 8.x release converged on the same date: **30 April 2026**. As you read this, that date is more than three months behind us.

The distinction between the two rows matters. For **8.11-LTS and 8.10-LTS**, 30 April 2026 was *Fix Availability Completion* with *Extended Support available from that date* — meaning there is a commercial path to continued coverage, if you're paying for it. For **8.9, 8.8 and 8.7-CD**, it was *Completion of Support* with no extended option listed. Those releases are done.

So there are exactly three honest positions to be in right now, and it's worth establishing which one you're actually in before your next audit does it for you:

1. **On 9.x already.** Your decision is which 9.x, and when to move between them. The rest of this series is for you.
2. **On 8.11 or 8.10 with Extended Support purchased.** You have runway, you are paying for it, and you have a hard commercial incentive to finish the migration. Know your Extended Support end date.
3. **On 8.x with nothing purchased.** You are running unsupported software. No new fixes, including security fixes. This is a live risk-register item, not a roadmap item, and it should be worded that way when it goes up the chain.

There's a fourth position people *think* they're in — "we're on 8.11, which is LTS, so we're fine." That was true. Fix Availability Completion was 30 April 2026. It is no longer true.

<aside>
⚠️ **Check this today:** If you're on 8.x, the single highest-value action from this entire post is to confirm in writing whether Extended Support has actually been purchased for your entitlement. Not "we think procurement handled it." Confirmed, with a contract reference. We have watched teams discover the answer during an incident, which is the worst possible moment to learn it.
</aside>

## 🔎 What IBM Publishes About 9.x Support Dates — And What It Doesn't

Here is where we have to be careful and specific, because the picture is uneven, and a confidently-stated wrong date is worse than an admitted gap.

**What is published.** IBM's product lifecycle page for MAS 9.0.x records general availability of 25 June 2024, a *Transition to Extended/Sustained or End of Support* date of **30 June 2027** under announcement letter AD26-0622 with Extended Support noted, and *Completion of Extended, Sustained, Extension availability* of **30 June 2031**. That page was last updated 24 June 2026.

Note how neatly that validates the model rather than just the dates: GA June 2024, transition June 2027 — three years, exactly as Support Cycle-3 promises. Then extended coverage running to June 2031, four years beyond the transition. The 3+1+3 shape is real and it's now observable in a published date.

**What is not published.** The MAS 9.1.x lifecycle page, last updated 13 February 2026, records general availability of 24 June 2025 under announcement letter AD25-1186 and the Support Cycle-3 policy — **but carries no transition or end-of-support date**. The MAS 9.2.x page tells the same story one year later: general availability of 25 June 2026 under announcement letter AD26-0673, the Cycle-3 policy, and **no transition date**. And IBM's *Supported Versions* page as of 1 May 2026 enumerates only 8.11-LTS and 8.10-LTS under Supported Versions; **9.0, 9.1 and 9.2 are not listed there at all.**

There is a pattern in that, and it is reassuring rather than alarming. MAS 9.0's transition date arrived under announcement letter **AD26-0622 — a 2026 letter, for a 2027 date**. IBM issues the transition date in a separate, later announcement, roughly a year ahead. So the blank cells for 9.1 and 9.2 are not IBM being evasive and not a gap in our research; they are letters that have not been written yet. Which also means: **do not fill those blanks yourself by adding three years to GA.** You will probably be close, and "probably close" is not a date you put in a board pack.

That last point needs handling carefully, because it is easy to misread in exactly the wrong direction. **The absence of 9.x from that page is not evidence that 9.x is unsupported.** The per-version lifecycle pages plainly exist and plainly state Support Cycle-3 coverage. The most reasonable reading is that the Supported Versions page hadn't been updated to reflect the 9.x lifecycle model at the time it was captured. But we're telling you what the page says rather than what we assume IBM meant, because you may well be asked to produce a screenshot of it.

**What this means for you practically.** You can plan MAS 9.0 against a published 30 June 2027 transition date. For 9.1 and 9.2, you can plan against the *policy* — at least three years from GA under Support Cycle-3, with at least six months' notice of any transition — but you cannot yet cite a specific published EOS date, because IBM has not put one on the page. If your governance process demands a hard date for 9.1 or 9.2, that date has to come from your IBM representative in writing, not from us and not from a blog post that sounds confident.

| Version | GA | Published transition / EOS | Extended completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| MAS 9.0.x | 25 Jun 2024 | **30 Jun 2027** (AD26-0622) | 30 Jun 2031 |
| MAS 9.1.x | 24 Jun 2025 | Not published as of 13 Feb 2026 | Not published |
| MAS 9.2.x | 25 Jun 2026 (AD26-0673) | Not published | Not published |

## 🖥️ Platform Reach: Where 9.2 Can Actually Run

A quieter structural change in 9.2, and one that matters enormously to a specific subset of readers: **multi-architecture support expanded.**

MAS 9.2 extends architecture support to **IBM LinuxONE (s390x)** and **IBM Power (ppc64le)**, using existing Red Hat OpenShift capabilities through OpenShift Container Platform for scalability and resilience. Critically, this is *not* blanket suite-wide support. IBM names specific applications, industry solutions and add-ons:

| Architecture | Supported applications, industry solutions and add-ons |
|---|---|
| **IBM LinuxONE (s390x)** | Maximo IT 9.2 · Asset Configuration Manager 9.2 · Health, Safety and Environment 9.2 · Maximo Health 9.2 · Maximo Aviation 9.2 · Reliability Strategies 9.2 |
| **IBM Power (ppc64le)** | Maximo IT 9.1 and 9.2 · Asset Configuration Manager 9.1 and 9.2 · Reliability Strategies 9.2 · Health, Safety and Environment 9.2 · Maximo Aviation 9.2 · Maximo Health 9.2 |

Two observations that should shape how you use this list.

First, **Power support reaches back to 9.1** for Maximo IT and Asset Configuration Manager, while everything else is 9.2-only. If you're a Power shop running either of those, you had a route in one release earlier than the rest.

Second — and this is the honest caveat — **the published multi-architecture section carries evidence that the list was still being finalised at general availability.** IBM's own page retains an unresolved internal editorial note stating that the support list was still to be cross-checked with a development lead, and that per-solution multi-architecture delivery is completed *for each monthly Feature Channel release*.

That second clause is the operationally significant one. Multi-architecture parity is not landing as a single big-bang at GA; it is being delivered incrementally, solution by solution, through the monthly release train. So the list above is a **snapshot, not a settled matrix.** If your architecture decision depends on a specific industry solution running on s390x or ppc64le, verify it against current IBM documentation at the moment you commit — and get it confirmed by IBM in writing if it's load-bearing for a procurement.

<aside>
⚠️ **Unverified:** No published compatibility matrix confirming that *every* industry solution and add-on has a shipped 9.2 build was located in August 2026. The editorial note above is direct evidence that parity was still in flux at GA. What would settle it: an IBM-published, dated application-and-add-on compatibility matrix for 9.2, or written confirmation from IBM for your specific add-on set. Until then, treat add-on availability as a thing to confirm per-solution, not assume.
</aside>

This connects to the single most consequential sentence in IBM's entire upgrade documentation, which we'll quote here and then take apart properly in Part 6: *"Before you upgrade, you must consider whether the IBM Maximo Application Suite applications and add-ons are available for upgrade. If the applications or add-ons are no longer available, you must deactivate and delete those applications and add-ons."*

Deactivate and delete. Not "run in compatibility mode," not "continue with a warning."

Read that carefully though, because there are two situations hiding in it and Part 6 separates them properly. **"No longer available" is IBM's term for a product that has been withdrawn** — the same page uses that exact phrasing for Parts Identifier at 8.11 and Maximo Safety at 8.9, both of which customers permanently lost at an upgrade boundary. An add-on that simply has not shipped at 9.2 yet is a different situation, and its consequence is a delay rather than a deletion.

What is true in both cases is the part that actually bites: IBM documents **no way to upgrade base MAS now and let a lagging component catch up afterwards**. The check happens before you start. So whether your add-on is withdrawn or merely late, it governs your upgrade date — which is why it deserves to be the first thing you check, not the last.

## 🧱 The Platform Shifts Underneath 9.2

Beyond architecture, three prerequisite platform changes define what a 9.2 environment looks like. All three are structural rather than feature-level, which is why they belong in this post rather than a later one.

**Db2 12 is supported in MAS 9.2** — and the upgrade path has a trap in it. Upgrading from Db2 11 to 12 requires explicit opt-in activation and a valid IBM Db2 Warehouse licence file. IBM's warning is blunt: without a valid licence, Db2 12 operates under a **30-day trial licence that causes production system outages after expiration.**

IBM has, to its credit, engineered a guardrail. The 11 → 12 upgrade requires both a valid Db2 Warehouse licence file obtained from IBM *and* the `db2_v12_upgrade` flag set to `true`. **The upgrade fails if the licence file is not provided** — deliberately, to prevent a silent downgrade into a trial licence. Note the asymmetry that follows: **new Db2 12 installations do not require a licence file. Only 11 → 12 upgrades enforce this.** Greenfield deployments never meet this gate; upgrades always do.

**IBM Cloud Pak for Data 5.2 can be installed with MAS 9.2**, and MAS components use **Python 3.12** with CP4D 5.2. If you have custom code, analytics assets or scripts pinned to an earlier Python, that's a compatibility check to schedule now rather than discover during cutover.

These aren't the whole prerequisite picture — Part 6 covers the upgrade watch-list properly, including the authentication changes and removed APIs that will break integrations. But they establish the shape: **9.2 is a release with real platform movement underneath it.** This is not a version you slide into over a weekend.

## 📋 How to Get Real Version Floors: The SPCR Method

Here's a question you have almost certainly asked, and almost certainly struggled to answer cleanly: *what OpenShift version does MAS 9.2 require?*

Search for it and you'll find forum posts, consultant blogs, and half-remembered numbers from a previous project. What you won't easily find is an IBM page with a number on it.

That's deliberate, and understanding *why* makes the workaround obvious. **IBM does not state version floors inline.** Requirements differ depending on which applications you deploy and the size requirements of those applications — a single-site Manage deployment and a full suite with Monitor, Predict, MVI and three industry solutions are not the same infrastructure problem, and a single published floor would be wrong for both. So instead of publishing a number that misleads everyone, IBM directs you to generate a **Software Product Compatibility Report (SPCR)** for your specific version.

Most Maximo practitioners have never run one. It takes about two minutes.

### Generating an SPCR, step by step

1. Go to the **Software Product Compatibility Reports** page on ibm.com.
2. From the menu, **select the type of report** to create — for system requirements work, choose **Detailed system requirements**.
3. **Search for "Maximo Application Suite"** and select the product version you're planning against.
4. Click **Submit** to generate the report.
5. **Download it as a PDF, or bookmark the permanent link.** IBM explicitly offers both.

That final step is the one to actually do rather than skim past. The SPCR is generated live, which means it reflects IBM's current position — and IBM's current position can change. A PDF captured on the day you made an architecture decision is *evidence of the basis for that decision*. When someone asks in eighteen months why you sized the cluster the way you did, "here is the IBM compatibility report as it stood on the day we decided" is a complete answer. "I remember reading it somewhere" is not.

### Build it into your process

Three places the SPCR belongs in a well-run MAS programme:

- **At business-case time.** Generate one for your target version before you commit budget. Infrastructure surprises discovered at this stage are line items; the same surprises discovered during cutover are incidents.
- **At design freeze.** Regenerate and compare against the business-case copy. Anything that moved is a change you need to know about while you can still respond to it.
- **Before each upgrade rehearsal.** Regenerate for the specific version you are rehearsing. This is how you catch a raised floor before it catches you.

<aside>
💡 **Key insight:** The absence of published version floors isn't IBM being unhelpful — it's IBM refusing to publish a number that would be wrong for most readers. The SPCR is the supported answer, it takes two minutes, and the PDF you export is audit evidence. Teach it to your team; most Maximo people have genuinely never been shown it.
</aside>

## ⚠️ What We Could Not Verify

Every post in this series carries this section, because a release analysis that only tells you what it knows is not trustworthy. Here is what remains genuinely open as of **August 2026**, and what would close each gap.

**Published EOS dates for MAS 9.1 and 9.2.** IBM's 9.0.x lifecycle page publishes a 30 June 2027 transition and 30 June 2031 extended completion. The 9.1.x page, last updated 13 February 2026, publishes a GA date and the Support Cycle-3 policy but **no transition or end-of-support date**. No equivalent 9.2 lifecycle page could be retrieved. Compounding this, IBM's Supported Versions page as of 1 May 2026 does not enumerate 9.x at all — an absence of listing, which is not the same as evidence of non-support. *What would settle it:* IBM publishing transition dates on the 9.1.x and 9.2.x lifecycle pages, or written confirmation from your IBM representative. **Do not let anyone hand your governance board a specific 9.1 or 9.2 EOS date without a source.**

**Supported upgrade paths into 9.2.** Upgrade *prerequisites* are documented in detail. The version-to-version *path matrix* is not — specifically, whether an 8.x environment can go directly to 9.2, and whether 9.0 → 9.2 is supported or requires a hop through 9.1. This is a first-order planning question and we could not answer it from published documentation. *What would settle it:* an IBM-published supported-upgrade-path matrix for 9.2. Part 6 covers what the prerequisites do tell us. If you're on 8.x or 9.0, make this an explicit question to IBM before you build a plan on an assumption.

**Complete add-on and industry-solution parity at 9.2.** Discussed above. The editorial remnant in IBM's own multi-architecture section is direct evidence that the support list was still being cross-checked at GA, with delivery landing per monthly Feature Channel release. No comprehensive compatibility matrix was located. *What would settle it:* a dated, IBM-published 9.2 application-and-add-on availability matrix.

**AppPoints ratio or licensing-cost changes at 9.2.** MAS 9.2 introduces an AppPoints usage dashboard for real-time licence tracking, and AI Service AppPoint consumption is now trackable. Whether the underlying **ratios or costs** changed is a separate question we could not answer from published documentation. *What would settle it:* the 9.2 licensing guidance and License Information documents read in full against the 9.1 equivalents. If your renewal is close, this is a question for IBM, not an inference from a feature list.

**Feature Channel entitlement — a note on why this one is settled.** You may encounter secondary commentary treating Feature Channel production entitlement as an open question. It isn't, and we want to be clear about why we're stating it plainly rather than hedging: IBM's *Software Support Lifecycle Policy for Versions 9.0+* page states the position in its own words, quoted verbatim earlier in this post. Non-production components exclusively; early access for non-production environments. What we have *not* separately read is the dedicated Feature Channel technote IBM links from that page, or the individual per-release readmes, either of which could add nuance around specific components. But the governing policy statement is unambiguous and it is IBM's.

<aside>
💡 <strong>Not a gap — a letter IBM has not written yet.</strong> Transition dates for 9.1 and 9.2 are absent from their lifecycle pages, and that absence is itself confirmed rather than unresolved: IBM issues each transition date in a separate, later announcement letter, which is how 9.0's 30 June 2027 date arrived under a 2026 letter (AD26-0622). Plan 9.1 and 9.2 against the Support Cycle-3 shape, plan 9.0 against its published date, and get anything your governance process treats as binding confirmed by IBM in writing.
</aside>

## ✅ The Structural Verdict

Strip away the feature list and MAS 9.2 is characterised by five structural facts. These are the five things to put in front of a steering committee:

1. **It's an annual-cadence June release** — 9.0 in June 2024, 9.1 in June 2025, 9.2 announced June 2026. Plan your upgrade calendar around a yearly rhythm you will usually be one version behind.
2. **You upgrade by subscribing to a channel**, and the Feature Channel is not that channel for production. IBM's lifecycle policy scopes it to non-production early access to *next-release* features.
3. **Support Cycle-3 (3+1+3) replaced the LTS/CD fork.** One lifecycle per minor version, reset by every minor bump, with at least six months' notice before any transition. MAS 9.0's published dates — transition 30 June 2027, extended completion 30 June 2031 — show the model working exactly as described.
4. **The 8.x deadline is behind you, not ahead of you.** 30 April 2026 has passed. You are on 9.x, on Extended Support, or unsupported. There is no fourth option.
5. **The platform moved underneath the release.** Db2 12 with a licensing gate on 11 → 12 upgrades, Cloud Pak for Data 5.2, Python 3.12, and expanding multi-architecture reach that was still landing incrementally at GA.

And one method to take away regardless of what you decide: **generate an SPCR, export the PDF, keep it.** It's the supported way to get real version floors, it takes two minutes, and it turns an architecture opinion into documented evidence.

None of this tells you whether 9.2's features justify the move. That's what the rest of the series is for. But it tells you what you'd be moving *to* — and that's the part that survives contact with your governance process.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### When was MAS 9.2 released and what is it positioned around?

IBM publicly announced Maximo Application Suite 9.2 in a post published **25 June 2026**, followed by a webinar on **30 June 2026**. The announcement frames 9.2 around **"asset-first AI"** — embedding AI directly into reliability, maintenance, field service, safety and operations workflows — plus **agentic workflows**. Structurally it continues the annual June cadence: 9.0 reached general availability on 25 June 2024 and 9.1 on 24 June 2025, per IBM's product lifecycle pages.

### What is the MAS Feature Channel, and can I run it in production?

The Feature Channel is a monthly continuous-delivery stream delivered through IBM's regular Operator Catalog updates. IBM's own *Software Support Lifecycle Policy for Versions 9.0+* page states that IBM provides **non-production components exclusively** through it, that it introduces features **developed for the next release**, and that it allows **early access for non-production environments** alongside the normally maintained releases. Read plainly: it is a preview stream for the next version, not a production update train. Your production and upgrade-rehearsal environments belong on the maintained channel.

### What is Support Cycle-3 and how is it different from LTS and CD?

From version 9.0 onward MAS follows IBM Standard Lifecycle Policy **Support Cycle-3**, described by IBM as *"3 years of support with critical fix extension of 1 year and usage and existing fixes for 3 (3+1+3)."* It replaces the 8.x split where you chose between a Long Term Support release and a shorter-lived Continuous Delivery release. In 9.x there is one supported lifecycle per minor version, and **a MINOR version bump resets the lifecycle clock**. IBM commits to announcing any lifecycle transition at least six months before it takes effect.

### Are MAS 8.10 and 8.11 still supported?

Both **8.11-LTS and 8.10-LTS reached Fix Availability Completion on 30 April 2026**, with Extended Support available from that same date. That date has passed. **8.9-CD, 8.8-CD and 8.7-CD all reached Completion of Support on 30 April 2026** with no extended option listed. If you are on any 8.x release today you are either paying for Extended Support or running unsupported software with no new fixes, including security fixes. Confirm in writing which of those is true for your entitlement.

### How do I find the exact OpenShift, database and Java versions MAS 9.2 requires?

IBM deliberately does not publish inline version floors, because requirements vary by which applications you deploy and at what size. Instead you generate a **Software Product Compatibility Report (SPCR)**: go to the SPCR page, choose a report type such as *Detailed system requirements*, search for Maximo Application Suite, select your version, and submit. You can export the result as a PDF or bookmark a permanent link — do that, because it becomes your audit evidence for the sizing decision you made on that date.
