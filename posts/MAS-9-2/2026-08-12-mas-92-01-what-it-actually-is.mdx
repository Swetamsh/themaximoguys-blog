# What MAS 9.2 Actually Means for Your Upgrade Plan

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** Business owners, functional leads, Maximo administrators, and project sponsors who need to understand whether MAS 9.2 matters, when to plan for it, and what questions to ask before approving an upgrade.

**Series:** Part 1 of 7 — *MAS 9.2: An Honest Read* | **Read time:** 19 minutes

---

## 📖 Start With the Business Question

When IBM releases a new Maximo Application Suite version, most teams jump straight to the feature list. They ask what is new, what looks useful, and whether AI is finally worth paying attention to.

Those are fair questions, but they are not the first questions.

Before you evaluate features, you need to know:

- Is MAS 9.2 a release we can run in production?
- How long will IBM support it?
- What happens if we are still on MAS 8.x?
- Are all the applications and add-ons we use available on 9.2?
- What platform requirements do we need to budget for?

This post answers those planning questions in plain language. The feature-by-feature discussion starts in the next posts.

The short version: MAS 9.2 is a real release, but it is not something to treat as a simple feature update. It affects support planning, upgrade timing, environment strategy, and platform readiness.

---

## 📅 What MAS 9.2 Is

IBM announced Maximo Application Suite 9.2 on **25 June 2026** and followed it with a webinar on **30 June 2026**.

IBM positions the release around **asset-first AI**. In business terms, that means IBM is trying to put AI into asset, maintenance, reliability, field service, safety, and operations work instead of treating AI as a separate side tool.

That message matters, but it is not the full story. From an upgrade-planning perspective, the more important point is that MAS 9.2 continues IBM's yearly release rhythm:

| Version | Date |
|---|---|
| MAS 9.0 | 25 June 2024 |
| MAS 9.1 | 24 June 2025 |
| MAS 9.2 | Announced 25 June 2026 |

> 💡 **Key insight:** Treat MAS as a yearly planning cycle. If your organization needs six to nine months to approve, test, and deploy an upgrade, you will usually be moving to the previous release while IBM is already talking about the next one. That can be a smart strategy, but it should be intentional.

---

## 🔀 The Feature Channel, Explained Simply

One confusing part of MAS 9.x is the word **channel**.

For a business or functional reader, think of a channel like a lane your MAS environment follows for updates.

The important distinction is this:

- Your **production environment** should follow the normal supported release lane.
- The **Feature Channel** is for early access and evaluation, not for production use.

IBM's lifecycle policy says the Feature Channel provides non-production components and early access to features being developed for the next release. In plain English: it is a preview lane.

That means a demo in a Feature Channel sandbox does not automatically mean the same feature is ready, supported, or entitled for your production system.

### What this means for your environments

Use three different environment purposes:

| Environment | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Production | Normal maintained release | Supported business use |
| Pre-production / staging | Same lane as production | Rehearse the upgrade you will actually perform |
| Innovation sandbox | Feature Channel | Preview future capabilities |

Do not use a Feature Channel sandbox as your upgrade rehearsal. It may contain preview features that your production system will not run in the same way.

> 💡 **Key insight:** If someone says "we tested it in the sandbox," ask which channel that sandbox is on. If it is on the Feature Channel, it is useful for previewing, but not for proving your production upgrade.

## ⏳ The Support Model Changed

In MAS 8.x, customers had to understand two release types:

- **LTS**, which gave a longer runway.
- **CD**, which moved faster but had a shorter support window.

That model changed in MAS 9.x.

From MAS 9.0 onward, IBM uses **Support Cycle-3**. IBM describes it as **3+1+3** support. For planning purposes, read that as:

- At least three years of standard support from general availability.
- A possible period of extended or limited support after that.
- IBM gives at least six months' notice before a support transition takes effect.

The practical point is simple: moving from **9.1 to 9.2 is not just a small patch**. It starts a new minor-version support clock.

Here is the business comparison:

| Dimension | 8.x era (LTS / CD) | 9.x era (Cycle-3 + Feature Channel) |
|---|---|---|
| Main choice | Long support or faster features | Which release and channel each environment should use |
| Production strategy | Choose LTS or CD | Stay on a maintained release channel |
| Early feature access | Sometimes tied to the release track | Use the Feature Channel in non-production |
| Support planning | Different by track | One support policy for each 9.x minor version |

> 💡 **Key insight:** The old question was "Should we choose LTS or CD?" The new question is "Which environments need production stability, and which environments are only for previewing future features?"

## 🚨 The 8.x Deadline Has Already Passed

If your organization is still on MAS 8.x, this is the most important section.

IBM's supported-versions information shows that the key MAS 8.x support date was **30 April 2026**. That date has already passed.

| Version | GA | Announcement letter | Key date | Status at that date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.11-LTS | 26 Sep 2023 | 223-0230 | **30 Apr 2026** | Fix Availability Completion; Extended Support available |
| 8.10-LTS | 28 Mar 2023 | 223-043 | **30 Apr 2026** | Fix Availability Completion; Extended Support available |
| 8.9-CD | 22 Nov 2022 | 222-285 | **30 Apr 2026** | Completion of Support |
| 8.8-CD | 26 Jul 2022 | 222-117 | **30 Apr 2026** | Completion of Support |
| 8.7-CD | 22 Feb 2022 | 222-011 | **30 Apr 2026** | Completion of Support |

For business planning, there are three possible positions:

1. **You are already on MAS 9.x.** Your decision is when to move within the 9.x line.
2. **You are on MAS 8.10 or 8.11 and have purchased Extended Support.** You have some runway, but you are paying for it and should know the end date.
3. **You are on MAS 8.x without Extended Support.** You are running unsupported software. That is a business risk, not just an IT roadmap item.

The common misunderstanding is this: "We are on 8.11 LTS, so we are fine." That used to be true. After **30 April 2026**, it is only true if Extended Support is actually in place.

> ⚠️ **Check this today:** If you are on MAS 8.x, confirm in writing whether Extended Support has been purchased. Do not rely on "we think procurement handled it." Get the contract reference.

## 🔎 What IBM Has Published About 9.x Support Dates

IBM has published clear lifecycle information for MAS 9.0, but not every future date for 9.1 and 9.2.

For **MAS 9.0**, IBM lists:

- General availability: **25 June 2024**
- Transition date: **30 June 2027**
- Extended completion: **30 June 2031**

For **MAS 9.1** and **MAS 9.2**, IBM has published the general availability dates and the Support Cycle-3 policy, but not the exact transition or end dates.

That does not mean 9.1 or 9.2 are unsupported. It means IBM has not yet published those specific future dates. MAS 9.0's transition date was published later, under a later announcement letter, so the same pattern may apply to 9.1 and 9.2.

The practical rule: use the published MAS 9.0 dates where they exist. For MAS 9.1 and 9.2, plan against the support policy, but do not invent exact end dates. If your approval process requires a hard date, ask IBM for written confirmation.

| Version | GA | Published transition / EOS | Extended completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| MAS 9.0.x | 25 Jun 2024 | **30 Jun 2027** (AD26-0622) | 30 Jun 2031 |
| MAS 9.1.x | 24 Jun 2025 | Not published as of 13 Feb 2026 | Not published |
| MAS 9.2.x | 25 Jun 2026 (AD26-0673) | Not published | Not published |

## 🖥️ Where MAS 9.2 Can Run

MAS 9.2 expands support for some IBM hardware platforms, including **IBM LinuxONE** and **IBM Power**.

This does not mean every MAS application and every industry solution automatically runs everywhere. IBM lists specific applications and add-ons.

| Architecture | Supported applications, industry solutions and add-ons |
|---|---|
| **IBM LinuxONE (s390x)** | Maximo IT 9.2 · Asset Configuration Manager 9.2 · Health, Safety and Environment 9.2 · Maximo Health 9.2 · Maximo Aviation 9.2 · Reliability Strategies 9.2 |
| **IBM Power (ppc64le)** | Maximo IT 9.1 and 9.2 · Asset Configuration Manager 9.1 and 9.2 · Reliability Strategies 9.2 · Health, Safety and Environment 9.2 · Maximo Aviation 9.2 · Maximo Health 9.2 |

If your organization depends on one of these platforms, do not assume compatibility from the MAS version number alone. Confirm that your exact MAS applications, industry solutions, and add-ons are available for your target platform.

> ⚠️ **Important:** Treat add-on availability as something to verify per application. A base MAS release being available does not automatically mean every add-on you use is ready.

IBM's upgrade guidance includes a very important warning: before upgrading, you must confirm whether your MAS applications and add-ons are available for upgrade. If an application or add-on is no longer available, IBM says it must be deactivated and deleted before the upgrade.

That is why add-on review should happen early. It can change the project scope, timeline, and business decision.

## 🧱 Platform Items to Budget For

MAS 9.2 also has platform changes underneath the business features. These are the items your technical team needs to check before anyone approves the schedule.

**Db2 12 matters.** MAS 9.2 supports Db2 12. If you are upgrading from Db2 11 to Db2 12, IBM requires a valid Db2 Warehouse license file and an explicit upgrade setting. Without the right license in place, Db2 12 can fall into a 30-day trial situation that may cause an outage after expiration.

**Cloud Pak for Data 5.2 and Python 3.12 matter.** If you use analytics, AI, scripts, or custom work around MAS, the technical team should confirm compatibility.

These items do not mean "do not upgrade." They mean "do not approve the upgrade until the platform checks are complete."

## 📋 How to Get Real Version Floors: The SPCR Method

One question comes up in almost every upgrade discussion:

**What OpenShift, database, Java, and platform versions does MAS 9.2 require?**

IBM does not publish one simple answer that fits every customer. Requirements depend on which MAS applications you run, which add-ons you use, and how large your environment is.

IBM's supported way to answer the question is the **Software Product Compatibility Report**, usually called **SPCR**.

Think of SPCR as IBM's compatibility report for your exact product version.

### What to do

1. Go to IBM's Software Product Compatibility Reports page.
2. Choose **Detailed system requirements**.
3. Search for **Maximo Application Suite**.
4. Select the version you are planning for.
5. Generate the report.
6. Save the PDF or permanent link.

Do not skip the last step. The saved report gives you evidence for the decision you made at that point in time.

Use SPCR at three moments:

- **Before budget approval**, so infrastructure costs are visible early.
- **Before design sign-off**, so the team confirms nothing important changed.
- **Before each rehearsal**, so the test environment matches IBM's current requirements.

> 💡 **Key insight:** Do not rely on remembered version numbers from another project. Generate the SPCR for your own MAS version and environment.

## ⚠️ What We Could Not Verify

Some items were not fully published by IBM when this series was written in **August 2026**. Rather than guessing, we are listing them clearly.

**Exact support transition dates for MAS 9.1 and 9.2.** IBM has published the MAS 9.0 transition date. IBM had not published the same exact dates for 9.1 and 9.2. Plan against IBM's support policy, but get written confirmation if your approval process needs exact dates.

**Supported upgrade paths into 9.2.** IBM documents many prerequisites, but we did not find a simple matrix confirming every allowed starting version and path. If you are on 8.x or 9.0, ask IBM whether you can move directly to 9.2 or whether an intermediate step is required.

**Complete add-on and industry-solution availability.** We did not find one complete IBM matrix proving that every add-on and industry solution was available for 9.2. Check your exact footprint before committing to a schedule.

**AppPoints and licensing cost details.** MAS 9.2 improves AppPoint visibility, especially around AI Service usage. That does not automatically tell you whether your costs change. If renewal timing is close, ask IBM directly.

**Feature Channel production use.** This one is not open based on the policy we read. IBM describes the Feature Channel as non-production early access. Treat it as a preview environment, not a production lane.

> 💡 **Plain-language rule:** If the date affects budget, audit, support, or contract decisions, get it from IBM in writing.

## ✅ The Structural Verdict

Here is the simple steering-committee version:

1. **MAS 9.2 is part of IBM's yearly MAS release rhythm.** Plan upgrades as a program, not as a quick patch.
2. **The Feature Channel is for previewing future features.** Do not use it as proof that production is ready.
3. **MAS 9.x uses a new support model.** The old LTS/CD decision has been replaced by Support Cycle-3 and channel planning.
4. **MAS 8.x support deadlines have already passed.** If you are still on 8.x, confirm Extended Support immediately.
5. **Platform readiness matters.** Db2 12 licensing, CP4D 5.2, Python 3.12, add-on availability, and SPCR checks can all affect the plan.

The takeaway: MAS 9.2 may be valuable, but the decision should start with support, readiness, and upgrade eligibility before anyone gets pulled into the feature list.

For any formal approval, ask for three pieces of evidence: the SPCR, the add-on availability check, and written confirmation of any IBM support or lifecycle date your plan depends on.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### When was MAS 9.2 released and what is it positioned around?

IBM announced Maximo Application Suite 9.2 on **25 June 2026** and followed it with a webinar on **30 June 2026**. IBM positions the release around **asset-first AI**, meaning AI built into maintenance, reliability, field service, safety, and operations work.

### What is the MAS Feature Channel, and can I run it in production?

The Feature Channel is a preview lane for future capabilities. IBM describes it as **non-production** early access, so it should not be treated as the production upgrade lane. Use it for evaluation, not for proving your production upgrade.

### What is Support Cycle-3 and how is it different from LTS and CD?

From MAS 9.0 onward, IBM moved away from the old 8.x LTS/CD choice. MAS 9.x uses **Support Cycle-3**, often summarized as **3+1+3**. For planning, the important point is that each 9.x minor version has its own support clock, and IBM gives notice before support transitions.

### Are MAS 8.10 and 8.11 still supported?

MAS 8.10 and 8.11 reached their key support milestone on **30 April 2026**. If you are still on MAS 8.x, confirm whether Extended Support has been purchased. Without it, the system should be treated as unsupported.

### How do I find the exact OpenShift, database and Java versions MAS 9.2 requires?

Use IBM's **Software Product Compatibility Report (SPCR)**. Select Maximo Application Suite and the version you are planning for, generate the detailed system requirements report, and save the PDF or permanent link for your project records.
