# HSE in the Field: Maximo Incident Reporter and Permit to Work on Mobile

> 🎯 **Who this is for:** HSE leads, Maximo administrators and mobility leads who have been asked "can the crews do incidents and permits on their phones?" Developers who want to see how these apps sit in the framework should read Part 1 and then the [MAS-MAF-DEV series index](/blog/mas-maf-dev-00-series-index).

*Part 3 of 3 in the MAS Mobile series. This is a three-part mini-series with no separate index post; the parts link to each other at the bottom.*

**How to read the tags in this post.** Maximo Mobile is a native store shell that downloads server-published Maximo Application Framework (MAF) apps. It is **not** a PWA. IBM's public pages for the two HSE apps are short, so we separate what IBM states from what we infer. **[IBM]** means IBM's page says it. **[INFERRED]** means a reasonable reading that you should test. **[UNVERIFIED]** means we looked and could not confirm it. Where you see no tag, the sentence is general Maximo practice, not a claim about these two apps.

## 🦺 The Two Apps Nobody Budgeted For

Most mobile conversations in Maximo circle around the Technician app. Work orders, asset lookups and inventory dominate the slides. Then a safety manager asks a different question: "A contractor sees a leak on the pipe rack. Where does she report it? And when she needs a hot work permit for the repair, does she walk back to the control room?"

Two apps exist for exactly those moments, and they are unusual in a specific way. Across the MAF application inventory we compiled in Part 1, they are the **only industry-solution apps we found**. Every other app belongs to Manage, Mobile or the suite core. These two belong to Maximo Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) and Maximo for Oil and Gas:

| App | Introduced | Latest change | What IBM says it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incident Reporter | HSE 9.1 | Maps added in 9.2 | Quickly capture details for fugitive emissions or safety incidents from anywhere [IBM] |
| Permit to Work (also called "Permit request" in one 9.2 entry) | Maximo Mobile 9.2 | None yet | Create and update work permit records on the device, integrated with HSE and Oil and Gas workflows [IBM] |

A third fact makes this worth a post. IBM's public ideas portal has an entry titled "Permit to Work and Isolation Certificate Mobile Application" (MOBILE1-I-286), created in February 2023, which collected 49 votes and carries the status **Delivered**, with an IBM admin response dated 13 July 2026 saying the idea was delivered in a recent release. That is a rare case where you can trace a customer request to a shipped app. It is also a reminder that the original request mentioned *isolation certificates*, while IBM's 9.2 text talks about permit records. Do not assume the delivered app covers the whole of the request. We return to that in the gaps section.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** When you write a business case for these apps, quote IBM's own sentences and cite the what's-new pages. A claim such as "supports isolation certificates on mobile" will not survive a pilot if it is not on IBM's page.

## 🧭 Where the Apps Sit in the Architecture

If you only remember one idea from this post, make it this one: **the mobile apps are front ends**. They do not carry their own safety logic. The approval, validation and control rules live in the HSE and Oil and Gas applications in Manage. IBM's 9.2 entry says the Permit to Work app "integrates with existing Maximo Health, Safety and Environment and IBM Maximo for Oil and Gas business processes and workflows, ensuring that mobile-created permits align with industry established safety processes, approval, validation, and control mechanisms" [IBM].

```
 Field worker (phone, native Maximo Mobile shell)
        |
        |  MAF app: Incident Reporter / Permit to Work
        |  (server-published, downloaded by the shell)
        v
 Maximo Manage 9.2  --->  classic HSE / Oil & Gas objects and workflows
        |                   Incidents / Review Incidents
        |                   Permits (create, approve, issue ... )
        |                   Inspections, Risk Assessments, Waste, Companies
        v
 Dashboards, AI incident categorization, Envizi CO2e calculation
```

Three consequences follow.

1. **App versus workflow ownership.** The app captures; HSE governs. Any change to approval routing happens in the HSE workflow and security configuration, not in the mobile app. [INFERRED from IBM's integration wording]
2. **Two release trains.** The app revision is published from the server. The shell on the device is a store update. A camera or location problem is often a shell or permission problem, while a missing field is an app revision problem. Part 2 covers this split for the general mobile estate.
3. **Same configuration tooling.** Like other MAF apps, these appear in Application Configuration, where you can duplicate and change an app. Their app IDs are not published in IBM's what's-new pages. [UNVERIFIED] We list the lookup approach in the administration section instead of guessing identifiers.

## 💳 Licensing and Prerequisites: The Question to Settle First

Before you design anything, settle three things with your IBM account team in writing.

| Question | What we found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Is the HSE (or Oil and Gas) solution required to use the mobile apps? | IBM places both apps inside those solutions and says they integrate with the "existing" HSE and Oil and Gas processes | [INFERRED] yes |
| Do HSE users count as Premium users? | An IBM Community poster states that anyone using HSE or other add-ons is treated as Premium; another reply gives the AppPoints cap per user as 15. Unofficial forum text | [UNVERIFIED] by IBM |
| Does an occasional reporter, such as a contractor, consume AppPoints? | Not answered in the public pages we could fetch. A separate community thread notes authorized users consume AppPoints whether or not logged in, while concurrent use consumes only when logged in | [UNVERIFIED] |
| Do install AppPoints apply to the industry solution? | The same thread notes some add-ons reserve AppPoints at install time and that this is not monthly | [UNVERIFIED] for HSE specifically |

The reason to ask early: Incident Reporter is the kind of app you want *everyone* to use. A near-miss reporting culture depends on a low barrier, and a licence that forces each reporter into a named-user seat changes the economics. IBM's HSE overview page does say that "Self-Service users can create incident records to report unsafe conditions" [IBM], which suggests a lower-cost route exists in the classic apps. Whether the mobile Incident Reporter can be used by that same user type is not stated on that page. [UNVERIFIED]

**Technical prerequisites worth checking** (general Maximo Mobile practice, not specific to these two apps):

- Maximo Manage 9.2 with Maximo Mobile enabled and the current shell on the device
- A mobile-enabled security group for each persona, with the HSE application options granted deliberately rather than inherited
- Persons, labor and crew records that match sign-in identities, so the "reported by" and "requested by" fields resolve to real people
- Assets and locations that are loaded in the offline data set for the sites where the app is used, which matters for both the incident asset picker and the 9.2 map

## 🚨 Incident Reporter: What IBM Actually States

The Incident Reporter page in the HSE documentation is brief. These are the facts it gives us, nothing more.

| Topic | IBM statement | Version |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Capture details for fugitive emissions or safety incidents from anywhere | 9.1 |
| Incident types | Injury and illness, security incidents, a near miss, a spillage, a safety observation, a failure in a safety process, and emissions | 9.1 |
| Status checks | List active or completed incidents on the Incident Reporter page to verify report status | 9.1 |
| Maps | Preview existing incident details from a map, or add a new incident associated to an asset or location from the map | 9.2 |

Everything else is for you to establish in a pilot. That includes which fields are mandatory, whether photos can be attached, what the report screen looks like for a contractor, and whether a draft can be saved offline. We list these as test cases later.

### What the field worker experiences

Based on the IBM text, the intended flow is short: open the app, create an incident, pick the type, fill in the description, tie it to an asset or location, and submit. The list page lets the reporter come back and check whether the incident is still active or completed. In 9.2 the same activity can start from a map: tap an existing incident marker to preview it, or place a new incident against an asset or location from the map.

The value of the map is not decoration. It addresses two real field problems:

1. **Duplicate reports.** When three people see the same spill, three incidents appear. A map preview lets the second and third person see that the incident already exists.
2. **Bad location data.** A reporter who does not know the location code can often find the right asset visually. That improves downstream triage and trending.

> ⚠️ **Warning:** Map behavior depends on your asset and location data. If your assets lack coordinates or your locations lack spatial data, the map has nothing to show. IBM's page does not say how the map positions an incident that is attached to an asset without coordinates. [UNVERIFIED] Test with your own data.

### Incident types and your classification scheme

IBM lists the incident types, but your organisation almost certainly has its own incident classification: severity, potential severity, injury categories, regulatory reportability. The practical question is *where that classification happens*. A sound design is:

- The **field reporter** chooses only what they can reliably judge: what happened, where, and whether anyone is hurt.
- The **HSE reviewer** assigns severity, category and regulatory flags in Review Incidents.

That split is consistent with how 9.2 treats categorization on the back-office side. IBM added **AI-powered incident categorization** in the Tickets application: a multi-class classifier (MCC) model analyses the Summary and Details text and suggests values for the Incident Type and Incident Category fields, ranked by confidence, trained on your own historical incident data [IBM, HSE 9.2]. The reviewer selects the best suggestion manually. Notice what this means for the mobile app: the quality of the text a field worker types now feeds a model. A one-word summary such as "leak" gives the model little to work with. Coach reporters to write one sentence about what, where and how much.

## 🔄 From the Phone to the Classic HSE Apps

A phone submission is only the first third of the journey. Here is the full path, with the 9.2 features that touch each step.

| Step | Where it happens | Who | 9.2 feature that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Capture | Incident Reporter on the device | Any reporter | Maps to place the incident |
| 2. Triage | Review Incidents / Incidents in Manage | HSE coordinator | AI-powered categorization suggestions |
| 3. Investigate | Incidents, linked inspections, risk assessments | Investigator | Inspection assignment to individuals or groups; Related Inspections section |
| 4. Emissions follow-up | Incident events table | Environmental officer | Emission gas type field and automatic CO₂e through the Envizi GHG API |
| 5. Corrective action | Work orders, MOC, audits | Supervisor | Existing HSE links |
| 6. Close and trend | Dashboards, asset and location incident history | HSE manager | Incident history on asset or location pages in Health and Predict |

Several of these deserve a closer look because they change what you should plan for the mobile rollout.

### Inspection assignment reduces download noise

The 9.2 HSE page says you can now assign inspections to specific individuals or groups, "to reduce unnecessary downloads of unassigned inspections to mobile devices". New fields on inspection results identify the owner and owner group, and applications use that to route the inspection. The updated process is supported in Access Permits, Audit and Surveys, Incidents, Operator Rounds in Operator Log, Risk Assessments, Management of Change and Waste management, each with a new **Related Inspections** section under the Related Records tab. Inspections are completed in the Inspections application or the Maximo Mobile Inspections app [IBM].

This matters because a follow-up inspection after an incident is usually performed on a phone. If you assign it to a group of 60 people, all 60 devices may download it. Assign it to the investigator.

### Emissions: the field reports, the back office calculates

For Oil and Gas and environmental teams, a fugitive emission reported from the field lands in the Incident events table. In 9.2 the table gains an **Emission gas type** field. You select the gas type and enter the amount and unit of measure; when you save, Manage sends the details to Envizi, which calculates the CO₂e value and returns the CO₂e amount and unit of measure automatically [IBM]. The 9.1 release added emissions category, type and impact fields so that remote-site users can refer to the data while offline [IBM].

What we could not verify is whether the mobile Incident Reporter exposes the emission fields directly or only the back-office Incidents application does. [UNVERIFIED] Check this in the pilot. If the mobile app does not capture gas type and amount, your process is: the field worker reports the leak, the environmental officer enriches the record. That is a fine process, but it needs an owner and a service level.

### Waste and Companies: neighbours, not mobile features

The 9.2 HSE page also lists a new **Waste management** application, a **Companies** application in the Safety module for contractor and vendor safety data, and predefined domains in Locations for Subunit/Component Code and Maintainable Item/Part Code [IBM]. None of these is described as a mobile app. We mention them because the Companies application is relevant to the contractor reporter question: contractor safety data lives there, and your permit-holder and contractor processes will lean on it.

## 📝 Permit to Work: What IBM Actually States

The Permit to Work story has one oddity and one clear statement.

**The oddity:** IBM's Maximo Mobile 9.2 page names the app twice. One entry, under enhancements for technicians, calls it the **Permit request** mobile application and says it can be used to create and update work permit records, integrated with HSE and Oil and Gas for "real-time approval of workflows and documentation for high-risk activities". A later entry calls it the **Permit to work** mobile application, says it creates and updates work permit records, and adds that it integrates with existing HSE and Oil and Gas business processes and workflows so that mobile-created permits "align with industry established safety processes, approval, validation, and control mechanisms" [IBM]. We read these as the same app. Tag it [INFERRED] and name the app exactly as it appears on your device in your procedures.

**The clear statement:** the HSE 9.2 page says the app "enables technicians to initiate Permit to work requests directly at the job site so that permit data is captured accurately and promptly in industrial and safety-sensitive environments" [IBM]. The verbs are *initiate*, *create* and *update*. They are not *issue*, *authorize* or *close*.

### What the desktop HSE permit model gives the mobile app to integrate with

IBM's HSE overview says permits support "creating permits for work and defining their activities", and covers permit and certificate planning, risk matrices and risk assessment [IBM]. The documentation index lists a section titled "Managing work processes with permit to work", with topics for creating, issuing, completing, returning, suspending and cancelling permits. So the lifecycle in Manage is richer than create and update. The open question is which of those transitions the phone can trigger.

| Permit lifecycle action (HSE desktop docs) | Documented for the mobile app? |
|---|---|
| Create / initiate a permit request | Yes [IBM] |
| Update a permit record | Yes [IBM] |
| Approve | Implied by "real-time approval of workflows" but not itemised [UNVERIFIED] |
| Issue | Not stated [UNVERIFIED] |
| Complete / return | Not stated [UNVERIFIED] |
| Suspend / cancel | Not stated [UNVERIFIED] |
| Attach risk assessment / hazards | Not stated [UNVERIFIED] |
| Link isolations / LOTO | Not stated [UNVERIFIED] |

The phrase "real-time approval of workflows" in the Permit request entry deserves a careful reading. It can mean the approver uses the mobile app, or it can mean the approval workflow in Manage runs in real time as soon as the field record arrives. Both fit. Ask IBM, then test.

### A realistic hot work scenario

To keep this concrete, here is a scenario you can run in a pilot. A pipefitter needs to cut a flange on a line in Unit 4.

1. The pipefitter opens the work order in the Technician app and sees the work needs a hot work permit.
2. In the Permit to Work app, the pipefitter initiates a permit request tied to the work order, asset and location. [INFERRED: the link to the work order is part of HSE's model; whether the mobile app lets you pick the work order is not stated]
3. The record syncs to Manage. The HSE workflow routes it to the area authority.
4. The area authority reviews hazards and controls in the HSE application (or on a device if the app supports it), then approves.
5. Issuing, gas testing, isolations and closure follow your site procedure.

Step 5 is where public information thins out. That is not a criticism; it is the line between "the app speeds capture and approval" and "the app is your permit system".

## 📴 Offline Considerations

The reason to run these apps in Maximo Mobile rather than in a browser is offline tolerance. Remote sites, tank farms, underground plant and offshore platforms all have dead zones. Here is what we can responsibly say.

| Area | What we know | What to test |
|---|---|---|
| General model | Maximo Mobile is designed to work offline and sync when connected; records created offline wait for sync | Does each HSE app follow the general model? |
| Incident Reporter creation | HSE 9.1 says emissions fields can help users complete inspections at remote sites while disconnected or offline [IBM] | Create an incident in airplane mode after a sync. Confirm it queues and syncs |
| Incident Reporter list | IBM says users can list active or completed incidents to check status [IBM] | Is the list available offline, and how fresh is it? A status shown offline is only as new as the last sync |
| Maps | Added in 9.2 [IBM] | Do base-map tiles load offline? Do existing incident markers load offline? [UNVERIFIED] |
| Permit creation | Not described [UNVERIFIED] | Create a permit request offline. Confirm queue behavior and how conflicts are shown |
| Approval latency | Approvals in Manage need connectivity from the approver's side | Measure time from field submission to approver notification |
| Lookups | Asset, location, person and domain data must be in the offline set | Check that the sites and hazard lists the worker needs are in the download scope |

### The one rule that safety teams must hear

An offline-created permit request is **not** an approved permit. The device can hold a request locally, but approval happens when the record reaches the server and the workflow runs. If a worker starts hazardous work because the app showed a "saved" state, that is a procedural failure waiting to happen. Make the status language in your training explicit, and include a rule such as "no work starts on a permit that does not show approved status after a fresh sync". Whether the app clearly distinguishes "saved on device", "synced" and "approved" is a test case for the pilot, not something IBM's page documents. [UNVERIFIED]

### Sync design tips (general Maximo Mobile practice)

- Use Mobile Configuration to control what is downloaded and when; Part 2 describes the preloaded database, background fetch and individual record synchronization added in 9.2.
- Prefer individual record sync when a worker needs the latest status of one permit rather than a full sync.
- Keep the HSE-related lookup data small. A hazard library with thousands of entries slows every login if it is in the offline scope.

## ⚖️ Gaps Against Dedicated Permit-to-Work Tools

Industrial permit-to-work (PTW) products have a long feature list that has grown out of regulatory and incident-investigation requirements. Here is a comparison framed as questions to ask, not a scorecard, because we can verify very little about the IBM app beyond create and update.

| Capability typically expected of a PTW platform | What IBM's pages say about the mobile app | Gap assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Create and update permit requests at the job site | Stated | Covered |
| Alignment with approval and validation controls | Stated: integrates with HSE workflow | Covered by integration [INFERRED] |
| Multi-party digital sign-off on the device (issuer, receiver, holder) | Not stated | [UNVERIFIED] Ask IBM |
| Biometric or PIN-based signature on permits | Part 2 notes biometric e-signature support in the 9.2 shell, but not tied to permits | [UNVERIFIED] for permits |
| Isolation certificate handoff and verification | The original idea title included isolation certificates; the delivered text does not | [UNVERIFIED] |
| Simultaneous operations (SIMOPS) conflict view | Not stated | Likely a gap |
| Live permit board / site map of active permits | Not stated; incident map exists, permit map does not appear in the text | Likely a gap |
| Gas test and instrument capture | Not stated | Likely a gap; may be done through inspections |
| Permit offline viewing for remote shutdowns | Not stated | [UNVERIFIED] |
| Permit audit trail for regulators | Inherited from HSE records in Manage | Covered by platform [INFERRED] |
| Handover between shifts | Operator log has a Shift Log Handover action in HSE [IBM], not described for permits | Different tool |

The summary is not that IBM's app is weak. It is that IBM's *claim* is narrow: capture permit data accurately and promptly, and keep it aligned with HSE controls. If your current process has a paper permit with seven signature blocks and a physical lock board, the app will digitise the request but may not digitise the whole ceremony. Decide up front which parts of the ceremony the app has to carry.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** Draw your current permit lifecycle as a swim-lane diagram, then mark every step with one of three labels: "mobile app proven", "HSE desktop only", or "outside Maximo". Do this before you demo the app to the safety committee. It turns a feature debate into a gap list.

## 🛠️ Administration: Finding, Securing and Configuring the Apps

Because IBM's pages do not publish the app IDs, locate them on your own system rather than relying on a forum post. The queries below are illustrative; table and column names follow common Maximo conventions and should be checked against your 9.2 schema before you rely on them. [UNVERIFIED]

```sql
-- Find candidate HSE mobile app rows (illustrative; verify columns on your schema)
SELECT app, description
FROM   maxapps
WHERE  UPPER(description) LIKE '%INCIDENT%'
   OR  UPPER(description) LIKE '%PERMIT%'
ORDER  BY app;

-- Which security groups have options on those apps?
SELECT g.groupname, a.app, a.optionname
FROM   applicationauth a
JOIN   maxgroup g ON g.maxgroupid = a.groupname -- adjust the join to your schema
WHERE  a.app IN ('<APP_ID_FROM_QUERY_ABOVE>');
```

A safer discovery path is the UI. Open **Application Configuration**, search for the app by name, and note its identifier. Then duplicate the app before changing anything, in the same way you would duplicate `TECHMOBILE`, and keep the original as your fallback. IBM states that configuration changes made to apps based on the earlier Maximo Asset Management framework cannot be migrated to MAF apps, so Application Designer knowledge does not transfer. [IBM, MAS docs, quoted in DOC17]

### Security design for safety apps

Use a deny-by-default design. Create separate security groups for each persona and grant only what each needs:

| Persona | Likely needs | Likely does not need |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor reporter | Incident Reporter: create, view own | Permit approval; view all incidents |
| Technician | Incident Reporter; Permit to Work: initiate and update | Permit approval and issue |
| Area authority / permit approver | Permit to Work: approve actions as the workflow requires | Incident triage changes |
| HSE coordinator | Review Incidents in Manage; mobile read access | Mobile permit approval unless proven necessary |
| Admin | Application Configuration | Day-to-day record access |

This table is a design pattern, not an IBM-documented role model. IBM's public pages for these apps do not list their security options. [UNVERIFIED]

### Change control for a safety app

A published app revision changes what a worker sees without a store release. For safety apps, add controls:

1. Duplicate and configure in a non-production workspace first.
2. Record the app revision number and the shell version in every change ticket.
3. Re-run the pilot test cases after any revision.
4. Keep a rollback plan that republishes the previous revision.

## 🧪 A Pilot Plan You Can Run in Two Weeks

Run this with one site, one shift pattern and a small group (three reporters, two technicians, two approvers, one HSE coordinator).

**Week 1: capture and triage**

| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Create each incident type from the device | All seven types selectable; record visible in Review Incidents |
| 2 | Create an incident from the 9.2 map | Incident linked to the chosen asset or location |
| 3 | Check the duplicate scenario: second reporter opens map | Existing incident visible before a new one is raised |
| 4 | Create an incident in airplane mode after a full sync | Record queues; syncs on reconnect without duplicates |
| 5 | Attach photos if the app allows | Photo arrives, size acceptable [UNVERIFIED feature] |
| 6 | Reporter checks active and completed lists | Statuses match Manage |
| 7 | Triage with AI categorization | Suggestions appear after enough history exists; reviewer can override |
| 8 | Assign a follow-up inspection to one person | Only that person's device downloads it |

**Week 2: permits**

| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Initiate a permit request from the device | Record in the HSE permit application with correct requester |
| 10 | Link to work order, asset, location | Links resolve in Manage |
| 11 | Update the request after submission | Update visible; audit trail shows both versions |
| 12 | Approver acts | Workflow routes to the right person within agreed time |
| 13 | Offline create | Request queued, clearly shown as not approved |
| 14 | Try forbidden actions with a technician login | Issue, close and approve are blocked |
| 15 | Try a rejected permit | Reason visible to the requester on the device |

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

These are scenarios to expect based on how MAF apps and HSE data behave, not documented IBM defects. Treat each as a hypothesis to check.

| Symptom | Likely causes to check |
|---|---|
| App does not appear on the device | Security group lacks the app; app not published; device not re-synced after the change; wrong workspace |
| Incident saved but not in Review Incidents | Sync not completed; site or organisation mismatch; record failed validation on the server (check the sync log) |
| Map is empty | Assets and locations lack coordinates; map not enabled; tiles blocked by network policy |
| Asset picker missing the asset | Asset not in the offline download scope or filtered by site |
| Permit request lacks the work order link | The link might not be offered on the device; test whether the field is read-only [UNVERIFIED] |
| Approver sees nothing | Workflow assignment not set for the permit type; approver lacks security permission; notifications disabled |
| Duplicate incidents after reconnect | Retry after a timeout; check for client-side duplicates before training users to "tap again" |
| Slow login after enabling HSE data | Large hazard or lookup domains in scope; review preloaded database settings from Part 2 |

## ✅ The Commandments of HSE on Mobile

1. **Quote IBM, tag the rest.** If it is not on IBM's page, mark it [UNVERIFIED] until your pilot proves it.
2. **Build the back-office queue before the app.** A reported incident nobody triages is worse than a paper form.
3. **Name an owner for every handoff** from mobile submission to approval, triage and closure.
4. **Settle licensing in writing**, especially for contractors and occasional reporters.
5. **Separate "saved", "synced" and "approved"** in training and in procedure wording.
6. **Assign inspections to people, not crowds,** so devices download what they need.
7. **Version the shell and the app revision** separately in change records.
8. **Do a gap analysis against your permit procedure** before calling the app a permit system.
